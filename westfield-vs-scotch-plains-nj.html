Westfield vs Scotch Plains, NJ: Which Is Right for You?

Neighboring Union County towns on the same Raritan Valley Line, sharing a border and a lot of the same buyers. One has the station and the downtown. The other has the lots and the lower number.

The Short Answer

Buy in Westfield if you want one of New Jersey's best downtowns with the station in the middle of it: median $989,000, 9 days on market, 10/10 schools. Buy in Scotch Plains if you want larger lots and $260,000 back at the median, and you're willing to drive to the train.

Westfield is the premium buy on this stretch of the Raritan Valley Line and prices like it. Scotch Plains is what you choose when the schools are still strong, the lot is bigger, the tax bill is $3,122 a year lighter, and the extra ten minutes to the platform does not break your morning.

Choose Westfield if…

You want the downtown to be part of daily life, not a weekend errand. Westfield has one of the best downtowns in New Jersey and the Raritan Valley Line station sits inside it, which is the whole reason the town prices where it does. Add a 10/10 district feeding Westfield High School and you have the premium address on this line.

Understand what you are competing against. Nine days on market is the fastest number in this comparison, and Westfield has been appreciating at 9.7% year over year through Q1 2026 — among the fastest in our coverage area. Homes here do not wait for you to think about it. If you are still shopping lenders when the right listing hits, you will lose it to someone who is not.

Best fit: Commuters who want to walk or make a five-minute drive to the platform, families who want a 10/10 district and a downtown in the same purchase, buyers with pre-approval already in hand.

Choose Scotch Plains if…

Lot size matters more to you than walkability, and you would rather put the $260,000 into the house than into the address. Larger lots and the shared Scotch Plains-Fanwood school district are the two things buyers here consistently name as the reason they chose it. The district rates 9/10 and feeds Scotch Plains-Fanwood High School, which serves both towns.

The tax picture is the underrated part. Scotch Plains has historically sat at the lower end of Union County's effective-rate distribution while still offering train access and solid schools, and the 2025 average residential bill of $15,818 is $3,122 below Westfield's — roughly $260 a month you keep.

Best fit: Families who want a yard rather than a walk-to-downtown block, hybrid commuters going in two or three days, two-car households, buyers who want a strong Union County district under $800K.

How long is the commute from Westfield and Scotch Plains to NYC?

Both towns ride the Raritan Valley Line, and that line requires a transfer at Newark Penn most hours — roughly 8 to 12 minutes added versus a one-seat ride. That transfer is exactly why these towns cost meaningfully less than Midtown Direct towns with comparable schools and downtowns. If your employer has flexible start times, it is the best per-dollar commuter line in the region. If you need a guaranteed one-seat ride into Penn, this is the wrong line for you.

Westfield station is scheduled at 45 minutes to New York Penn with a $328 monthly rail pass. Scotch Plains has no station of its own — residents drive to Fanwood station, scheduled at 48 minutes, or to Westfield station. A buyer who assumes there is a station in Scotch Plains finds out at the first showing.

One honest note on the numbers. The station timetable on our train map lists Westfield at 45 minutes, while the Westfield town snapshot on this site says roughly 55 minutes and Scotch Plains roughly 60. Those are measuring different things — a scheduled platform-to-platform ride versus a door-to-Penn estimate that absorbs the walk, the drive, the parking and the transfer. I would not plan around either in isolation. Pull the NJ Transit timetable for the specific peak train you would take, then do the trip once, on a Tuesday, before you write an offer.

The cost side matters too: monthly pass, station parking and occasional tickets add up to thousands a year on top of the mortgage. What the NJ-to-NYC commute actually costs has the breakdown.

What are the honest trade-offs?

The gap is $260,000 at the median plus $3,122 a year in taxes. That is what Westfield's downtown, in-town station and 10/10 rating cost against Scotch Plains' 9/10 and larger lots. Roughly $260 a month of the difference is pure carrying cost you never get back. Whether the premium is worth it comes down to how often you actually walk to that downtown and how much one rating point is worth to you.

Scotch Plains' commute has a hidden step. The 48-minute Fanwood figure is platform to platform. Your real number includes driving to Fanwood or Westfield, finding parking, and the Newark transfer. That is a car you need and a lot spot you have to secure — plan for it rather than discovering it in month two.

Westfield's speed cuts both ways. Nine days on market and 9.7% annual appreciation are great once you own. They are brutal while you are shopping. Expect to compete, and expect to lose one or two before you win.

A regional district is a different animal. Scotch Plains shares its district with Fanwood. That is generally a strength — a bigger high school with more programs — but it means school decisions are made across two municipalities. If that matters to you, sit in on a board meeting before you buy.

District ratings are averages in both towns. A 10/10 and a 9/10 both hide school-by-school variation. Ask for the exact elementary catchment boundary on any house you are serious about; the difference between two streets can be the difference between two entirely different schools.

Where else should you be looking?

If Scotch Plains appeals on price, Fanwood next door shares the same district and sits right at the station — worth pricing side by side. If Westfield appeals but the competition is discouraging, the Midtown Direct towns trade the Newark transfer for a higher entry price, and the neighboring Raritan Valley stops trade downtown scale for value. Frequently asked questions

Is Westfield or Scotch Plains cheaper?
Scotch Plains, by $260,000 at the median — $729,000 against $989,000 — plus $3,122 a year less in average property taxes.

Does Scotch Plains have a train station?
No. Residents drive to Fanwood station (48 minutes scheduled) or Westfield station (45 minutes), both Raritan Valley Line with a Newark Penn transfer most hours.

Which has better schools?
Westfield rates 10/10 and Scotch Plains 9/10 on GreatSchools. Westfield runs its own district; Scotch Plains shares the Scotch Plains-Fanwood Regional district with Fanwood.

How fast do homes sell?
Westfield in 9 days, Scotch Plains in 12. Westfield is also up 9.7% year over year through Q1 2026, among the fastest-appreciating towns we cover.

Is the Newark transfer worth it?
Usually, if your start time is flexible. It adds 8 to 12 minutes and saves meaningfully on price versus comparable Midtown Direct towns. See the Midtown Direct towns guide for the other side of that trade.

What will my monthly cost be?
Price, taxes and commute together. Run it with the mortgage calculator and the closing costs calculator, and check any NJ town's average bill in the NJ property tax guide.

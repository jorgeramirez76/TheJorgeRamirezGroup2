Evaluate a cash proposal beside its real alternative

A cash label does not reveal the complete transaction. Compare a written cash offer with a public-market path using the same property facts, condition, costs, contingencies, dates, access, occupancy, and professional estimates before deciding what matters for your address.

Working plan

A cash comparison is an evidence exercise

A proposal can be straightforward or contain material conditions. The process below makes the assumptions visible without predicting which path will produce a particular result.

01

Verify the proposal

Obtain the complete written offer and identify buyer, signatory, funds evidence, assignment rights, contingencies, inspection provisions, dates, credits, and closing contacts.

02

Review the property

Document condition, disclosures, occupancy, title questions, access, seller priorities, and current comparable sales so the alternative is grounded in the same home.

03

Model both paths

Create clearly labeled estimates for the cash proposal and a public-market launch. Use the same cost categories and state which numbers require professional confirmation.

04

Select instructions

Review the complete written terms and professional advice, then give transaction instructions based on the tradeoffs that matter to the owner—not on the word cash alone.

Prepare the evidence

Documents and questions for a cash-offer review

Do not compare an advertised headline with an estimated list price. Bring the complete proposal and use a consistent file for both transaction paths.

  • Full written offer, addenda, buyer identity, signature authority, and expiration
  • Proof-of-funds material and the attorney, title, or closing contact identified by the buyer
  • Inspection, due-diligence, cancellation, assignment, credit, and access provisions
  • Property condition, disclosures, occupancy, included items, title issues, and move needs
  • Property-specific net assumptions with the same cost categories for both paths
  • Legal, title, tax, and wire-instruction questions for the appropriate professionals

Compare the paths

Compare a direct proposal with a public-market launch

Neither path is automatically appropriate for every seller. Compare actual written terms, property readiness, seller priorities, execution risk, and estimated net using consistent assumptions.

Accept or negotiate a direct proposal

Evaluate the buyer and complete contract terms, not only price and closing date. Clarify inspection, assignment, credits, access, possession, title, closing administration, and seller obligations with the appropriate advisers.

Prepare a public-market launch

Build a property-specific comparable set and launch plan, then estimate preparation, access, marketing, timing variables, contingencies, and costs. A public listing creates an opportunity for market response, not a promised response.

Official-source notebook

What the public sources say—and what they do not

Each source below answers a defined question. Open the original, check its current date and coverage, and keep legal or tax decisions with the appropriate professional.

New Jersey Department of Banking and Insurance

Real Estate Commission Bulletin 24-11

Use: Explains New Jersey brokerage relationships, written brokerage services agreements, the property condition disclosure statement, and negotiable broker compensation.

Limit: It is statewide regulatory guidance, not advice about a particular contract, title issue, property, or dispute.

New Jersey Division of Taxation

Realty Transfer Fee

Use: Explains the current Realty Transfer Fee framework, forms, rates, exemptions, and the seller's statutory responsibility for the fee when it applies.

Limit: The amount and any exemption depend on the transaction and current law; a closing or tax professional should calculate the property-specific figure.

New Jersey Division of Consumer Affairs

Seller's Property Condition Disclosure Statement

Use: Shows the state's residential disclosure questions concerning occupancy, systems, defects, environmental conditions, flood risk, and other known property facts.

Limit: The form records the seller's knowledge and does not replace inspections, legal advice, or a property-specific review of disclosure duties.

New Jersey Department of Environmental Protection

Flood Disclosure Law

Use: Explains New Jersey flood-risk notifications for real-estate sales and rentals and provides the state's address-level flood notification tool.

Limit: Flood maps and notices answer defined questions; they do not predict whether or when a property will flood or replace professional advice.

United States Environmental Protection Agency

Lead-Based Paint Disclosure Rule

Use: Explains the federal lead-based-paint disclosure process that generally applies before a buyer is obligated under a contract for most pre-1978 housing.

Limit: The federal page describes coverage and exceptions; property age and transaction facts should be checked with the appropriate professionals.

Common questions

New Jersey cash-offer comparison

Does cash mean the offer has no contingencies?

No. Cash describes the funding method, not every contract term. Read the written inspection, due-diligence, title, cancellation, assignment, access, credit, date, and possession provisions with an attorney.

How should I compare the net from two paths?

Use the same worksheet and label every estimate. Include price, payoffs, liens, negotiated compensation, Realty Transfer Fee when applicable, professional charges, credits, repairs, carrying costs, moving costs, and other property-specific items. Have the proper professionals confirm their figures.

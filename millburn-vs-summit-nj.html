Millburn vs Summit, NJ: Which Is Right for You?

Two Midtown Direct towns, both 10/10 schools, both walk-to-train downtowns, nine minutes and one county line apart. The decision usually comes down to the tax bill and which downtown you want to live in.

The Short Answer

Buy in Millburn if the shortest possible one-seat ride to Penn Station is the thing you optimize for, and you can absorb a $26,292 average tax bill. Buy in Summit if you want a bigger, more commercial downtown and $6,595 a year back in your pocket. Millburn's median is $1,250,000, Summit's $1,095,000.

Millburn suits the buyer whose calendar runs on the 7:12 and who wants the Short Hills address or the walkable Millburn Center blocks. Summit suits the buyer who wants a downtown with real depth, a slightly lower entry price, and does not need to shave nine minutes off each leg.

Choose Millburn if…

The commute is the deciding factor and you have the budget to buy it. Thirty-five minutes to Penn Station on a one-seat ride is close to the best number available in a 10/10 school district. Millburn Township is really three products: Short Hills, where the estate-scale houses and the biggest lots are and where the train map lists a typical home price of $2,100,000; Millburn Center, walkable to the station, restaurants and shops, with older housing stock on tighter lots; and the Wyoming section, which delivers comparable quality to Short Hills at a lower number.

Understand what the median is telling you. The $1,250,000 township figure blends all three. A Short Hills house and a Millburn Center house are not competing for the same buyer, and they will not appraise off each other.

Best fit: Four-or-five-days-a-week Penn Station commuters, families who want the Short Hills address, buyers who value lot size over downtown density, anyone comfortable underwriting a $26,292 average annual tax bill.

Choose Summit if…

You want the downtown to be the reason you live there. Summit is dense, compact and unusually well run — a real commercial center rather than a village strip, with the station in the middle of it. My own office is on Springfield Avenue in Summit, so I'll be blunt about the trade: you give up nine scheduled minutes each way against Millburn and you pay $53 more a month for the pass, and in exchange you get a lower median, a materially lower tax bill, and more to walk to.

Summit is also the junction where the Gladstone Branch splits off the main line, which matters if your household has two different commutes or you want optionality on where you eventually move within the corridor.

Best fit: Hybrid commuters going in two or three days, executives and DINK couples who want to walk to dinner, families who want a 10/10 district without Essex County's top tax bill, buyers who would rather spend the tax delta on the house.

How long is the commute from Millburn and Summit to NYC?

Both towns are on Midtown Direct, which means a one-seat ride into New York Penn Station with no transfer at Newark and no PATH leg. That is the single biggest thing they have in common and the reason both carry a premium over comparable towns a line over.

Scheduled times from the station table: Millburn 35 minutes, Short Hills 37 minutes, Summit 44 minutes. The monthly rail pass is $309 from Millburn or Short Hills, $362 from Summit — a $636 annual difference, which is small next to the tax gap running the other way.

The town-level snapshots on this site quote slightly different door-to-Penn figures (~32 minutes for Millburn, ~38 for Summit) because they measure a different thing than a station timetable. I would not plan a life around either number. Pull the actual NJ Transit timetable for the specific peak train you would take, and then walk from the house to the platform once, with a stopwatch, before you write an offer. That is the number that governs your next ten years.

Both towns also have direct I-78 access, along with Route 24, so a drive-in or a drive-to-Newark day is workable from either.

What are the honest trade-offs?

The tax gap is the real story, and it is bigger than the price gap. Millburn's 2025 average residential bill was $26,292; Summit's was $19,697. That $6,595 a year is roughly $550 a month of carrying cost that never touches your principal. Millburn's median is $155,000 higher and its average tax bill is higher too, so the price gap and the tax gap compound rather than offset. Run both towns as monthly numbers, not as purchase prices — that is where the real distance between them shows up.

Millburn's median is not a Short Hills number. Buyers routinely see $1,250,000, tour Short Hills, and discover the houses they liked are a different market entirely. If Short Hills is the goal, budget from Short Hills comps, not from the township median.

A 10/10 rating is a district average, not a guarantee about your school. Both districts earn the top rating. Neither rating tells you how the specific elementary school your address feeds into performs. Ask for the exact catchment boundary before you offer — in both towns, two houses a few blocks apart can feed different schools.

Nothing sits. Ten and eleven days on market means well-priced homes go to offers in a weekend. If you are still shopping for a mortgage when you find the house, you have already lost it. Get pre-approved, retain your attorney, and know your walk-away number before you tour.

County lines matter more than buyers expect. Millburn is Essex County; Summit is Union. That changes your county tax line, your county services, and — if you care — which regional comparisons you should be reading.

Frequently asked questions

Is Millburn or Summit more expensive?
Millburn, on both counts. Median $1,250,000 against $1,095,000, and a 2025 average tax bill of $26,292 against $19,697 — $6,595 a year apart.

Which has the shorter commute?
Millburn. Millburn station is scheduled at 35 minutes to New York Penn, Short Hills at 37, Summit at 44. All three are one-seat Midtown Direct rides.

Do both towns have top schools?
Yes — both districts rate 10/10 on GreatSchools, feeding Millburn High School and Summit High School respectively. Verify your specific elementary catchment before you offer.

Is Short Hills the same as Millburn?
Short Hills is a section of Millburn Township, not a separate town. Same district, same tax rate, its own Midtown Direct station at 37 minutes. It carries the township's highest prices, which is why the Millburn median sits below typical Short Hills trades.

How fast do homes sell?
Summit averages 10 days on market, Millburn 11. Both are among the fastest markets in the region.

What will my monthly cost actually be?
Price, taxes and rate together, not price alone. Start with the mortgage calculator and the closing costs calculator, and check every NJ town's average bill in the NJ property tax guide.

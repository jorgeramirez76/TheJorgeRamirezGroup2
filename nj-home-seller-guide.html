10 Planning Questions for Pricing, Preparation and Offer Review

Quick answer: Before listing, document recent comparable evidence, record the purpose and written scope for each preparation choice, review the marketing plan, request current written cost estimates, identify the professional who will answer each specialist question, and compare offers on both price and terms. This checklist separates property and market evidence from legal, tax, appraisal, engineering and other conclusions that require the appropriately qualified professional.

The 10 questions inside the planning guide

  1. What evidence supports the proposed list-price range? Compare recent closed sales, current competition, location, condition and market date.
  2. Which preparation items have a documented purpose? Separate safety or repair needs from optional presentation choices and obtain written scopes.
  3. What exactly is in the marketing plan? Review photography, description, distribution, showing access, reporting and fair-housing compliance.
  4. Which facts are property-specific? Confirm taxes, permits, certificates, association records, disclosures and known condition information.
  5. What should a qualified specialist inspect? Assign legal, tax, title, appraisal, engineering and environmental questions to the corresponding professional.
  6. Which selling costs apply? Obtain current estimates for brokerage services, the New Jersey Realty Transfer Fee, legal or settlement work, payoff, taxes and agreed credits.
  7. How does timing affect the plan? Coordinate occupancy, access, purchase of another home and the dates written into the contract.
  8. What does the listing agreement say? Review services, duration, compensation, authorization, cancellation and how compensation may be paid.
  9. How will showings protect the property? Set notice, access, security, pets, feedback and any occupancy constraints in advance.
  10. How will offers be compared? Evaluate price together with financing, contingencies, credits, dates and evidence of the buyer's ability to perform.

Start with a property-specific market estimate

A Comparative Market Analysis uses recent comparable evidence to develop a broker price opinion and marketing estimate; it is not the equivalent of an appraisal by a New Jersey licensed or certified real estate appraiser. Start with the home-value review, use the New Jersey valuation evidence guide to understand the method and its limits, review the seller-service plan, or open the NYC-to-New Jersey relocation guide when a move is part of the decision.

Verify statewide rules with primary sources

Use the New Jersey Division of Taxation Realty Transfer Fee page for the current statutory fee schedules and instructions. The New Jersey Department of Banking and Insurance Bulletin 24-11 explains that broker compensation is fully negotiable and not set by law. Ask an attorney, tax adviser, settlement professional and brokerage representative to apply current rules and written agreements to the transaction.

What makes searching for a home in NJ different

Most national search tools sort by bedrooms and price. In NJ, the four things that actually move a home's value aren't in a Zillow filter — they're specific to how NJ commutes, schools, and transit lines work.

Premium 1

Midtown Direct access

Direct trains to NY Penn (no Hoboken transfer) command a premium. Towns like Summit, Chatham, Madison, Millburn, Maplewood, South Orange, and Westfield sit on this line. All else equal, walking distance to a Midtown Direct station adds meaningful value versus a car-dependent equivalent.

Premium 2

Walk-to-train radius

Under a 10-minute walk to the platform is where the pricing shifts. A house half a mile from the station and a house two miles from the same station live in different markets — same town, different comps. Filter for this, not just the zip code.

Value play

Raritan Valley Line

Cranford, Westfield, Fanwood, Plainfield, Bound Brook — the Raritan Valley Line has a one-seat ride to Newark and requires a transfer to reach NYC. That transfer typically translates to 15–25% less per square foot than Midtown Direct towns with comparable schools.

Schools

Top-rated districts by county

Millburn (Essex), Chatham (Morris), Westfield & Summit (Union), Bernards Township (Somerset), and Edison (Middlesex) consistently anchor the top NJ Monthly rankings. School boundaries can literally split a street — always confirm the exact district before making an offer.

New to NJ? Read this before you search.

If you're relocating from NYC, Philly, or out of state, the fastest way to narrow 138 towns down to 3–5 candidates is to pick your commute route first. Working in Midtown Manhattan? Look at the Midtown Direct line. Working in the Financial District? PATH-served Jersey City and Hoboken beat any suburban commute. Hybrid, going in 2 days a week? The Raritan Valley Line towns give you 20–25% more house per dollar for the same overall lifestyle.

Once you know your commute, filter by school district if you have kids, then by walk score around the station. That's how most out-of-state buyers actually find their house — not by scrolling Zillow.

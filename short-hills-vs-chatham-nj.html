Short Hills vs Chatham, NJ: Which Is Right for You?

Short Hills and Chatham have different scheduled Midtown Direct trips, housing prices, tax bills, and school assignments. Verify current NJ Transit schedules, property-specific costs, and NJDOE school information independently.

Jorge Ramirez · NJ Real Estate License #1754604 · Keller Williams Premier Properties · Updated August 6, 2026

Who is Short Hills for, and who is Chatham for?

First, a point that trips up half the buyers who search this comparison: Short Hills is not a municipality. It is the most prominent section of Millburn Township, in Essex County. It has its own station and its own ZIP code, but its property taxes, its town government, and its schools are Millburn's. Chatham, by contrast, is in Morris County and is genuinely two municipalities — Chatham Borough and Chatham Township — that share one school district.

With that settled: Short Hills is the buyer who wants the shortest one-seat ride on the Morris & Essex line and is buying at or above $1.35M anyway. Chatham has a separate school district; review current NJDOE reports directly, will accept eleven more minutes on the train, and would rather put the difference — nearly half a million dollars at the median, plus $7,921 a year in average taxes — into the house or the retirement account.

Choose Short Hills if…

The commute is the constraint and the budget is not. Verify the scheduled trip to New York Penn directly with NJ Transit. The local district feeds Millburn High School; review current NJDOE reports and address assignments separately. The housing runs from grand estates on half-acre to multi-acre lots along Old Short Hills Road, to the expanded colonials and newer construction near Glenwood Elementary, to the more secluded splits and colonials toward Hobart Gap and the South Mountain Reservation.

The trade you accept: a $1,350,000 median and a $26,292 average residential tax bill — the highest of any station town on my map.

Consider if: Households where one or two people ride the train five days a week, buying above $1.3M, who want the shortest possible Midtown Direct ride and local schools in the same purchase.

Choose Chatham if…

You want the same school-quality tier and the same one-seat Midtown Direct ride, and you are willing to sit on the train eleven minutes longer for it. The $875,000 median is roughly $475,000 under Short Hills, and the $18,371 average tax bill runs about $7,900 a year lighter. Chatham also gives you two very different housing markets in one district: a walkable Borough with a real Main Street minutes from the platform, and a Township of larger lots and newer construction.

The trade you accept: a 48-minute listed ride, a $376 monthly pass instead of $309, and an 18-day median market that is if anything faster than Short Hills.

Consider if: Compare current NJDOE district information, downtown access, and property-specific prices, and buyers who would rather own more house than shave eleven minutes off the ride.

What is the NYC commute like from Short Hills and Chatham?

This is the cleanest comparison on my whole train map, because there is nothing to untangle: both stations sit on the Morris & Essex Main Line and both get Midtown Direct service straight into New York Penn Station. No transfer at Newark, no bus, no asterisk. Short Hills is listed at 37 minutes, Chatham at 48.

Eleven minutes each way is roughly an hour and fifty minutes a week. Some buyers price that at nothing. Others have done five years of it and will pay half a million dollars never to do it again. Both positions are defensible — but decide it deliberately, because it is the single largest lifestyle difference between these towns.

The fares differ too: $309 a month from Short Hills, $376 from Chatham, straight off NJ Transit's New York Penn Station chart. That is $804 more per year from Chatham, which barely dents the $7,921 annual tax difference running the other way. Run both on my NJ train map, which puts commute time, monthly pass, and average tax bill in one row per station.

How do the schools compare in Short Hills and Chatham?

Short Hills is served by Millburn Township Public Schools, which has current information in NJDOE School Performance Reports and runs roughly 4,800 students across five elementary schools — Deerfield, Glenwood, Hartshorn, South Mountain, and Wyoming — plus Millburn Middle School and Millburn High School. Chatham is served by the School District of the Chathams, a unified district covering both the Borough and the Township with roughly 4,000 students, six elementary schools, Chatham Middle School, and Chatham High School, is reported separately in NJDOE School Performance Reports.

Read that gap honestly: it is narrow, and it is district-wide. Elementary catchments vary inside both towns. If a specific elementary school matters to you, the address matters more than the district average — check the catchment before you make an offer, not after.

What are the honest trade-offs between Short Hills and Chatham?

Against Short Hills

$26,292 is the highest average residential property tax bill of any station town on my map, and it does not go away when the mortgage does. On a thirty-year horizon that is a very large number. You are also buying into a market where the median is $1,350,000 and, as my Short Hills page notes, prices vary sharply by pocket — Old Short Hills and premium Glenwood locations run well above the median while the Millburn side of the township starts materially lower. A township median is a poor guide to what any given street costs.

Against Chatham

Eleven extra minutes each way is real, and it compounds. Chatham also splits into two municipalities with different tax rates and different housing stock, so "Chatham" on Zillow can mean a 1920s colonial four minutes' walk from the platform or a newer house on three-quarters of an acre that requires a car for everything. Know which Chatham you are shopping. And at 18 days on market, Chatham is moving slightly faster than Short Hills — do not assume the lower price means a calmer process.

Against both

Both towns are expensive relative to almost everything else on the Morris & Essex line, and both attract the same buyer, which keeps inventory thin. If your budget lands under $875,000, look one or two stops further out before you stretch. My Communities directory covers the alternatives.

Short Hills vs Chatham: frequently asked questions

Is Short Hills its own town?

No — Short Hills is the most prominent section of Millburn Township in Essex County. It has its own NJ Transit station and its own ZIP code, but Millburn Township sets the taxes and runs the schools. Any Short Hills tax figure you see is a Millburn Township figure.

Is Chatham cheaper than Short Hills?

Substantially. Chatham's median home sale price is about $875,000 against Short Hills' $1,350,000 — a gap of roughly $475,000. The average residential property tax bill favors Chatham as well, $18,371 against Millburn Township's $26,292.

How much longer is the Chatham commute?

About eleven minutes. Short Hills is listed at 37 minutes to New York Penn and Chatham at 48. Both are Midtown Direct with no transfer. The monthly pass is $309 from Short Hills and $376 from Chatham.

Do Chatham Borough and Chatham Township share a school district?

Yes. The School District of the Chathams serves both municipalities. The two towns stay separate for government and taxes, but the schools are one district ending at Chatham High School.

Which one holds value better?

I will not put a forecast in writing. What I will tell you is what the current data shows: both are local public schools with one-seat Midtown Direct rides, and both are selling in under three weeks. Those are the two attributes that have historically kept demand steady in this corridor.

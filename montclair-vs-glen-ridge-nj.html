Montclair vs Glen Ridge, NJ: Which Is Right for You?

Both towns post an $875,000 median and a 9/10 district, so price is not the deciding factor. Choose Glen Ridge for a small borough with its own high school and a $23,673 average tax bill. Choose Montclair for scale — more downtown, more dining — and $22,487 in taxes.

Who each town is actually for

Glen Ridge suits the buyer who wants a small, self-contained borough running its own schools end to end, and who will trade the highest tax bill in this comparison for it. Montclair suits the buyer who wants the town to supply the nightlife, dining, and arts — and who is comfortable moving fast in a 9-day market.

The unusual part of this matchup is that the sticker price is identical. Two Essex County towns that share a border and a rail line, both at an $875,000 median, both rated 9/10. Everything you are actually choosing between happens after you close.

Choose Montclair if…

You want the town itself to be the amenity. Montclair is the larger of the two by a wide margin, with the restaurant and arts density that makes a Friday night a walk instead of a drive. Its 9/10 district feeds Montclair High School, and the average residential tax bill is $22,487 — $1,186 a year less than Glen Ridge.

The cost of that scale is speed. Homes average 9 days on market. If you tour on Sunday and want to think it over until Wednesday, you will lose houses in Montclair.

Ideal for: Buyers who want walkable dining and arts as part of daily life, want the lower of the two tax bills, and are pre-approved and ready to write inside a week.

Choose Glen Ridge if…

You want a small borough that runs its own everything. Glen Ridge has its own district feeding Glen Ridge High School, also rated 9/10, in a town a fraction of Montclair's size — which is exactly the appeal for buyers who find Montclair too big.

You pay for that scale in reverse. Glen Ridge's average residential tax bill is $23,673, the highest in this comparison, and $14,416 of it funds the schools — more school spending per household than Montclair despite the far smaller town. Homes average 12 days on market, which is the one place Glen Ridge gives a buyer more room than Montclair.

Ideal for: Buyers who want a small, residential, self-governing borough with its own K-12 district, and who would rather pay it annually in taxes than give it up in town character.

How do the NYC commutes compare?

Both towns ride the Montclair-Boonton line, so this is not a line-versus-line decision — it's a station-versus-station one.

The published figures on our own community pages are soft, and I'd rather say so than pretend otherwise. The Glen Ridge page lists roughly 32 minutes in one table and about 38 in another. Montclair's lists about 38 in one place and 40 in another. The honest read is that both towns land somewhere in the mid-30s to about 40 minutes, and the spread inside each town — which station, which train, whether you catch a peak express — is wider than the gap between them.

I'm also not going to quote you a monthly pass for either. The NJ train map on this site carries station-level fares for four lines — Midtown Direct, the Gladstone Branch, the Raritan Valley Line, and the Northeast Corridor — and Montclair-Boonton is not among them. No verified fare means no fare printed here. Pull your exact station on NJ Transit before you build a monthly budget around it.

What I'd tell a buyer to do instead: pick the two or three streets you'd actually buy on, walk from each to the nearest platform, and time it. That number decides more about your next five years than any published average.

What are the honest trade-offs?

Identical medians do not mean identical houses

Both towns show an $875,000 median, but a median is the middle of a spread, not a price list. Montclair's inventory runs across a much larger and more varied town; Glen Ridge's is smaller and more uniform. The same $875,000 buys different things in each, and only side-by-side comps within a half-mile will tell you which one is buying you more.

Glen Ridge's tax bill is the real premium

$23,673 versus $22,487 is about $1,186 a year, or roughly $11,900 over a decade before any further increases — and both towns are increasing, Glen Ridge +3.0% and Montclair +4.0% year over year. Because the purchase prices match, this line is the entire financial difference between the two towns. Do not let it get buried under the mortgage math.

A 9/10 on both sides is not a tie

Same rating, very different structures: a small borough district versus a large one. Which suits your child is a question about class size, program depth, and school culture — not about a number that happens to match. Sit in on both before you decide, and pull the NJ Department of Education report for each.

What I could not verify

No monthly rail fare for either town, and no single reliable commute time — our own pages disagree with themselves by several minutes in both towns. I've printed the ranges rather than picking the flattering end.

Montclair vs Glen Ridge: common questions

Is Glen Ridge more expensive than Montclair?

Not at purchase — both post an $875,000 median. Glen Ridge is more expensive to hold: $23,673 in average annual property tax against Montclair's $22,487.

Do the two towns share a school district?

No. Glen Ridge High School and Montclair High School serve separate districts, both rated 9/10.

Which town sells faster?

Montclair, at 9 days on market against Glen Ridge's 12.

Which has the better commute to New York?

Both are on the Montclair-Boonton line, and the published figures overlap — Glen Ridge roughly 32 to 38 minutes, Montclair roughly 38 to 40. Verify your specific station on NJ Transit's timetable.

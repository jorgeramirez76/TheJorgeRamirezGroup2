Who is Cranford for, and who is Garwood for?

These two towns share a border, a county, and a train line. They do not share a school district, a tax bill, or a price point. Cranford is the bigger, more self-contained option: its own high school, its own downtown, its own identity. Garwood is the borough next door where the same commute costs about $100,000 less in purchase price and about $2,000 less per year in property taxes.

Garwood and Cranford have different current price points, housing stock, municipal services, and district structures. Compare property condition, recent sales, train schedules, taxes, and address-specific school information independently.

Choose Cranford if…

You want one town to do everything. Cranford has its own K-12 district ending at Cranford High School, a downtown large enough to hold a real Saturday, and a station that puts you 40 minutes from New York Penn on paper. At a $649,000 median it is not the cheapest Union County commuter town, but it is the one where you stop needing to drive to the next town over.

The trade you accept: the higher tax bill of the two, $13,728 on average, and an 11-day median market that gives you no room to deliberate.

Choose Garwood if…

Your budget is the constraint and your commute is not negotiable. Garwood is on the same Raritan Valley Line, three listed minutes further from New York Penn, at a $549,000 median — and its average residential tax bill of $11,724 runs about $2,000 a year under Cranford's. That is real money against a mortgage payment.

What is the commute like from Cranford and Garwood?

Both towns are on the Raritan Valley Line, and that one fact shapes everything about this decision. The NJ train map lists Cranford at 40 minutes to New York Penn Station and Garwood at 43. The three-minute difference between them is noise. The line itself is the thing to understand.

Raritan Valley riders transfer at Newark Penn, which adds roughly 10 minutes to the trip. That is why my Cranford page puts the real door-to-Midtown number closer to 50 minutes and my Garwood page closer to 52. If you are comparing these towns against Midtown Direct towns on the Morris & Essex line, compare transfer to no transfer, not 40 minutes to 44.

One practical difference: Cranford appears on NJ Transit's New York Penn Station monthly pass chart at $309. Garwood does not appear on that chart, so I cannot quote you a Garwood pass price and will not invent one. If the monthly rail cost is decisive for you, price it at the ticket window before you write an offer.

Cranford vs Garwood: frequently asked questions

Is Garwood cheaper than Cranford?

Yes. Garwood's median home sale price is about $549,000 against Cranford's $649,000 — roughly a $100,000 gap. The average residential property tax bill follows the same direction: $11,724 in Garwood versus $13,728 in Cranford, per the 2025 NJ Department of Community Affairs figures on my train map.

Do Cranford and Garwood share a high school?

No. Cranford runs its own district through Cranford High School. Garwood sends its high school students to Arthur L. Johnson High School in Clark. This is the single biggest structural difference between the two towns.

Are Cranford and Garwood on the same train line?

Yes — both have their own station on the Raritan Valley Line. Cranford is listed at 40 minutes to New York Penn and Garwood at 43. Both involve a transfer at Newark Penn, which adds roughly 10 minutes.

Which town sells faster?

Cranford, by about three days: an 11-day typical time on market versus Garwood's 14. In practice both are fast. If you see a house you like on Thursday and plan to sleep on it through the weekend, expect to lose it.

Can I get a bigger house in Garwood for the same money?

Usually, yes — that is the entire argument for Garwood. But house-by-house comparisons beat medians every time. A median is the middle of a range, not a promise about the specific listing you are looking at. Send me the two addresses and I will tell you which is the better buy.

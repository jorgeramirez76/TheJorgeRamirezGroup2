Selling With a Tenant, an Estate, or a Divorce in Montclair

These are the situations cash buyers advertise hardest against, because sellers in them assume a normal sale is impossible. Usually it is not.

  • Tenant-occupied: New Jersey tenant protections survive the sale, so the lease and the notice requirements shape your buyer pool more than your price does.
  • Inherited or in probate: you generally need authority to sell before you can close, and that timing is workable if handled in parallel rather than in sequence.
  • Divorce: the practical problem is decision-making, not marketing. Agreeing in advance on list price, response to offers, and who signs what prevents the delays that cost money.
  • Deferred maintenance: in Essex County, inspection findings are negotiated, not fatal. Knowing which items a buyer will press on lets you decide what to fix before listing instead of conceding it afterward.

What It Costs to Sell in Montclair

Plan on 6–8% of the sale price in total: commission, the tiered NJ realty transfer fee, attorney fees of roughly $1,500–$2,500, and pre-listing preparation. You can model your own number with the seller net proceeds calculator, and the NJ selling costs guide breaks down each line item.

If your sale is above $1,000,000, add the Graduated Percent Fee. New Jersey replaced the old buyer-paid 1% “mansion tax” with a seller-paid fee of 1% to 3.5% of the entire sale price (P.L. 2025, c. 69, for deeds recorded on or after July 10, 2025). It is a cliff rather than a bracket, so a $2,000,000 sale owes $20,000 and a sale one dollar higher owes about $40,000. Combined with the standard transfer fee, the state alone takes roughly 2.0% of a $1.5M sale and 3.1% of a $2.5M sale — before commission. The 6–8% figure above is for sales under $1M; run yours through the net proceeds calculator, which now includes it.

Montclair Seller Questions

Do I need to renovate before selling in Montclair?

Rarely a full renovation. The work that reliably returns money is cosmetic and mechanical: paint, lighting, floors, and anything an inspector will flag. Large discretionary projects usually return less than they cost. I walk the house first and tell you which items are worth doing and which are not.

Can I sell a Montclair house with a tenant in it?

Yes. New Jersey tenant protections survive the sale, so the lease and notice requirements shape your buyer pool more than your price does. Marketed to the right buyers — including investors who want the tenant in place — an occupied property does not have to be discounted.

Can I sell an inherited Montclair house before probate finishes?

Usually you need authority to sell before you can close, but that timing is workable when it is handled in parallel rather than in sequence. Selling as-is and selling at a discount are two separate decisions that get conflated constantly.

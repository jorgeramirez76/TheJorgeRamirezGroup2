How Much Are Closing Costs in NJ? The Complete Buyer & Seller Breakdown

Last updated  ·  August 2026

Buyers typically pay 2-5% of the purchase price. Sellers pay 8-10%. Here is every line item, with worked tables from $400K to $1.5M — and the one big fee buyers no longer pay.

By Jorge Ramirez | Keller Williams Premier Properties | Updated August 2026 · NJ licensed agent since 2017, License #1754604

The Quick Answer

In New Jersey, buyers typically pay 2% to 5% of the purchase price in closing costs. On a $600,000 home with 20% down and a conventional loan, that works out to roughly $18,231 — attorney, title, lender fees, inspection, recording, and prepaid taxes and insurance, all in.

Sellers pay far more: typically 8% to 10% of the sale price, because commission and the NJ Realty Transfer Fee sit on their side of the table. The full seller breakdown lives in our guide to NJ home selling costs; the summary tables below cover both sides.

One thing to get out of the way immediately, because half the articles on the internet still get it wrong: buyers no longer pay the 1% mansion tax in New Jersey. That fee was moved to the seller for deeds recorded on or after July 10, 2025. More on that below.

New Jersey runs higher than most states on closing costs for a simple reason: it is an attorney state, property taxes are high, and lenders collect months of those taxes in escrow at the closing table. None of that is optional — but several line items are negotiable, and knowing which ones is worth real money.

Buyer Closing Costs in NJ, Line by Line

These are the same line items and figures our NJ closing costs calculator uses. Run your own numbers there in one click; here is what each item actually means.

Attorney Fees ($1,500 – $3,500)

NJ practice is for an attorney to review the contract, negotiate during attorney review, handle title, and represent you at closing. Flat fees are typical. The tables below assume $2,000.

Title Insurance (~0.5% of the purchase price)

Protects you and the lender against title defects. It is a one-time premium paid at closing, and most lenders require it. On a $600,000 purchase, that is about $3,000, plus roughly $450 for the title search and settlement charges.

Lender Fees (0.5% – 1.5% of the loan)

Origination, underwriting, and application fees. These are negotiable — always get a Loan Estimate from at least two lenders and compare. The tables below use ~0.75% of the loan amount for origination, plus $650 for the appraisal and $350 for credit report and processing.

Inspection & Appraisal

Inspection runs about $500-$900 and the appraisal about $500-$800. Both are paid before closing, not at the table, but they count toward the total cash you need to buy.

Recording & Transfer Fees ($300 – $800)

County recording fees and, in some cases, a small buyer portion of transfer charges. A survey, if your lender or attorney requires one, adds about $500.

Prepaids: Taxes and Insurance Escrow

Lenders require 2-6 months of property tax and insurance in escrow at closing, plus interest from your closing date to the end of the month. In high-tax NJ towns, this is often the single largest closing-cost line item — and it is the one figure on this page that depends entirely on which town you buy in, not on the purchase price.

FHA loans add an upfront mortgage insurance premium of 1.75% of the loan; VA loans add a funding fee of 2.15%. Cash buyers skip every lender line and the prepaid interest.

The 1% Mansion Tax: Buyers No Longer Pay It

Buyers used to pay 1% of the price on homes over $1,000,000. That fee was eliminated for buyers by P.L. 2025, c. 69, effective for deeds recorded on or after July 10, 2025 — the statute was amended to strike “grantee” and insert “grantor.”

It was replaced by a seller-paid Graduated Percent Fee of 1% to 3.5% of the entire sale price, which applies only when the price exceeds $1 million: 1% up to $2M, 2% up to $2.5M, 2.5% up to $3M, 3% up to $3.5M, and 3.5% above that. Each rate applies to the whole price, not just the portion above the threshold. If you are buying a $1.2M home in Summit or Short Hills, you owe none of it — the seller owes $12,000. Legal responsibility sits with the seller, though the Division of Taxation notes the parties may apportion the burden by contract, so read your contract.

Seller Closing Costs in NJ

The seller's side is a different animal — bigger numbers, different line items. The two that matter most:

Agent Commission (negotiable — the largest item)

Commission rates are not set by law and are negotiated between the seller and the listing agent, then typically split between the listing agent and the buyer's agent. The table below uses the same illustrative amounts as our selling costs guide — $30,000-$36,000 on a $600K sale, $50,000-$60,000 on $1M — purely to show scale.

NJ Realty Transfer Fee (RTF)

A state tax the seller pays at closing, calculated on a marginal sliding scale. For sales above $350,000, the rates run from $2.90 to $6.05 per $500 of the price, with each rate applying only to the portion of the price inside its band. Worked through, that is $5,185 on a $600,000 sale and $9,575 on $1,000,000. The full band-by-band schedule is in the selling costs guide.

Graduated Percent Fee (seller-paid, above $1M only)

The replacement for the old mansion tax, described above. Zero at $1M or below; 1% of the entire price from just over $1M to $2M — so $15,000 on a $1.5M sale.

How Much Cash Do You Actually Need to Close?

Closing costs are only half the answer. Cash to close is your down payment plus closing costs. On that $600,000 home with 20% down: $120,000 down plus roughly $18,231 in closing costs — about $138,000 total. Remember that the inspection and appraisal are paid earlier in the process, so a small slice of that money leaves your account weeks before closing day.

Which Closing Costs Can You Negotiate?

  • Lender fees: The most negotiable item on the page. Get a Loan Estimate from at least two lenders and compare line by line — origination fees vary widely for the same rate.
  • Attorney fees: Flat fees vary by firm. Ask up front what the fee covers.
  • Seller concessions: Buyers can negotiate a credit toward closing costs during attorney review, especially on homes that have sat on the market. This is a strategy conversation, not a form field — it depends on the specific listing and how much leverage you have.
  • What you cannot negotiate: Government charges — recording fees, the Realty Transfer Fee, the Graduated Percent Fee — are fixed by law.

Frequently Asked Questions About NJ Closing Costs

How much are closing costs in NJ for buyers?

New Jersey buyers typically pay 2% to 5% of the purchase price in closing costs. On a $600,000 home with 20% down and a conventional loan, expect roughly $18,231 including attorney fees, title insurance, lender fees, inspection, recording, and prepaid taxes and insurance. The biggest line items are prepaid property taxes, lender origination, title insurance, and the attorney fee.

Do buyers still pay the 1% mansion tax in NJ?

No. The buyer-paid 1% mansion tax was eliminated by P.L. 2025, c. 69, effective for deeds recorded on or after July 10, 2025. It was replaced by a seller-paid Graduated Percent Fee of 1% to 3.5% of the entire sale price, which applies only when the price exceeds $1 million. A buyer owes none of it, though the parties may apportion the burden by contract.

Who pays closing costs in NJ, the buyer or the seller?

Both, but different sets. Buyers pay their own attorney, title insurance, lender fees, inspection, appraisal, recording fees, and prepaid taxes and insurance — typically 2-5% of the purchase price. Sellers pay the agent commission, the NJ Realty Transfer Fee, their own attorney, and, above $1 million, the Graduated Percent Fee — typically 8-10% of the sale price in total.

How much are seller closing costs in NJ?

Total seller costs typically run 8% to 10% of the sale price, with commission the largest item. The NJ Realty Transfer Fee alone is approximately $5,185 on a $600,000 sale and $9,575 on a $1,000,000 sale. Sales above $1 million also owe the seller-paid Graduated Percent Fee of 1% to 3.5% of the entire price. See the full selling costs guide for the complete breakdown.

Can I negotiate closing costs in NJ?

Several lines are negotiable. Lender fees vary widely — get a Loan Estimate from at least two lenders and compare. Attorney flat fees vary by firm. Buyers can also negotiate seller concessions toward closing costs during attorney review, especially on homes that have sat on the market. Fixed government charges like recording fees are not negotiable.

How much cash do I need to close on a house in NJ?

Cash to close is your down payment plus closing costs. On a $600,000 home with 20% down, that is $120,000 down plus roughly $18,231 in closing costs — about $138,000 total. Inspection and appraisal are paid before closing day, but they still count toward the total cash you need to buy.

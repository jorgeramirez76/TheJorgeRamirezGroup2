Professional stagers don't advertise this, but IKEA has a short list of pieces that consistently make NJ homes show better. Here's what they actually buy — and why each one works.

Staging Doesn't Have to Cost a Fortune to Work

I've walked hundreds of NJ homes — in Westfield, Summit, Chatham, Maplewood, Cranford, Madison — and I can tell you with certainty that the difference between a home that gets four offers and a home that sits is almost never the renovation. It's almost always how the home feels when buyers walk in.

Staging creates that feeling. And you don't need a $5,000 staging bill to do it.

Professional stagers have a short list of IKEA pieces they return to repeatedly because those pieces photograph cleanly, read as neutral and intentional to buyers, and cost a fraction of their impact on the sale. I'm going to give you that list, with the real estate logic behind each piece — because understanding why something works is the difference between using it correctly and wasting your money.

One important caveat before we get into it: these pieces work within a staged home, not as a substitute for decluttering and deep cleaning. If the house is cluttered, these won't fix it. If the house is clean and clear, these will give it the visual finish that makes buyers linger — and lingering buyers make offers.

The 7 IKEA Pieces That Actually Move the Needle on NJ Home Sales

1. BESTÅ Storage Combination — Living Room

$250–$400 depending on configuration

The BESTÅ is the most consistently useful staging piece I've seen stagers use in NJ living rooms. Here's why: NJ homes, especially colonials and split-levels in Union and Essex Counties, often have living rooms with no built-in storage. The result is furniture clustered against walls and media equipment on mismatched stands that buyers see and immediately calculate as problems.

A BESTÅ configuration — TV stand and flanking cabinets — solves three buyer objections at once: it hides the cables and electronics behind clean cabinet doors, it provides visual grounding for a room that often lacks it, and it reads as built-in to buyers walking through quickly.

NJ Seller Note

In older NJ homes where the living room and dining room flow together without separation, a BESTÅ unit anchors the living side and creates a visual break. That distinction — which doesn't require a wall — makes rooms feel larger and more purposeful in listing photos.

2. BILLY Bookcase — Home Office or Study

$90–$160 per unit

Post-2020, a home office space went from a nice-to-have to a buyer requirement in NJ. The problem in many NJ homes is that the "office" is a spare bedroom with a folding table. BILLY bookcases flanking a simple desk transform that room from a dumping ground into a space that buyers can actually picture themselves working in.

More importantly, bookcases read as thought — someone set this room up intentionally. That's the signal staging sends. It's not about the furniture; it's about convincing buyers this home has been taken care of.

Two BILLY units flanking a LINNMON desk runs about $350. That same room without the treatment costs you $10,000 to $20,000 in perceived value when buyers are comparing it against a competing listing that has an actual home office.

NJ Seller Note

In Summit, Chatham, and Madison — where buyers are overwhelmingly NYC commuters working hybrid schedules — a home office that actually looks like an office is not staging. It's a feature. Treat it like one.

3. HEMNES 6-Drawer Dresser — Primary Bedroom

$350–$500

The primary bedroom is where NJ buyers make their most emotional decision. They walk in, and within 20 seconds they're picturing their morning routine. A bedroom with mismatched furniture, a cheap dresser from 2003, or worse — no dresser because it's in a pile in the corner — breaks that picture immediately.

The HEMNES dresser has clean lines, comes in white and dark stain finishes that read as timeless rather than trendy, and is large enough to signal real storage without overwhelming a room. It's the kind of piece buyers barely notice — which is exactly what you want. Good staging is invisible. You don't want buyers complimenting your furniture; you want them to feel at home.

NJ Seller Note

If you're selling a three-bedroom Colonial in Cranford, Scotch Plains, or Westfield and the primary bedroom dresser looks dated, replace it before listing photos. The photo is the first showing. The bedroom photo is the photo buyers stare at longest.

4. KALLAX Shelving Unit — Kids' Rooms or Entryway

$100–$200

KALLAX units are the secret weapon for two spaces that NJ sellers consistently overlook: kids' rooms and the entryway. In a kids' room, a KALLAX with fabric bins replaces the visual chaos of toys on the floor with something that looks controlled and even charming. Buyers with kids see it and feel relief rather than dread.

In the entryway — which is often the first thing buyers see in NJ townhouses and smaller colonials — a KALLAX cube unit or narrow KALLAX shelf with a few baskets says "storage was thought about here." That first impression sets the buyer's mental model for every room that follows.

NJ Seller Note

In NJ's older housing stock, entryway closets are small or nonexistent. A KALLAX bench configuration (the horizontal 2x4 unit with legs) gives buyers a visual storage solution for the entryway that makes the absence of a coat closet feel intentional rather than like a deficiency.

5. POÄNG Armchair — Reading Nook or Corner

$130–$180

Every NJ home has at least one corner or awkward alcove that sellers don't know what to do with. An empty corner in a living room or bedroom reads as dead space in listing photos — and dead space makes rooms look small. A POÄNG chair, side table, and a small lamp turn that corner into a vignette that photographs beautifully and communicates "this home has character."

The POÄNG's bent-birch frame is distinctive enough to add visual interest without competing with the room. Buyers photographing the house on their phones — which every buyer does now — will photograph that corner. It goes on the "I love this" list.

NJ Seller Note

In the primary bedroom of NJ Victorians and Colonials — which often have bay windows or window seats — a POÄNG and lamp positioned to read the light is the most effective $150 I've seen spent in a staging context.

6. LACK Side Tables — Bedroom and Living Room

$20–$25 each

LACK side tables are the most ruthlessly practical staging item on this list. They are cheap, clean-lined, and come in black and white finishes that work in almost any NJ home. Two LACK tables flanking a bed as nightstands costs $50 and makes a room look finished in a way that's hard to achieve otherwise without spending 10 times as much.

The point isn't that LACK is impressive furniture. The point is that mismatched or absent nightstands make a bedroom look unfinished, and an unfinished bedroom triggers buyers to start looking for other things that aren't done. Staging is risk management. You're not decorating; you're removing objections.

NJ Seller Note

In spare bedrooms that are currently being used as storage, two LACK tables, a simple bed frame, and a decluttered closet turn a buyer liability into a selling point. NJ buyers count bedrooms. Give them four rooms that look like bedrooms, not three bedrooms and a storage room.

7. ÄPPLARYD or EKTORP Sofa — Unfurnished Living Rooms

$600–$900

This one requires the biggest commitment, but it earns the biggest return. Empty living rooms consistently underperform in NJ showings. Buyers cannot gauge the size of a room without furniture in it. An empty 14x18 living room reads as either too large or too small — buyers lose their reference point and start generating concerns instead of enthusiasm.

An ÄPPLARYD sofa in light gray or beige, flanked by two LACK tables and anchored by a simple area rug, gives buyers the room they came to see. The EKTORP has a slipcover design that reads as casual and livable — which is exactly what NJ family buyers want to feel in a living room.

If you're selling a vacant property — which is common when NJ sellers have already relocated — furniture rental starts at $800 to $1,200 per month. A purchased IKEA setup covers the same visual ground for less and you can resell the furniture after closing.

NJ Seller Note

I've listed vacant homes in Maplewood, Madison, and Florham Park. The ones that went on with empty rooms consistently took longer and sold for less. It costs more to leave rooms empty than to furnish them. This is not a debate in NJ's market.

What IKEA Can't Fix — And What You Need to Do First

Staging Amplifies, It Doesn't Compensate

Every staging piece on this list works inside a home that's already been decluttered and deep cleaned. If the closets are full, the counters are loaded, or the carpet hasn't been cleaned in two years, no amount of IKEA furniture will get you to multiple offers.

The order matters: declutter first, deep clean second, do any small repairs third, then stage. If you reverse that order — staging a cluttered house — you'll spend money on furniture that buyers walk right past while cataloging everything else that's wrong.

Frequently Asked Questions

Does staging really increase NJ home sale prices?

Yes. The National Association of Realtors consistently reports that staged homes sell faster and for more money than unstaged ones. In NJ's competitive markets — Summit, Westfield, Chatham, Maplewood, Cranford — where buyers are often comparing four to six homes in a single weekend, staging is the difference between a bidding war and a price reduction. Even modest staging improvements can shift buyer perception by tens of thousands of dollars.

Is IKEA furniture good for home staging in New Jersey?

For the right pieces, yes. IKEA works for staging because staging furniture doesn't need to last decades — it needs to look good in photos and in person for the duration of a listing. IKEA's clean lines and neutral palettes photograph well, which is critical since listing photos are where most NJ buyers make their first decision. The BILLY bookcase, BESTÅ storage system, and HEMNES bedroom pieces are consistently used by professional NJ stagers for exactly this reason.

How much should NJ home sellers spend on staging?

For most NJ sellers, a staging budget of $500 to $2,000 in furniture and accessories pays for itself many times over. The highest-leverage investments are in the living room, primary bedroom, and kitchen — the three spaces that most influence NJ buyer decisions. A few well-chosen IKEA pieces in those rooms plus decluttering and deep cleaning will outperform expensive full-home staging in the majority of NJ listings under $1.2M.

What rooms should NJ sellers focus on when staging?

In NJ homes, the priority rooms for staging investment are: (1) the kitchen, which drives the first visceral reaction from buyers; (2) the primary bedroom, where buyers picture their daily life; (3) the living room, where buyers decide if they can see themselves hosting; and (4) the primary bathroom. Secondary bedrooms, basements, and garages matter too, but returns diminish quickly once those four key spaces are right.

Should NJ home sellers rent staging furniture or buy IKEA?

It depends on the price point. For NJ homes under $800K, strategic IKEA purchases often make more financial sense than full rental staging. You'll spend less, retain ownership of the furniture after closing, and the visual result in key rooms can be nearly identical. For NJ homes above $1.2M — especially in Summit, Short Hills, or Chatham — professional staging with higher-end rentals typically returns more than it costs, because buyer expectations at that price point are calibrated to a specific aesthetic level.

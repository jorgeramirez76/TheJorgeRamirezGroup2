Restyle a NJ Home for Fall Showings: 7 Easy Changes

House Outlook’s newsletter this week included a Real Simple list of easy ways to restyle a home for fall. Most of it is textiles and light. For a New Jersey seller showing a house in October, when buyers arrive at 5:30 and the sun is already going, those same changes are staging, and they are cheap.

The tip House Outlook sent this week

Tucked into this week’s House Outlook newsletter was a Real Simple list of seven easy ways to restyle a home for fall. Nothing in it costs much. Swap some textiles, change the light, simplify a few surfaces, let the season in without redecorating. I read it the way I read most home articles now, as a New Jersey listing agent, and it is a fall showing checklist.

Why fall styling is staging in New Jersey

By mid-October the light is lower and the days are shorter. Buyers who work in the city tour Westfield, Summit, Chatham and Morristown after the train gets in, which means they walk into your living room as the sun leaves it. The houses I have seen show well in October are the ones that feel warm and lit at 5:30. The ones that struggle are the same houses that looked great in June, with the same furniture, now sitting in a dim room with bare floors.

The other thing about fall in my experience is that inventory thins and the buyers who remain are serious. Your preparation gets noticed because there is less to compare it to.

Seven changes that photograph well in October

  1. Swap the textiles, not the furniture. A heavier throw, two pillows in a warm neutral, and the summer linens packed away. Keep patterns quiet; the textile prep guide covers how they should look on camera.
  2. Layer the lighting. Three light sources in every main room, bulbs of the same warm color temperature, and the key lamps on timers so the house is lit before the buyer parks. Replace every dead bulb first.
  3. Clear and simplify the mantel and the entry. One seasonal element, not a display. The rule of three is the limit, and the entry table gets a bowl for keys and nothing else.
  4. Bring the season to the table. A bowl of apples or a few stems in a plain vase, cut to fit as in the fresh flowers guide. No themed tableware.
  5. Dress the porch and the front door. A clean mat, one planter of mums, a swept walk and a wiped storm door. Rake the leaves the morning of showings; the fall curb appeal checklist has the rest.
  6. Put the rugs back down. Bare hardwood reads cold in October light and echoes when buyers go quiet. A rug with a pad fixes both, as the noise guide explains.
  7. Fresh air, not fragrance. Open windows for twenty minutes before a showing, run the kitchen and bath fans, skip the plug-ins and the cinnamon candle. Buyers notice scent and wonder what it covers; see preparing indoor air for showings.

Jorge’s tip: Schedule the exterior photos now, while there is still color on the trees in Cranford and Scotch Plains, even if the interior is not ready. Interiors can be shot later. A bare-tree exterior taken in November is the photo your listing wears until spring.

What to leave out

Holiday decor that is personal or religious, inflatables on the lawn, a pumpkin on every porch step, and garlands wound around the banister. The garland is the one people argue with me about. It looks festive and it covers the handrail a buyer is supposed to be able to hold, which is a safety question before it is a styling one. Save all of it for the house you are moving to.

For buyers: what fall staging can hide

Lamps and rugs are honest staging, but they flatter. If you are touring in Montclair, Hoboken or Woodbridge after dark, come back once in daylight to see the floors and the natural light. Ask how the heating system has been serviced, how the gutters handle the leaves, and whether the windows fog in cold weather; the condensation guide explains what that signals. Put all of it on the inspection list.

Questions about fall styling, showings and selling a NJ home in October

Is October a bad time to list a home in New Jersey?

No. In my experience fall inventory thins out and the buyers still looking in October tend to be serious, often with a reason to close before year end. Your house competes with fewer listings, which means preparation shows more. The timing guide below covers how to weigh it for your property.

How much fall decor is too much for a listing?

One seasonal element per room, and nothing that blocks a walkway, a handrail or a window. A pumpkin on every step and a garland on every banister reads as clutter in photos and as a trip hazard in person. Think warm textiles and light first, decor last.

Should all the lights be on for a fall showing?

Yes, every lamp and fixture, with bulbs of the same warm color temperature in each room. Put the main lamps on timers so the house is lit before buyers pull up after work. Dark rooms in a fall showing read as small and cold.

Do I need new listing photos for fall?

If your exterior photos show full summer trees and the house now looks different, a refreshed front photo helps buyers recognize it when they pull up. Shoot it before the leaves are gone, and add a twilight photo if the house has good exterior lighting.

What should a buyer check at a fall showing?

See the house once in daylight, because lamps can flatter worn floors and dim rooms. Ask about the heating system, the gutters and how the yard drains when the leaves come down. Check the windows for condensation, and bring anything you notice to the inspection.

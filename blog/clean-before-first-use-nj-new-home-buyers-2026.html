Move-In Cleaning: What to Check Before First Use

A practical NJ move-in cleaning plan for food storage, bathrooms, laundry and surfaces, with model-specific checks and a clear handoff from inspection to cleaning.

Make a handoff list before unpacking

Walk through the home and separate cleaning from defects. Photograph leaks, unusual staining, damaged seals and appliances that will not operate before treating them as dirt. Keep the inspection report, appliance model numbers and seller-provided manuals together. This checklist starts after possession or with permission; it does not authorize work in a seller’s home before closing.

Start where food and daily routines will go

  1. Refrigerator and pantry: Remove abandoned food and loose debris. Check the refrigerator’s manual for shelf removal and cleaning; allow glass parts to reach a safe temperature before handling as directed.
  2. Food-preparation surfaces: Identify the counter material and use a compatible product before setting down dishes or preparing meals.
  3. Cabinets and drawers: Clean and dry them before lining or filling them. Do not cover an unresolved leak or pest problem.
  4. Bathroom fixtures: Clean visible soil with a surface-compatible product. Keep toilet-cleaning supplies separate from kitchen supplies.

EPA advises following product labels, checking compatible surfaces and never mixing cleaners. Cleaning and disinfecting are separate operations; a disinfectant needs its labeled contact time. EPA: biological contaminants and cleaning.

Treat appliances as model-specific equipment

For a washer, find the correct manual and cleaning cycle before adding any chemical. Maytag cautions that vinegar can affect washer components and that bleach is not suitable for every model. Do not copy a recipe from a different machine. Maytag: washer cleaning and model compatibility.

Inspect accessible dishwasher filters, removable refrigerator parts and dryer lint filters using their manuals. Stop if access requires unfamiliar disassembly, electrical work or moving a heavy appliance. Record service needs separately so cleaning does not become an improvised repair.

Unpack in a usable sequence

Choose one food-preparation area, one bathroom and the sleeping space first. Bring labeled cleaners, clean cloths, trash bags and the manuals you need into that zone. Finish drying before placing belongings back. Then work through lower-priority storage areas.

At the end, keep a short log: item, model, manual location, what was cleaned and any unresolved concern. For purchase budgeting and reserves, use the NJ buyer-cost guide. No universal checklist can certify a home as sanitary or replace an inspection.

Practical questions

Does broom-clean mean every appliance is ready for use?

Do not assume that it does. Check the contract and actual condition, then follow each appliance’s care instructions before use.

Should every surface be disinfected?

Choose the task and product for the situation. Remove visible soil, check material compatibility and follow any disinfectant’s labeled directions rather than applying it indiscriminately.

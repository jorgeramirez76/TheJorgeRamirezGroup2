2026 Affordability Reality Check

The Cheapest Places to Live in NJ Near NYC in 2026 — What $500K Actually Buys

By Jorge Ramirez · Licensed NJ Real Estate Agent #1754604 · Keller Williams Premier Properties

Last updated: 2026-07-08

Is Anything Near NYC Still Under $500K?

Direct answer

Barely, and not where you'd expect. New Jersey's statewide median sale price is $563,000 as of May 2026. I checked six towns in the Union County commuter corridor and every single one is at or above $525,000. Rahway is the closest thing left to an entry point. If someone tells you they can get you a house near NYC transit for $400K right now, ask them when they last actually checked.

I get some version of this question a lot: "Where can we still buy something reasonable near the train?" A couple years ago I'd have real towns to point to under $500K. I went and pulled current numbers instead of guessing, and the honest answer surprised me too — that price point has mostly disappeared from the towns people usually ask about.

I live in Roselle Park. I've been full-time in this market since 2017. So this isn't a national trends piece — it's what's actually selling, right now, in the towns I drive through every week.

The Six Towns, Ranked by Actual 2026 Median Price

These are current Redfin median sale prices, most from May 2026 unless noted. I'm not rounding these to make a nicer list — this is what they actually are.

Linden's median has moved between $549K and $575K across recent months depending on the data pull, so I'm showing it as an approximate midpoint rather than false precision. Garwood's year-over-year figure wasn't reliably available at time of writing.

Rahway and Linden sit on NJ Transit's Northeast Corridor line, with direct service toward Newark and New York Penn Station. Roselle, Roselle Park, and Garwood are Raritan Valley Line towns, which historically has meant a change of trains at Newark for a one-seat ride into the city on most departures. Kenilworth doesn't have its own station — it's a short drive to the nearest Raritan Valley Line stop.

What Happened to Roselle Park?

I'll call out the number that jumps off that table: Roselle Park up 67.7% year-over-year. I live there, so I've watched this happen in real time, not from a spreadsheet. A jump that size in one year, in a town this small, is almost never "every house got worth 68% more." It's much more often a shift in which homes happened to sell that period — more renovated houses, more larger lots, fewer of the smaller starter homes that used to anchor the median. The town didn't change that fast. The mix of what sold did.

That's the actual lesson here, and it applies to all six towns on this list: a median is a snapshot of what sold, not a price tag on your specific house or the one you're about to bid on. If you're using a number like this to price a listing or size up an offer, it's a starting point for a conversation, not the final word.

“Cheapest in NJ” and “Cheapest Near NYC” Are Two Different Lists

If you search for the cheapest places to live in New Jersey, most lists you will find are answering a question you probably did not ask. The genuinely cheapest municipalities in the state sit in Cumberland, Salem and Atlantic counties — the far south and the southwest. Housing there is a fraction of what it costs up here, and for someone who works locally, retires there, or works fully remote, those lists are perfectly good advice.

They are useless if you need Manhattan. From most of South Jersey you are looking at two hours or more each way, often with a transfer, and no realistic daily commute. So the cheap price is not a discount — it is a different life.

Everything ranked on this page is the other list: the cheapest places to live in New Jersey while keeping a workable NYC commute. That constraint is what sets the floor. Once you require a one-seat or one-transfer ride to the city, the state’s cheapest towns drop out of consideration entirely, and the realistic entry point moves up by several hundred thousand dollars.

Worth naming the trade honestly: every dollar you save on price out here you tend to pay back in commute time, property tax rate, or condition. The towns below are the ones where that exchange is still reasonable rather than punishing.

If You Are Buying Your First Place in Your Twenties or Thirties

The cheapest-town question gets asked most often by younger buyers making the jump from renting in Jersey City, Hoboken or the city itself. Two things matter more for that buyer than the median price:

  • The monthly number, not the sticker. A $475,000 house in a high-tax town can cost more per month than a $525,000 house in a lower-rate one. New Jersey tax rates cannot be compared directly between towns — assessment ratios vary from 13% to 70%, which makes published rates meaningless on their own.
  • Condition, honestly assessed. The cheapest listing in a good town is usually cheapest for a reason. Sometimes that reason is cosmetic and worth taking on. Sometimes it is a roof, a heating system and a 1970s electrical panel, and the discount is not a discount.

My Honest Take

"Affordable NJ town near NYC" used to mean something specific. In 2026, in this corridor, it means $525K to $575K, not $350K to $400K. If your budget is fixed below that, you're either looking further out — Middlesex or further into Union's western towns — or you're looking at a smaller property type: a condo, a two-family, a fixer-upper that needs real work.

None of that is bad news, exactly. It's just the real number, and I'd rather tell you that up front than let you spend three weekends touring houses at a price point that doesn't exist here anymore.

If Your Budget Is Under $575K

  • Widen the search past Union County. Parts of Middlesex County — New Brunswick, Edison's more modest pockets — still trade meaningfully below these numbers, at the cost of a longer or transfer-required commute.
  • Look at property type, not just town. A two-family in Rahway or Linden can bring real numbers down per-unit and add rental income to help qualify.
  • Get pre-approved before you fall for a listing. At these prices, well-priced homes in Rahway and Roselle move fast — competitive-market listing patterns, not sit-and-wait ones.
  • Ask about first-time buyer programs. NJHMFA and local down-payment assistance programs can change what's realistic more than another six months of saving will.

Common Questions

Are there any NJ towns near NYC still under $500,000 in 2026?

Almost none, within a reasonable commute of Manhattan. New Jersey's statewide median is $563,000 as of May 2026, and every town I checked in the Union County commuter corridor is at or above $525,000. Rahway is the closest thing left to an entry point in that specific area.

What is the cheapest town to buy a house in Union County NJ?

Among the towns with reliable 2026 data, Rahway has the lowest median sale price at around $525,000, followed by Roselle at roughly $555,000. Prices shift month to month, so it's worth checking current listings rather than relying on a single number.

Why did Roselle Park home prices increase so much in 2026?

Roselle Park's median sale price jumped roughly 68% year-over-year as of March 2026, per Redfin. A jump that large in a small town is usually driven by a shift in which homes sold that period, not every house suddenly being worth 68% more — a sign to look at actual comparable sales rather than the headline median.

Is it still worth buying in Union County NJ in 2026?

For buyers priced out of Essex and Morris County towns, Union County corridor towns like Rahway, Roselle, and Linden remain the more accessible entry point into NJ Transit commuter territory, even though "affordable" now means $500K-$575K rather than under $500K.

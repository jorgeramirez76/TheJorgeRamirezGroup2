14 Things to Clear Out of Your Garage Before NJ Buyers See It

Your garage swings from freezing to sweltering and back again every year. It is quietly ruining half of what you keep out there. Here are the 14 items to move or toss before listing, and what buyers see when the door goes up.

The garage is the last room you clean and the first one buyers judge

Almost every buyer I show a home to in Union, Morris, Essex, Hudson, and Middlesex counties opens the garage door, because it answers the question they have been asking since the front hall: where does all the stuff go?

In older towns like Westfield, Cranford, Maplewood, and Glen Ridge, closets are small and basements are damp, so the garage fills up with everything the house cannot hold. The problem is that a NJ garage is one of the worst places to store most of it.

An uninsulated garage drops below freezing in January and runs hotter than the driveway in August. Humidity and road salt come in on the tires. Those swings destroy paint, warp wood, corrode electronics, and let mildew into anything cardboard or fabric. The garage is hurting your listing and ruining your belongings at the same time.

Jorge's rule on the garage: If it cannot survive a winter on your front porch, it should not be living in your garage. That one test sorts most of what is out there, and it gets you to the clear floor buyers want to see.

14 things to move or toss before your NJ listing goes live

  1. Leftover paint Latex paint spoils once it freezes, so the cans you saved for touch-ups are likely useless after a NJ winter. Take them to your town's hazardous waste day and keep one labeled quart per room in a hall closet.
    Move inside or dispose
  2. Propane tanks and gas cans A spare propane tank belongs outside, not next to a water heater. Gas cans are the same story. Inspectors note them, and buyers who see them start thinking about safety instead of square footage.
    Inspection item
  3. Photos, documents, and keepsakes in cardboard Cardboard wicks moisture, and the contents get musty, stained, and eventually moldy. Anything sentimental or important moves into a closet inside or a climate-controlled unit for the sale.
    Move inside
  4. Electronics and old TVs Temperature swings and condensation corrode circuit boards. The old TV out there is not coming back to life. NJ towns hold electronics recycling days. Get them gone.
    Recycle
  5. Canned and dry food Cans freeze and burst, and dry goods invite mice. If a buyer spots droppings during a showing, the conversation shifts to pests and stays there.
    Move to the pantry
  6. The second refrigerator or freezer An old fridge in a hot garage costs money to run and takes up a wall buyers want to see. If you need it through the sale, clean it and pull it away from the door into the house.
    Sell, donate, or tidy
  7. Wood furniture and instruments Humidity swells wood and winter air shrinks it. That is how tables crack and guitars warp. Move them inside or let them go.
    Move or sell
  8. Bedding, clothing, and fabric Anything soft absorbs moisture and smell. Donate what you do not need and store the rest in sealed bins inside.
    Donate
  9. Books and paper Paper takes on moisture and warps. Boxes of old textbooks and magazines are a donate or recycle pile.
    Donate or recycle
  10. Sports gear you no longer use Outgrown bikes, the treadmill that became a coat rack. Buyers see a wall of gear and think "no room for ours." Keep what you use this season.
    Sell or donate
  11. Half-used cleaners and chemicals Pesticides, pool chemicals, and old cleaners degrade in heat, leak in cold, and make the garage smell like a hardware aisle. Keep what you use and dispose of the rest properly.
    Dispose properly
  12. Expired or duplicate tools Three hammers, two broken drills. Keep one working set on a pegboard. A tidy tool wall helps a listing because it shows the garage works as a workshop.
    Edit and hang
  13. Old tires and car parts Rubber breaks down in heat, and the pile reads as a project the buyer is inheriting. Most NJ tire shops take old tires for a small fee.
    Dispose
  14. Anything blocking the door into the house The door from the garage into the home is fire-rated in most NJ houses, and inspectors test it. Keep a clear three-foot path to it.
    Clear the path

What the garage says to buyers after you clear it

Once the fourteen categories above are handled, what is left usually fits on wall shelving and a pegboard. I tell sellers in Summit, Chatham, Millburn, and Scotch Plains that a garage does not need to be empty. It needs to be legible. Buyers should see the floor, see the walls, and picture their car pulling in.

Clearing the garage is also the first real step of your move. Everything you sort now is something you do not pay a mover to carry.

Sort into four piles: toss or recycle, donate, take to the new house, and keep for showings. Take the hazardous items to your town's collection day. Then sweep the floor, put in a working bulb, and open the door for the photographer.

Frequently asked questions

Why does it matter what I store in the garage when I sell my NJ home?

Two reasons. First, a packed garage tells buyers the house is short on storage, which is a common objection in older homes in towns like Westfield, Cranford, and Maplewood. Second, some of what people store in a garage, like propane, gas cans, and half-used chemicals, raises safety questions during the inspection. A clear garage removes both problems at once.

Does a NJ garage really get hot and cold enough to damage things?

Yes. An uninsulated NJ garage can drop below freezing in January and climb well past outdoor temperatures on an August afternoon. Add humidity in summer and salt tracked in from the driveway in winter, and you have conditions that ruin paint, warp wood, corrode electronics, and let mildew take hold in cardboard and fabric.

Where should I put the things I remove from the garage before listing?

Sort into four groups: toss or recycle, donate, take to the new house, and keep for showings. Chemicals and old paint go to your town's hazardous waste drop-off, not the trash. Keepsakes and documents move inside to a closet or a climate-controlled storage unit. What stays in the garage should fit on wall shelving with the floor clear.

Should the garage be completely empty for showings and photos?

Not empty, but clear. Buyers want to see the floor, the walls, and enough open space to picture their own car and bikes. A few organized items on wall shelves actually help, because they show the garage functions as storage. Piles on the floor, open boxes, and anything blocking the door to the house hurt.

How much does clearing out the garage help a NJ home sale?

In my experience it is one of the highest-return jobs a seller can do for the cost of a weekend and a trip to the dump. Buyers in Summit, Chatham, and Montclair open the garage door on almost every showing. When they see a clean floor and open space, they add it to the plus column. When they see a wall of boxes, they subtract.

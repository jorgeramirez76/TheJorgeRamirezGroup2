Cleaning and Storing Outdoor Furniture Before Fall Listing Photos in NJ

The porch, deck and patio end up in the photos and on the showing route. Clean what stays, store what does not, and decide in writing what goes with the house.

The outdoor furniture is in the pictures

A September or October listing in Morristown, Chatham or Scotch Plains still gets exterior photographs of the deck, patio and porch, and buyers walk that route on nearly every showing. A summer of pollen, grill smoke and rain shows on furniture more than sellers notice from the kitchen window. Green film on a white resin chair, rust bleeding from an iron table, a faded umbrella and a pile of mismatched cushions all read as neglect in a photo, even when the house itself is well kept.

The fix is an afternoon of cleaning and a few decisions. Treat it as part of the same walk you take for fall curb appeal, not a separate project.

Check the care label and clean by material

Outdoor furniture is not one material, and the wrong cleaner does more damage than the dirt. Look for the manufacturer’s care tag or instructions first, then match the method to the piece.

  • Resin and plastic: mild dish soap, warm water and a soft brush. Rinse well and let the piece dry in the sun.
  • Aluminum: soap and water, then a soft cloth. Skip abrasive pads, which scratch the powder coat.
  • Wrought iron and steel: wash, dry completely, and touch up chipped paint with a rust-inhibiting product so orange streaks do not return before the photographer arrives.
  • Teak and other wood: a wood-safe cleaner and a brush along the grain. Do not pressure wash softwood unless the instructions allow it; high pressure lifts the fibers and leaves a fuzzy surface.
  • Wicker: vacuum with a brush attachment, then wipe. Natural wicker should not be soaked.
  • Cushions and umbrella fabric: check the care code on the tag. Many covers unzip and wash; others take a mild soap solution, a rinse and full drying before they go back on.

If you are choosing a new cleaner, the EPA Safer Choice label identifies products reviewed for safer ingredients. Test any product on an underside first.

Decide what stays in the frame

A photograph needs one clear idea. On a deck, that is a single clean seating group: a table with matching chairs, or a pair of chairs with a small table between them. Remove the extra folding chairs, the toys, the hose reel, the grill cover that has seen better days and the planters with dead annuals. Leave the view of the yard open. Keep steps, the door to the house and any walkway clear, both for the camera and for the people who will use them during showings.

Faded or broken pieces should come off the deck entirely rather than being moved to the edge of the frame. A buyer who sees the porch in Westfield and then a broken chaise behind the garage in Cranford draws the same conclusion in both towns.

Store the rest before the first frost

Northern New Jersey gets its first frost in the weeks after most fall listings go live. Furniture you are not using should be fully dry before it is stacked or covered, because damp cushions and covered wet metal are where mildew and rust start. Bring cushions indoors and off the floor. Covers help for frames left outside, but tie them so they do not flap in a November wind on the day of a showing.

Use the garage or a dry basement, and keep the pile away from the furnace, water heater and the door into the house. The garage storage and disposal guide covers what belongs in that space and what does not.

Say what goes with the house

Buyers ask whether the patio set, the grill, the umbrella and the planters stay. Movable outdoor furniture is generally personal property unless the contract lists it, and the answer changes the negotiation more often than sellers expect. Decide before the first showing, tell your agent, and put the inclusions and exclusions in writing with your attorney. Do not stage a set you plan to take with you as though it were part of the deck. The NJ seller disclosure guide covers the condition questions that sit alongside those inclusion decisions.

Cleaning does not fix a soft deck board or a railing that moves. If you find one while you are out there, have it evaluated by a qualified contractor and discuss disclosure with your agent and attorney rather than arranging furniture around it.

Questions about this checklist

Should I leave outdoor furniture out for fall showings?

Leave one clean, complete seating arrangement that shows how the space is used, and store anything faded, broken or mismatched. Bring cushions in when rain or frost is forecast.

Can I pressure wash patio furniture?

Only when the manufacturer’s care instructions allow it. High pressure can gouge softwood, strip finishes and split resin wicker, so start with mild soap, water and a soft brush.

Does patio furniture stay with the house in NJ?

Movable furniture, grills, umbrellas and planters are generally personal property unless the contract lists them. Decide before listing and confirm the wording with your agent and attorney.

What if the deck or railing itself has a problem?

Cleaning does not fix a loose railing or a soft board. Have it evaluated by a qualified contractor and discuss disclosure with your agent and attorney before showings.

Where should I store cushions during a listing?

Somewhere dry, indoors and off the floor, such as a closet shelf or labeled bins in a dry basement. Avoid stacking them against the furnace, water heater or garage door.

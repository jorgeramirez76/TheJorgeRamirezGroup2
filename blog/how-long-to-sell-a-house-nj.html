“How long will it take?” is the second question every NJ seller asks, right after “what is it worth?” The honest answer is that a statewide average is close to useless for it. A well-priced house in Westfield and a well-priced house in Harding are not running the same race, and no state-level figure will tell you which one you own.

Which NJ towns sell the fastest?

By the figures above, the shortest days on market belong to Caldwell, Montclair, and Westfield at 9 days, then Maplewood and Summit at 10. Seven more towns sit at 11 days: Madison, Chatham Borough, Cranford, Fanwood, Metuchen, Millburn, and New Providence.

The pattern is not subtle. Almost every town in that group is a train town with a deep, specific buyer pool — the same towns covered in the Midtown Direct guide and in where NJ homes sell fastest. Deep buyer pools compress days on market because there is always another buyer behind the one who passed.

Which NJ towns take the longest?

At the other end, Harding shows 35 days, and Mendham Township and Peapack-Gladstone both show 30. These are larger-lot, higher-priced towns, and that is the whole explanation: fewer buyers can write the check, so the pool is thinner and the search takes longer. A longer figure here is a function of the price band and lot size, not a signal that something is wrong with the market.

The most common single figure across all 129 towns is 22 days, which appears on 26 town pages. If you want a rough mental default for New Jersey before you look up your own town, that is the number to hold.

What does days on market actually measure?

Days on market is the stretch between the day a listing goes live and the day it goes under contract. It is not the time until you get paid. After the contract comes New Jersey's three-business-day attorney review, then inspections, appraisal, and mortgage underwriting before you reach the closing table. The NJ home selling timeline walks through every phase and attaches a duration to each one.

So read your town's number as the first leg only. It answers “how long until I have a buyer,” and the rest of the timeline stacks on top of it.

What moves your timeline more than your town does?

Your town sets the baseline. These four things decide whether you beat it or trail it:

  • Price on day one. The single largest lever, and the only one you cannot fix later without visible damage. A price cut is public and buyers read it as weakness.
  • Condition and presentation. Two identical houses on the same street can sit weeks apart on days on market purely on photography, decluttering, and paint.
  • The week you list. Hitting the market against a wave of similar inventory means competing on price. The best time to sell in NJ covers the seasonal side of this.
  • Whether your buyer pool is actually in town. A four-bedroom colonial in a train town has a deeper pool than a five-acre estate, and the days-on-market spread between them is that difference showing up in the data.

Frequently Asked Questions

How long does it take to sell a house in NJ?

There is no single statewide answer, because the pace varies enormously by town. Of the 129 New Jersey towns on this site that publish a days-on-market figure, Caldwell, Montclair, and Westfield show the shortest at 9 days, while Harding shows the longest at 35 days. Those figures measure how long a listing takes to go under contract, not how long until you get your money. Attorney review and the contract-to-close period are added on top of them.

What is the average time to sell a house in NJ?

Days on market is the number most sellers mean when they ask this, and on this site's town pages it clusters in the teens and low twenties: the most common single figure across the 129 towns is 22 days, which appears on 26 town pages. Union and Essex commuter towns tend to sit below that and larger-lot Morris and Somerset towns tend to sit above it. The full listing-to-closing timeline, including attorney review and underwriting, is covered on the NJ home selling timeline page.

Where do these days-on-market numbers come from?

Each figure is copied directly from the market snapshot published on that town's own page on this site. Nothing here is recalculated, averaged, or adjusted. The town pages present these as 2026 figures and describe the days-on-market number as the average for residential properties and the median price as being based on recent sales. Nine of the 138 town pages do not publish either figure and are therefore not in the table.

Which NJ towns sell the fastest?

By the days-on-market figures published on their town pages, the fastest are Caldwell, Montclair, and Westfield at 9 days, followed by Maplewood and Summit at 10 days. Madison, Chatham Borough, Cranford, Fanwood, Metuchen, Millburn, and New Providence all show 11 days. These are commuter towns with deep buyer pools, which is the pattern behind the number.

Does a faster town mean I will sell faster?

Not on its own. A town-level days-on-market figure describes the typical listing there, not yours. Price, condition, photography, and the week you hit the MLS move your own timeline more than the town average does. The number is best used as a baseline: if your house is priced and presented correctly you should land near your town's figure, and if you are drifting well past it the cause is usually price.

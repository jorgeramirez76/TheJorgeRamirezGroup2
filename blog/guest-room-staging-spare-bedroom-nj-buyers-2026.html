Staging the Guest Room: What NJ Buyers Want to See in a Spare Bedroom

House Outlook’s newsletter this week included a Real Simple list of the essential items for a welcoming guest room. Read it as a seller and it is a staging checklist. The spare bedroom in most New Jersey listings is the room that has quietly become storage, and buyers count bedrooms by what they can see, not by what the listing says.

The tip House Outlook sent this week

Among this week’s House Outlook stories was a Real Simple piece on the essentials of a welcoming guest room: a comfortable bed with clean linens, a nightstand and a lamp you can reach from the pillow, somewhere to put a suitcase, empty hangers in the closet, a clear surface and a few basics within reach. The idea is that being a guest in someone else’s house teaches you what your own guest room is missing.

A buyer at a showing is a guest in your house for twenty minutes. The same list that makes a visitor comfortable is what makes a spare bedroom read as a bedroom in the photos and on the walkthrough.

Why the spare room decides more than you think

In the colonials and split-levels of Westfield, Cranford and Scotch Plains the fourth bedroom is usually the small one over the garage or at the end of the hall, and after a few years it holds the treadmill, the holiday bins and the ironing board. In Hoboken and Jersey City the second bedroom is the whole reason a buyer is paying for a two-bedroom instead of a one-bedroom. In Edison and Woodbridge a multi-generational buyer is looking for a room a parent can actually sleep in.

Buyers count bedrooms with their eyes. A room full of boxes does not read as a bedroom, it reads as a house that is short on storage. A made bed, a lamp and a cleared closet turn the same square footage back into the fourth bedroom the listing promised.

The guest room list, turned into a staging list

  1. A real bed, plainly made. Not an air mattress and not a daybed buried in pillows. A simple frame with smooth, neutral bedding. If the room is small, a full bed with space around it reads larger than a queen jammed wall to wall.
  2. One nightstand and one lamp per reachable side. It tells the buyer the room is used for sleeping, and the lamp gives the photographer a warm light to turn on.
  3. Luggage space. A bench at the foot of the bed or just clear floor. The point is that the room has room.
  4. A closet with empty hangers. Take it down to a third full. Buyers open closet doors in every room, and a guest room closet packed to the ceiling is the storage problem showing itself.
  5. One clear surface. A dresser top or a small desk with nothing on it. That is where a remote-work buyer pictures the laptop.
  6. Remove the things that say storage. The exercise equipment, the bins, the file boxes, the ironing board and the drying rack. If they have nowhere to go, rent a small unit for the listing period.

Jorge’s tip: Leave the guest room staged for the whole listing period, not just the photos. Buyers who liked the photos come back to that room on the second visit, and a bed that has turned back into a laundry pile undoes the work. Count the room honestly

Staging a spare room as a bedroom is fair when it is one. Before the listing calls a room a bedroom, make sure it has what a buyer and an appraiser expect to find: a door that closes, a window, heat, and in most New Jersey homes a closet. A finished basement room or an attic space has its own egress and code questions, and the answer varies by town. Ask your agent before counting it, and if a pre-listing inspection raises a question about a bedroom, resolve it before the photos rather than after the buyer’s inspector does.

For buyers: the fourth bedroom test

If you are touring in Summit, Millburn, Montclair or Union, give the smallest bedroom the most attention. Measure it, because a staged full bed can hide a room that will not fit a queen and a dresser. Open the closet, close the door, find the heat register and count the outlets. Ask what was in the room before it was staged. If the room is still storage when you visit, do not assume it is a bedroom until you have checked the same list, and bring the answer to the inspection and the offer.

Questions about guest rooms, bedroom counts and selling a NJ home

Should I stage the spare bedroom or leave it empty?

Stage it. An empty room photographs smaller than a furnished one, and a room full of boxes does not read as a bedroom at all. A plainly made bed, one nightstand and lamp, a cleared closet and open floor space are enough. Keep it that way for the whole listing period, not just for the photos.

What size bed should go in a small guest room for showings?

A full bed with walking space on at least two sides usually reads larger than a queen that touches the walls. Buyers judge a room by the space around the furniture, not by the size of the mattress. If the room will not fit a full bed with room to walk, be honest in how the listing describes it.

Can I call a room a bedroom if it has no closet?

In most New Jersey homes buyers and appraisers expect a bedroom to have a door, a window, heat and a closet, and a basement or attic room raises its own egress and code questions that vary by town. Ask your agent before counting a room as a bedroom, and resolve any question before the photos rather than after the buyer’s inspection.

Where do I put the exercise equipment and storage bins while the house is listed?

Out of the bedroom. If the garage, basement or a relative’s house cannot take them, a small storage unit for the listing period is cheap compared with losing a bedroom in the buyer’s count. The guest room should hold a bed, a nightstand, a lamp and open floor, and nothing that says storage.

What should a buyer check in the smallest bedroom at a showing?

Measure it, open the closet, close the door, find the heat register and count the outlets. Ask what the room held before it was staged. A full bed can hide a room that will not fit a queen and a dresser, so check the dimensions against the furniture you own, and bring any doubt about whether it is a real bedroom to the inspection and the offer.

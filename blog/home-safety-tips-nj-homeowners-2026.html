10 Home Safety Tips Every NJ Homeowner Should Act On — Before You Sell or Buy

Published  ·  August 2026

Short answer

Most NJ homeowners have at least two or three safety issues they don't know about — an expired CO detector, a clogged dryer vent, an unknown buried oil tank, or missing GFCI outlets. These aren't just inspection items. They're fire hazards, carbon monoxide risks, and environmental liabilities that cost lives before they cost deals. Here's what to check, fix, and know before you buy or sell.

The safety issues NJ inspectors flag most often were all preventable. Here's the list — with what each one means for owners, buyers, and sellers.

Why Home Safety Should Come Before Home Staging

I've worked full time in New Jersey real estate since 2017. Every year I see the same thing: sellers spend weeks on staging, paint colors, and landscaping — and then get caught at inspection with a problem that's been sitting in the house for a decade. A Federal Pacific panel. An underground oil tank they didn't know existed. A CO detector that expired in 2019. A dryer vent packed with lint that should have been cleaned four years ago.

These aren't aesthetic issues. They're safety issues that become negotiating leverage for buyers, delay closings, and occasionally kill deals entirely. More importantly — before they're ever a real estate problem, they're a risk to the people living in the house.

Here are the ten home safety areas every NJ homeowner should address — whether you're planning to sell in six months or you're staying put for another decade.

1. Carbon Monoxide Detectors: They Expire and Most Homeowners Don't Know It

CO detectors have a useful life of 5-7 years. The sensor degrades over time and stops detecting accurately — often without any alarm or visible warning. In New Jersey, CO detectors are legally required on every level of the home, including the basement, and within 10 feet of each sleeping area. Before selling, NJ sellers must certify that smoke and CO detectors are operational.

Pull your CO detector off the wall. There's a manufacture date on the back. If it's more than 7 years old, it's effectively decorative. Replace it now. A new combination smoke/CO detector runs $30-$60. The life it saves might be yours before it ever becomes a line item on an inspection report.

2. Smoke Detectors: Test Them, Replace Them

Smoke alarms should be tested monthly and replaced every 10 years. NJ building code requires them in every sleeping room, outside each sleeping area, and on every level of the home. Interconnected alarms — where triggering one triggers all of them — are required in new construction and strongly recommended for older NJ homes.

A failing smoke alarm at inspection flags immediately. Buyers ask to have them replaced before closing. It's a $25-$40 item per unit — do it before the inspection rather than after.

3. GFCI Outlets: The Most Common Inspection Flag in Pre-1980 NJ Homes

GFCI (Ground Fault Circuit Interrupter) outlets cut power instantly when they detect an electrical fault — preventing electrocution near water sources. They're required in kitchens, bathrooms, garages, outdoors, and within 6 feet of any water source. In NJ homes built before the mid-1970s, they often don't exist in any of these locations.

GFCI outlets cost $10-$20 each and take 20 minutes to install. A handyman can do an entire house in an afternoon. Missing GFCIs appear on virtually every inspection report for pre-1980 NJ homes and are one of the easiest things to fix before listing. Test the ones you have — press the test button and verify the outlet goes dead. If the reset button doesn't work, the outlet needs replacement.

4. Your Electrical Panel: Know What You Have

If your NJ home was built between 1950 and 1990, there's a reasonable chance it has a Federal Pacific Electric (FPE) Stab-Lok panel or a Zinsco/Sylvania panel. Both have documented histories of breaker failure and fire risk. They are flagged on virtually every home inspection where they appear and are a significant buyer concern in NJ's older housing stock — particularly in Union, Essex, and Morris county towns like Springfield, Maplewood, Florham Park, and Livingston.

FPE panel replacement runs $3,000-$6,000 in New Jersey. Some sellers replace it before listing to remove the issue entirely. Others price it in and disclose. What you don't want is to discover it at inspection when a buyer is already in contract and emotional leverage is at its worst. Know what panel you have. Check the nameplate inside the door.

5. The Dryer Vent Nobody Cleans

Dryer fires cause approximately 2,900 residential fires per year in the US, and the leading cause is failure to clean the vent. Lint buildup is highly combustible. In NJ townhouses and condos in communities like Woodbridge, Edison, Piscataway, and Parsippany, dryer vent runs can be long and include multiple bends — exactly the configuration where lint accumulates fastest.

Clean your dryer vent annually. A professional cleaning costs $80-$150 and takes about an hour. Signs you're overdue: dryer takes more than one cycle to dry a load, dryer feels hot to the touch during operation, or there's a burning smell. Also check the exterior vent cap — many have screens that clog completely and stop airflow. Inspectors check this and flag blocked vent runs every time.

6. Water Heater Temperature: Too Hot Is a Hazard, Too Cold Is Too

Water heater temperature should be set to 120°F. Above that, scalding becomes a risk — particularly for young children and elderly occupants. Below 120°F, Legionella bacteria can survive and multiply in the tank. Most water heaters have a dial on the unit; many are factory-set at 140°F and never adjusted.

Water heaters should also have a Temperature and Pressure Relief (TPR) valve tested annually and replaced if it's never been tested or shows corrosion. Inspectors test the TPR valve as part of every inspection. A failed valve is an inspection flag that adds cost to negotiations.

7. Underground Oil Tanks: The Hidden Environmental Liability

This is the one that blindsides NJ sellers more than any other. many NJ homes — particularly pre-1970 construction in Union, Morris, and Essex counties — were originally heated with oil. Many converted to gas decades ago. The underground storage tank (UST) they left behind may still be in the yard, rusting, potentially leaking.

An unknown leaking UST is an environmental cleanup that can run $10,000 to $100,000 or more depending on soil contamination. Buyers routinely request a tank scan as part of due diligence. If a scan finds an undisclosed UST with contamination, deals fall apart or sellers face massive remediation demands. Before you list any pre-1970 NJ home, order a professional tank scan. Cost: $200-$400. The alternative can be catastrophic.

8. Radon: It's More Common in NJ Than Most People Realize

New Jersey has significant radon exposure in many counties — particularly Morris, Somerset, and parts of Union and Essex. Radon is a colorless, odorless gas that enters homes through foundation cracks and is the second leading cause of lung cancer in the US. Most buyers require radon testing as part of their inspection contingency.

Testing is easy and inexpensive — a short-term test kit runs $15-$30 or you can hire a certified radon testing company for $100-$150. If levels exceed 4 pCi/L (the EPA action level), radon mitigation typically costs $800-$2,500 in NJ. Sellers who test proactively and remediate before listing eliminate a buyer contingency and control the cost. Sellers who don't often pay more after the buyer's inspector runs the test.

9. Bathroom Exhaust Fans: Moisture, Mold, and Inspectors

An undersized or malfunctioning bathroom exhaust fan is one of the most reliable paths to a mold problem in a NJ home. In Union, Essex, and Morris County homes I've listed, mold in bathroom ceilings and walls from inadequate ventilation is among the top five inspection findings. It's preventable — but only if the fan is the right size, positioned correctly, and actually runs long enough after showers.

Run your bathroom exhaust fan for 20-30 minutes after every shower — not just during. If the fan sounds like a small aircraft or hasn't been replaced in more than 10-12 years, replace it before listing. A new unit runs $60-$100. The alternative is a mold finding on the inspection report that triggers remediation estimates and buyer anxiety.

10. Know Your Home's Fire Escape Plan — And Whether Buyers Can Find Their Way Out

NJ homes built before modern egress requirements often have bedrooms with windows that are too small to climb out of in an emergency, or basement bedrooms with inadequate exit paths. This matters both as a safety issue for current occupants and as a disclosure and code issue when selling. NJ requires egress windows in habitable basement spaces — a window 5.7 square feet or larger, with at least 20 inches of width and 24 inches of height.

If you've converted a basement room to a bedroom, or if your home has sleeping spaces in areas with inadequate egress, deal with it before listing. It will be flagged at inspection. More importantly — deal with it because it's a real safety issue for everyone living in the house right now.

Pre-listing safety checklist for NJ sellers: CO detectors on every level (check manufacture date). Smoke alarms in every bedroom and on every level. GFCI outlets in all wet areas. Know your panel brand. Dryer vent cleaned in the last 12 months. Water heater TPR valve checked. Tank scan if pre-1970 construction. Radon test completed. Bathroom exhaust fans functional. All egress windows unobstructed. Addressing these before your first showing removes leverage from buyer negotiations and protects your closing timeline.

Frequently Asked Questions

Are carbon monoxide detectors required in NJ homes?

Yes. New Jersey law requires CO detectors on every level of a home, including the basement, and within 10 feet of every sleeping area. CO detectors expire after 5-7 years — pull yours off the wall and check the manufacture date on the back. Before selling, NJ sellers must certify that smoke and CO detectors are functional. Missing or expired detectors appear on the inspection report and can delay closing.

What is a GFCI outlet and why does it matter for NJ inspections?

GFCI outlets cut power instantly when they detect a ground fault, preventing electrocution near water sources. They're required in kitchens, bathrooms, garages, and outdoors. In NJ homes built before the mid-1970s, they often don't exist in any of these locations. Missing GFCIs are one of the most common inspection flags I see — they cost $10-$20 each to replace and are a simple pre-listing fix.

How often should a dryer vent be cleaned in a NJ home?

At minimum once per year. Lint buildup is the leading cause of dryer fires. In NJ townhouses with long vent runs — common in Woodbridge, Edison, and Parsippany — buildup happens faster. Signs you're overdue: the dryer takes more than one cycle to dry a load, or the unit runs hot. Professional cleaning runs $80-$150. Inspectors check and flag blocked dryer vents consistently.

Do NJ homes have underground oil tanks?

Many pre-1970 NJ homes — particularly in Union, Morris, and Essex counties — have buried underground storage tanks from former oil heating systems. Even homes that converted to gas decades ago may have a tank still in the ground, rusting and potentially leaking. A leaking UST is an environmental cleanup that can run $10,000-$100,000+. Before listing any older NJ home, order a professional tank scan. It costs $200-$400 and eliminates the worst-case scenario at the worst possible moment.

What electrical panels do NJ inspectors flag most often?

Federal Pacific Electric (FPE) Stab-Lok panels and Zinsco/Sylvania panels — both common in NJ homes from the 1950s through 1980s — are flagged by virtually every inspector due to documented breaker failure and fire risk. Know what panel you have before listing. Replacement runs $3,000-$6,000 in NJ and is often negotiated at closing on homes where it appears. Knowing it in advance lets you control how and when that conversation happens.

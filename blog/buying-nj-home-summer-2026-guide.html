What the Summer 2026 NJ Market Actually Looks Like for Buyers

Let me tell you what I tell every buyer who sits across from me this summer: stop looking at national headlines and start looking at the specific town and price tier you care about.

The national "housing market" does not exist. The Summit market at $900K-$1.2M is a completely different animal from the Rahway market at $375K-$475K, which is different from the Montclair market at $850K-$1.1M. National inventory numbers, national median prices, national rate projections — these are context, not guidance. The guidance is local data.

Here is the local NJ data that matters right now: statewide inventory is up 8.5% year over year, with about 31,600 homes for sale in May 2026. The statewide median is $563,000, up 3.3%. Mortgage rates are running 6.4%-6.8% for well-qualified conventional borrowers. And 47.2% of NJ homes are still selling above list price.

What does that mean for you as a buyer? More choices than you had in 2024 or 2025. Real competition in desirable towns. Rates that are historically normal but meaningfully higher than the 3% anomaly of 2020-2021. A market where preparation and speed separate the buyers who close from the buyers who keep losing to someone else.

Step 1: Get Fully Pre-Approved Before You Do Anything Else

I mean fully underwritten pre-approved — not a pre-qualification from an app, not a "soft pull" letter from your bank's website. A full pre-approval where a human underwriter has reviewed your income documentation, tax returns, employment history, assets, and credit profile.

The difference matters in this market. When two offers land on a listing agent's desk at the same price, the listing agent calls the buyers' lenders. If one call gets a real underwriter who confirms the file is clean and the loan will close, and the other call gets a voicemail, the first offer gets the home. I have watched this happen. Your pre-approval letter is a sales document, not just a formality.

Who to use: A local NJ mortgage broker or a national lender with a strong NJ track record. Not a rate aggregator. Not someone you've never spoken to. Pick someone whose number you can call when a listing goes live at 9 AM on a Friday and you want to write an offer by noon. Speed matters in this market.

Step 2: Know Your Real Budget — All In

The mortgage payment is only part of it. NJ has the highest property taxes in the country by most rankings. In commuter towns, taxes are a real line item.

Here are the numbers I want every buyer to understand before they fall in love with a house:

  • Principal and interest: On a $600K loan at 6.5%, you are paying approximately $3,792/month
  • Property taxes: Vary enormously by town. Summit averages roughly $18,000-$22,000/year. Cranford runs $10,000-$14,000. Rahway $8,000-$11,000. That is $700-$1,800/month depending on where you buy
  • Homeowner's insurance: Budget $1,800-$3,000/year for most NJ homes
  • PMI (if under 20% down): Typically 0.5%-1.0% of the loan amount annually
  • HOA (if applicable): Condos and some townhomes carry $300-$800/month

Add those up before you set your target price, not after. I have watched buyers fall in love with a home in Summit at $1.1M and then be blindsided when the combined PITI runs $8,500/month. Know that number going in.

Step 3: Pick Your Town Based on Two Non-Negotiables, Not Six

I have sat with hundreds of buyers who have a list of criteria that looks like this: walkable downtown, top schools, Midtown Direct, under $800K, big backyard, good restaurants, low taxes, and commute under 45 minutes. That town does not exist in NJ. You need to narrow it to two hard requirements and let the rest be nice-to-haves.

Here is a framework that actually works:

If commute time is your top priority

Focus on the Midtown Direct line: Maplewood (~30 min), South Orange (~32 min), Millburn (~35 min), Summit (~44 min), Chatham (~52 min), Madison (~55 min). The faster the train, the higher the price. Pick your budget tier and then pick the fastest train town within it.

If school quality is your top priority

Short Hills/Millburn is the top-ranked district in the state. Chatham and Summit follow. Westfield, Cranford, and Scotch Plains-Fanwood are excellent for the price. Tell me your budget and I will tell you the best school district you can access.

If budget efficiency is your top priority

Cranford, Fanwood, Metuchen, and Bloomfield give you the best combination of school quality, train access, and home value per dollar in summer 2026. Rahway and Highland Park push the lower bound while still offering Northeast Corridor access.

Step 4: Write Competitive Offers — What Works in Summer 2026

The days of waiving all contingencies and offering $100K over ask are largely gone from the mid-market. But "competitive" still has a specific meaning in NJ in summer 2026, and it differs from what worked even two years ago.

What makes an offer strong right now:

  • Fully underwritten pre-approval letter — not a pre-qual. The listing agent will call your lender. Make sure your lender picks up.
  • Clean terms — appropriate inspection period (7-10 days), mortgage contingency matching your actual timeline, reasonable closing date aligned with the seller's situation
  • Escalation clause in competitive situations — pre-authorize your agent to escalate by $5K-$10K increments up to a defined ceiling if competing offers are received
  • Personal letter from the buyer — still works in NJ family homes, particularly when sellers have lived there for decades. Do not underestimate it.
  • Flexibility on closing date — if the seller needs 60 days, give them 60 days. If they need 30, be ready to close in 30. Flexibility on terms can win you a home at your price.

What you can typically include without losing in this market that you could not in 2022: an inspection contingency (include it), a mortgage contingency (include it), an attorney review period (standard in NJ — always included).

Step 5: Move When You Find the Right Home — Not When the Rate Is Perfect

I have watched buyers wait for rates to drop from 6.8% to 6.2% for eight months — and during those eight months, the homes they wanted appreciated 6-8% in their target towns. The rate savings they were chasing got eaten by the price increases they were avoiding.

The formula is simple. Buy when you find the right home at the right price for your family. Rates are a financing variable that you can refinance. The home itself — the specific house on the specific block in the specific school district — cannot be refinanced into existence later if it sells to someone else.

If you are financially ready and you find a home that checks your two non-negotiables at a price supported by the comps, buy it. That is the summer 2026 buyer strategy. Not "wait for rates" — prepare, target, and move with conviction when the right opportunity appears.

Frequently Asked Questions: Buying in NJ Summer 2026

Is summer 2026 a good time to buy a home in NJ?

For financially prepared buyers, yes. Inventory is up 8.5%, giving you more options than at any point since 2019. The frenzy of 2021-2022 is over — you can get inspections and negotiate rationally. Mortgage rates are in the mid-6% range, which is historically normal. If your finances are solid and your target is clear, waiting costs you in potential appreciation on the homes you want now.

What mortgage rate should I expect when buying in NJ right now?

A 30-year conventional loan for a well-qualified buyer with 780+ credit score and 20% down is running roughly 6.4%-6.6% in summer 2026. With a 740 credit score, add approximately 0.25-0.375%. FHA rates are slightly lower but carry mortgage insurance. Jumbo loans above $766,550 vary more by lender. Get quotes from at least two lenders — a 0.25% difference on a $700K loan is about $120/month.

How much do I need for a down payment on an NJ home?

Minimum is 3% for conventional first-time buyers, 3.5% for FHA. But in competitive NJ markets above $600K, a 20% down payment significantly strengthens your offer by eliminating PMI and reducing financing contingency risk. NJ's NJHMFA and various county programs offer first-time buyer assistance — worth exploring if you are under the income limits. Call me for a referral to a lender who knows these programs.

Which NJ towns have the best value for buyers in summer 2026?

Best values depend on your priorities. For commute access under $750K: Cranford, Fanwood, and Metuchen. For schools at a real-people price: Scotch Plains, Clark, and Kenilworth in Union County. For space and Morris County quality: Denville, Randolph, and Roxbury. For entry-level Northeast Corridor access: Rahway, Linden, and Highland Park. Tell me your two non-negotiables and I will narrow it to three towns that fit your budget.

Should I buy now or wait for mortgage rates to drop in NJ?

Rates are projected to stay in the low-to-mid 6% range through most of 2026. If they drop significantly later — say, to 5.5% — NJ home prices will rise as more buyers enter the market. The home you could buy today for $750K may be $800K at that rate. The savings from a rate drop can get eaten by appreciation in the time you wait. If your finances are ready, buy the home you want now and refinance if rates improve. The decision about when to buy should be driven by your readiness, not by rate forecasts.

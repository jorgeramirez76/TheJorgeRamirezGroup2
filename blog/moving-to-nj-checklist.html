Moving to New Jersey: The Deadlines You Actually Have to Hit

Updated July 30, 2026 · by Jorge Ramirez, NJ License #1754604

Two clocks start the day you become a New Jersey resident, and a third one starts later. You have 60 days to apply for a New Jersey driver’s licence and 60 days to title and register your vehicle — sooner if your out-of-state registration expires first. Then, if your car is five model years old or older, it must be inspected within 14 days after you register it. That third one is the deadline almost nobody mentions, because it is triggered by the registration rather than the move.

Most relocation checklists are lists of chores — forward the mail, call the utilities. Those matter, but they do not have statutes attached. These do. Everything below is cited to the New Jersey Driver Manual or the MVC directly, because the penalty for guessing is driving on an invalid licence or an unregistered car.

1. Driver’s licence — 60 days, or sooner

The Driver Manual is unambiguous: a motorist with a valid out-of-state licence who moves to New Jersey must apply for a New Jersey licence within 60 days, or before the current licence expires, whichever is sooner. A commercial driver licence gets 30 days, not 60.

The trap is the “whichever is sooner.” If you move in October and your old licence expires in November, you do not have 60 days — you have until November. People read the headline number and miss the clause.

Your out-of-state licence must be surrendered when you receive the New Jersey one; you do not get to keep both. Authority: N.J.S.A. 39:3-17.1, 39:3-10.17 and 39:3-10.

If your licence is from another country rather than another state, the rule is different and far more generous: you may drive on it for up to one year (N.J.A.C. 13:21-8.2(f)).

2. Title and registration — also 60 days

“If a motorist moves into this State, the law requires his/her vehicle to be titled and registered within 60 days; sooner, if the out-of-state registration expires before then.” That is N.J.S.A. 39:3-17.1, and the same “sooner” clause applies.

Practical notes that save a wasted trip:

  • You complete Form BA-49 at an MVC Vehicle Center.
  • The titled owner has to appear, or someone with authority to act on the owner’s behalf.
  • Appointment required — no walk-ins for a new title or registration. The MVC says so on its own registration page.
  • Bring the title, your driver’s licence, and the name of the vehicle’s current insurance company.
  • An initial registration on a brand-new vehicle runs four years. Everything else is typically one year (N.J.S.A. 39:3-4).

3. Inspection — 14 days after you register, not after you move

This is the one that catches people. “When moving to New Jersey from another state, a motorist must have his/her passenger vehicle five model years or older inspected within 14 days after registering it” (N.J.A.C. 13:20-7.4).

Read that clock carefully. It does not start when you move. It starts when you register — which means if you use all 60 of your registration days, you have committed yourself to an inspection inside the following two weeks, in the middle of a move.

If the passenger vehicle is less than five model years old, the Driver Manual says it “will be exempted from inspection and will receive a new car dealer sticker expiring five years from the model year.” Commercial registrations are inspected annually. Worth a phone call before you skip the appointment, though — the manual is the MVC’s own summary of N.J.A.C. 13:20-7.4, and we have not read the regulation text itself, so confirm with the MVC (1-888-656-6867) rather than relying on a summary.

4. Voter registration — 21 days before an election

New Jersey’s voter registration deadline is 21 days before an election. The efficient move is to register at the motor vehicle agency while you are already there for the licence — the MVC reports the registration to the New Jersey Division of Elections for you, so it costs one extra form and no extra trip.

5. A name change gets two weeks, not sixty days

If your name legally changed through marriage, divorce or court order, you must report it to the MVC within two weeks (N.J.S.A. 39:3-9a). This catches couples who move and marry in the same summer and assume the licence deadline covers both.

The order that avoids a second trip

Licence, then title and registration, then inspection. The registration checklist asks for your driver’s licence, and the inspection window does not open until the vehicle is registered. Running them in that sequence keeps the 14-day inspection clock from starting while you are still unpacking, and keeps you from queueing twice for want of one document.

Before any of it, assemble your 6 Point ID Verification documents. New Jersey’s identification standard is stricter than many states’ and it is the single most common reason a first MVC visit fails.

What is not on this list, deliberately. School registration, utility transfers and mail forwarding vary by town and provider, and no statute sets your deadline. Municipal offices differ enough that a generic instruction would be wrong somewhere. Ask for the specific town once you have narrowed the search.

The money side of the move

The MVC deadlines are the urgent part; the tax layer is the expensive part. If you are arriving from New York City, the arithmetic is worth understanding before you sign anything: crossing the river removes the New York City resident income tax entirely, while New Jersey’s property taxes are the highest in the country. Frequently Asked Questions

How long do I have to get a New Jersey driver's license after moving?

Sixty days. The New Jersey Driver Manual states that a motorist with a valid out-of-state license who moves to New Jersey must apply for a New Jersey license within 60 days, or before the current license expires, whichever is sooner. A commercial driver license must be transferred within 30 days. Your out-of-state license has to be surrendered when you receive the New Jersey one. The authority is N.J.S.A. 39:3-17.1, 39:3-10.17 and 39:3-10.

How long do I have to register my car in New Jersey?

Sixty days from moving into the state, and sooner if your out-of-state registration expires before then. New Jersey law requires a vehicle to be titled and registered within 60 days of the owner moving into the state, under N.J.S.A. 39:3-17.1. You complete a vehicle registration application, Form BA-49, at an MVC Vehicle Center, and the titled owner has to appear in person.

Do I need a New Jersey inspection after I move?

Yes, and this is the deadline people miss. If your passenger vehicle is five model years old or older, it must be inspected within 14 days after you register it — not 14 days after you move. That is N.J.A.C. 13:20-7.4. The Driver Manual says a vehicle less than five model years old "will be exempted from inspection" and receives a sticker expiring five years from its model year. That is the MVC's own summary of the rule rather than the regulation text, so confirm with the MVC before skipping the appointment.

Can I walk into an MVC agency without an appointment?

For a new title or registration, no. The MVC's own vehicle registration page instructs new-title and new-registration customers to schedule an appointment and states plainly that there are no walk-ins for that transaction. Book before you drive over.

What if I have a driver's license from another country?

You may drive on a valid license from another country for up to one year, under N.J.A.C. 13:21-8.2(f). That is a longer runway than the 60 days a US out-of-state licence gets, but it still runs out.

When do I register to vote in New Jersey?

The voter registration deadline is 21 days before an election. You can register at a motor vehicle agency while you are applying for or renewing a licence or non-driver ID, and the MVC forwards the information to the New Jersey Division of Elections — so it costs you nothing to do it in the same visit.

What do I need to bring to the MVC?

New Jersey uses a 6 Point ID Verification standard, so plan on assembling six points of identification before you go. A new registration also calls for the vehicle title, your driver's license and the name of the vehicle's current insurance company.

What order should I do these in?

License first, then title and registration, then inspection. The registration checklist asks for your driver's licence, and the inspection clock only starts once the vehicle is registered — so doing them in that order avoids a second trip and keeps the 14-day inspection window from opening before you are ready for it.

Do I have to tell the MVC if my name changed?

Yes, within two weeks of a legal name change from marriage, divorce or court order, under N.J.S.A. 39:3-9a. Worth knowing if the move and the name change are happening in the same season.

Sources. Deadlines and statutory citations above are taken from the New Jersey Driver Manual (revised 09/25, published by the NJ Motor Vehicle Commission) and from the MVC’s vehicle registration and voter registration pages at nj.gov/mvc. Rules change — confirm current requirements with the MVC before you rely on a date.

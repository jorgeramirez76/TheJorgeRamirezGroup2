NJ Housing Market Summer 2026: What Buyers and Sellers Need to Know Right Now

Published  ·  July 4, 2026

Short answer

NJ's median home price hit $563K in May 2026, up 3.3% year-over-year. Inventory is rising (up 8.5%) but still tight. Mortgage rates sit in the mid-6% range. It is still a seller's market — but a calmer one than 2021-2022. Correctly priced homes sell. Overpriced ones sit.

The NJ market is shifting. Not crashing — shifting. Here is what the latest data actually means for anyone buying or selling in Union, Essex, Morris, Hudson, or Middlesex counties this summer.

The Numbers: Where NJ Stands Right Now

Every summer I get the same question: "Is now a good time to buy (or sell)?" My answer is always the same — it depends on the specific data, and the data right now tells a clear story.

New Jersey's median home price reached $563,000 in May 2026, up 3.3% from a year earlier. That is slower growth than the 8-10% we saw annually in the peak commuter towns during 2022-2024, but it is still growth. Prices are not falling. The market is normalizing, not reversing.

Inventory is up 8.5% year over year — about 31,600 homes statewide in May 2026. That sounds like progress, and it is. But keep it in context: we are still well below pre-2020 inventory levels. More options for buyers, but not enough supply to push prices down. Think of it as the market exhaling after holding its breath for three years.

The stat I watch most closely: 47.2% of NJ homes are still selling above list price. That is down from about 50% a year ago, but it means nearly half of correctly priced homes in this state are generating multiple offers and bidding competition. If you are a buyer thinking the market has gone soft enough to lowball, that number should recalibrate your expectations.

What This Means If You Are Selling in NJ This Summer

The calculus for NJ sellers has shifted in one important direction: pricing margin for error is thinner than it was. In 2022, you could overprice by 5% and the market would catch you. In summer 2026, that 5% overage buys you three or four weeks of sitting while correctly priced competition gets multiple offers around you.

Buyers in 2026 are more informed than any buyer pool I have dealt with in 15 years. They have Zillow, Redfin, and their own agent's data. They know what your neighbors' homes sold for. They know the difference between a 2019 kitchen remodel and a 2024 one. If your price does not match the product, they skip it and go to the next one — and there are more choices now than there were a year ago.

The towns where sellers continue to have the most leverage: Summit, Chatham, Westfield, Maplewood, South Orange, Montclair, and Millburn. These commuter-corridor addresses have a structural floor built on NYC buyer demand that does not fluctuate the way purely suburban markets do. If you own in one of those towns, summer 2026 is still an excellent time to sell — with honest pricing.

What This Means If You Are Buying in NJ This Summer

Mortgage rates in the mid-6% range are the defining constraint on the 2026 buyer. At 6.5% on a $563K median-priced home with 20% down, you are looking at a principal-and-interest payment around $2,850/month — before NJ property taxes, which can add $1,200 to $2,000 or more per month depending on the town and assessment.

That math tells you why affordability pressure is concentrated at the $400K-$650K range, and why towns like Rahway, Linden, Bloomfield, and Metuchen — where you can still find homes in that tier — have active, competitive buyer pools despite rates.

The good news for buyers: inventory is up 8.5% and still climbing. You have more choices than any buyer since 2019. The frenzy of sight-unseen offers with 20% over ask and no inspection contingencies has largely receded. You can still get an inspection. You can still negotiate modest credits for legitimate issues. The market is not a buyer's market — but it is a functional market, which is different from the dysfunction we saw during the pandemic surge.

My advice to any buyer this summer: get pre-approved before you go to a single open house. Not pre-qualified — pre-approved. A fully underwritten pre-approval letter from a reputable lender makes your offer competitive in a way that a pre-qual letter does not. I have watched well-positioned buyers with clean pre-approval letters beat out higher offers because the listing agent knew the deal would actually close.

The Counties to Watch: What Is Happening Where

Union County: Still the value corridor along the Midtown Direct and Raritan Valley lines. Summit and Westfield continue to see the lowest days-on-market of any commuter towns in the state. Cranford and Fanwood are picking up overflow buyers priced out of those two markets.

Essex County: Montclair, South Orange, and Maplewood remain among the most competitive markets in NJ for sub-$1M properties. Essex County's cultural cachet with NYC transplants continues to support demand that outpaces supply. The towns further out — Bloomfield, Nutley — are gaining traction as affordability pressure pushes buyers north and east.

Morris County: Chatham and Madison are as competitive as they have ever been at any price point. The further you go — Morristown, Denville, Randolph — the more you trade commute convenience for space, and you start to see more rational pricing and less bidding pressure. Buyers with flexibility are finding genuine value in the $600K-$800K range in those towns.

Middlesex County: Edison, Metuchen, and South Brunswick are attracting buyers with strong tech and pharmaceutical industry employment in the Route 1 and Route 9 corridors. The Northeast Corridor train access in Metuchen and New Brunswick keeps commuter demand active.

Hudson County: Hoboken and Jersey City operate on their own supply-demand dynamics, closer to a small-city condo market than a traditional suburban one. Both remain competitive, though condo inventory has improved more than single-family inventory in other counties.

My Read: What to Do Right Now

If you are a seller: summer is your window. Inventory rises further in fall as more people list before year-end. Price correctly from day one. Hire a photographer. Do not skip the pre-listing preparation. The delta between a home that shows well and one that does not is $20,000-$50,000 at the negotiating table in this market.

If you are a buyer: the right move is preparation, not hesitation. Get pre-approved. Get your finances clean. Define your budget and your two non-negotiable criteria. Then move with conviction when the right home appears. The buyers who stall lose to the buyers who are ready. Rates may drift slightly lower later this year, but they will not drop enough to justify waiting while good inventory goes under contract around you.

Frequently Asked Questions: NJ Housing Market Summer 2026

What is the median home price in NJ in summer 2026?

The statewide median was $563,000 in May 2026, up 3.3% year over year. That number hides a wide range: under $500K in towns like Rahway and Linden, $750K-$850K in Maplewood and South Orange, $1.2M-$1.5M in Summit and Westfield, and $1.5M+ in Short Hills and Chatham. The statewide median tells you the direction. The town-level data tells you the number that matters for your decision.

Is now a good time to sell a home in NJ?

Yes — if you price correctly. Nearly half of NJ homes are still selling above list price in summer 2026. The market is not the frenzy of 2022, but well-priced homes in desirable commuter towns are moving quickly. The window closes as fall inventory rises. If you have been thinking about selling, summer is when your competition is lowest relative to demand.

Are NJ home prices expected to keep rising?

Most forecasts for NJ in the second half of 2026 project modest appreciation in the 2-4% range — slower than the 8-10% annual gains in premium commuter towns during 2022-2024, but continued upward movement. The structural driver — chronic undersupply relative to demand — has not changed. Prices would need a significant inventory surge or a major spike in rates to reverse. Neither looks likely through year-end.

Should I wait for mortgage rates to drop before buying in NJ?

Rates are projected to stay in the low-to-mid 6% range through most of 2026. A drop to 5.5% would matter meaningfully — roughly $350/month on a $600K loan. But that kind of drop is not what most forecasters are projecting in the near term. In the meantime, if rates do fall significantly, a refinance is available to you. The home you want may not be available later. Buy when the home is right; refinance when the rate improves.

Which NJ towns are the best values in summer 2026?

Best values in the commuter-town context: Cranford and Fanwood (Raritan Valley Line, Union County, strong schools under $750K median), Metuchen (Northeast Corridor, Middlesex County, under $725K), and Bloomfield (Essex County, under $525K with easy Montclair access). For buyers willing to go further, Denville, Randolph, and Morristown in Morris County offer the most house per dollar among Midtown Direct corridor towns.

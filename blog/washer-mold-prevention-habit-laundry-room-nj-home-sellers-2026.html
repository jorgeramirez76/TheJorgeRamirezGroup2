The One Habit That Keeps a Washer Mold-Free Before You Sell a NJ Home

Monday’s House Outlook newsletter led with a simple one: the single habit that keeps a washing machine from growing mold. It is good appliance advice. For a New Jersey seller it is also the difference between a laundry room that reads as cared for and one that makes a buyer start sniffing for a moisture problem.

The tip House Outlook sent this week

The lead story in Monday’s House Outlook newsletter was about washing machines, and the point was almost too simple to be a story: the appliance that cleans everything else grows mold and smells unless you give it a chance to dry out. The fix is a habit, not a product. When the load comes out, leave the door open.

I have walked buyers through hundreds of basements in Cranford, Westfield and Scotch Plains. The laundry area is where they stop talking and start smelling. A musty front-loader does not stay a washer problem in their mind. It becomes a question about the whole basement.

Why a smelly washer matters in a NJ sale

In most New Jersey contracts the washer and dryer are listed as included or excluded, and buyers open the door to see what they are getting. In older colonials in Maplewood, Montclair and Union the laundry is usually in a basement that already runs damp. A washer that smells of mildew confirms what the buyer was afraid of, and now they are looking at the foundation walls and the floor by the utility sink.

In Hoboken and Jersey City condos the washer sits in a closet off the kitchen or hallway. There is no basement to blame. A stale, sour smell when the closet door opens lands directly on the kitchen the buyer just admired.

The habit, and the five-minute routine around it

  1. Leave the door and the detergent drawer open after every load. This is the habit House Outlook pointed to. A front-loader seals tight, and a sealed, wet drum is where mold starts. Prop the door a few inches. If kids or pets make that a problem, open it once the house is quiet.
  2. Wipe the gasket dry. Pull back the rubber fold around the door opening. That channel holds water, lint and the occasional sock. Dry it with a towel after the last load of the day.
  3. Use less detergent. High-efficiency machines need a small dose. Extra soap does not rinse out, and the residue is what mold feeds on. Follow the detergent and washer instructions, not the cap line.
  4. Run the cleaning cycle monthly. Most machines have one. Use a washer cleaner or the method in your owner’s manual. Do not improvise with mixed chemicals.
  5. Check the drain filter. Many front-loaders have a small access door at the bottom. Clearing it is in the manual and takes ten minutes. A clogged one holds standing water.
  6. Move wet laundry promptly. A load left overnight is the fastest way to a sour drum, and it is the smell buyers notice first.

Jorge’s tip: Once the house is listed, run an empty hot cleaning cycle the day before photos and before any open house, then leave the door open with a folded towel across the gasket. Buyers who open a washer and see a dry, clean seal read the whole laundry area differently. Pair it with the laundry habits checklist and the dryer vent guide so the two machines get handled together.

When the smell is not the washer

Some machines will not come back. Black staining deep in the gasket that does not wipe off, a smell that returns within days of a cleaning cycle, or water that stays in the drum after a spin are signs of a worn seal or a drain problem. Replacing a gasket is a contained repair. Deciding to exclude the washer from the sale is also an option, and it is cleaner than conveying a machine a buyer will complain about after closing.

If the drum is clean and the room still smells musty, the washer was never the problem. A basement in Chatham, Summit or Morristown that runs humid all summer needs a dehumidifier and a look at where the water is coming from. Use the guide to where mold hides and the hidden water leak checklist before you list.

For buyers: what to check in the laundry area

If the washer conveys, open it. Smell the drum, pull back the gasket, and look for standing water. Check the supply hoses for bulging or rust at the fittings, look at the floor and the wall behind the machine, and ask whether there is a drain pan or a floor drain. In Edison, Woodbridge and Linden homes where the laundry is on a finished level, that floor matters more than the appliance. Add the washer and the dryer vent to your inspection question list. A smelly washer is not a reason to walk away. A wet floor behind it might be.

Questions about washer mold, laundry rooms and selling a NJ home

What is the one habit that prevents mold in a washing machine?

Leave the door and the detergent drawer open after every load so the drum and gasket can dry. A sealed, wet front-loader is where mold starts. Wiping the rubber gasket dry after the last load of the day makes the habit work faster.

Does a musty washer affect what buyers think of a NJ home?

Yes, more than the machine deserves. Buyers smell the laundry area and carry the question into the basement walls, the floor and the rest of the house. A dry, clean washer removes a moisture question before the buyer asks it.

Should the washer and dryer be included when I sell my NJ home?

That is a contract decision to make with your agent and attorney. If the machines are clean and work well, including them is a common convenience for buyers. If the washer is worn or smells, excluding it is cleaner than conveying an appliance a buyer will complain about.

How do I get the mildew smell out of a front-load washer before showings?

Pull back the gasket and clean the channel, clear the drain filter, run the machine’s cleaning cycle with a washer cleaner per the manual, and then leave the door open. If the smell returns within days, the gasket may need replacing or the drain may be the problem.

Is a damp basement laundry room a disclosure issue in New Jersey?

A smelly washer is not. Known water intrusion, recurring dampness or a mold problem in the basement can be. Raise anything you know about with your agent and attorney when you prepare the seller disclosure rather than masking it with a cleaner.

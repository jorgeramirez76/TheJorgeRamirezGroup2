How Much Is My NJ Home Worth? 2026 Valuation Guide

A careful way to read a CMA, an appraisal and an automated estimate—without treating any estimate as a guaranteed sale price.

Request a Property ReviewAsk Jorge a Question
Reviewed by Jorge Ramirez, NJ real estate license #1754604
Published: April 24, 2026 · Updated: August 27, 2026

The direct answer

A useful answer is a property-specific range, not a universal online number. A property-specific range is tied to a specific property and date. It reflects the best available comparable evidence and the limits of that evidence.

A CMA is a broker price opinion and market estimate. It should not be considered the equivalent of an appraisal prepared by a New Jersey licensed or certified real estate appraiser. A CMA does not guarantee a sale price.

Market value can change as new listings, contracts and closed sales appear. The eventual contract price also depends on marketing, property condition, buyer demand, financing, inspections, appraisal and negotiated terms. That is why a responsible analysis explains both its evidence and its uncertainty.

How a careful CMA is developed

There is no reliable statewide formula for the number, radius or age of comparable sales. The selection and analysis must fit the property, available data, market and effective date. Fannie Mae and Freddie Mac appraisal guidance is written for appraisal assignments, but its emphasis on relevant comparables and market-supported adjustments is a useful guardrail against arbitrary formulas.

1

Define the property, effective date and question

Confirm the address, property type, physical characteristics, known condition and intended use. A possible listing conversation is different from a mortgage, tax, court or insurance decision.

2

Select relevant closed sales

Choose comparables for their locational, physical and market similarity. Explain why each sale is useful and why less-similar alternatives received less weight. Do not force a fixed count or distance when the market evidence calls for a different approach.

3

Review current competition and market direction

Active and pending listings can provide context about supply and buyer choices. A pending contract's final price is not known until the transaction closes, so it should not be presented as a closed-sale fact.

4

Analyze meaningful differences

Compare property type, size, layout, site, condition, permitted improvements, utility and market timing. Use market-supported adjustments only when the available evidence supports them; avoid universal price-per-feature percentages.

5

Explain the range and uncertainty

Reconcile the evidence into a reasoned range and state the data cutoff, assumptions and important limitations. Update the analysis when material new information becomes available.

A verified New Jersey benchmark—not an address estimate

The NJ REALTORS July 2026 statewide report recorded a July 2026 single-family median sale price of $650,000, up 4.0% from July 2025. For year to date through July, the median was $610,000, up 3.7% from the same period a year earlier. The report states that its data is as of August 9, 2026 and that reported statewide numbers have a ±4% margin of error at a 95% confidence level.

This is a statewide benchmark and not an estimate for a particular address. A median describes the middle sale in the report's defined property type and period; it does not adjust for an individual home's municipality, site, size, condition or features. The linked report is published through a rolling endpoint, so use the NJ REALTORS reports directory to confirm the report title, property type and period before relying on it. The FHFA New Jersey house-price index distributed by FRED is a separate index with a different method and release schedule; it should not be substituted for a municipal CMA.

Property factors to document—without universal premiums

  • Property type, size and layout: compare how buyers in the relevant market respond to functional differences, not merely raw square footage.
  • Condition and permitted work: document observable condition, known defects and verified improvements rather than assuming a standard return for a project.
  • Lot and site: consider usable area, topography, drainage, access and other property-specific characteristics supported by comparable evidence.
  • Structural and mechanical systems: note available information about the roof, foundation, electrical, plumbing, heating and cooling without turning replacement costs into automatic value adjustments.
  • Market timing and supply: distinguish closed evidence from active competition and describe changes that occurred after older sales.
  • Objective housing information: evaluate the property and documented market evidence consistently. For housing-rights guidance, consult the New Jersey Division on Civil Rights.

When you need more than a CMA

  • Mortgage or refinance: ask the lender which valuation product is required. A licensed or certified appraiser may be assigned for the lending decision.
  • Property-tax appeal: follow the New Jersey property-tax appeal instructions, deadlines and evidence requirements. A CMA may help organize research, but it does not guarantee an appeal result; consult an attorney or qualified tax professional about your circumstances.
  • Divorce, probate, bankruptcy or another proceeding: under NJDOBI Bulletin 13-05, the court or administrative body decides whether a CMA is acceptable and what weight it receives. Ask counsel which document and effective date are needed.
  • Insurance: market value and replacement cost answer different questions. Review dwelling coverage and replacement-cost needs with an insurance professional; the New Jersey home-buying and insurance guide provides consumer context.

Frequently asked questions

What is a CMA?

A comparative market analysis is a broker price opinion and market estimate that reviews relevant comparable evidence for a specified property and date. It is not equivalent to an appraisal and does not guarantee a sale price.

Is a CMA the same as an appraisal?

No. An appraisal is prepared by a licensed or certified appraiser for an identified assignment. NJDOBI requires a prominent disclosure that a CMA or broker price opinion should not be considered equivalent to an appraisal.

What data does a Zestimate use?

Zillow says its automated model uses public records, multiple listing service data, user-submitted facts, listing data, comparable homes and market trends. Zillow also says the Zestimate is not an appraisal.

How long does a CMA take?

The scope and timing depend on the property, the intended use, the available records and whether additional information is needed. The agent should confirm the scope and expected timing after reviewing the request.

Does New Jersey's statewide median tell me my home's value?

No. The July and year-to-date figures describe statewide single-family sales in those reporting periods. An address-specific analysis needs relevant property and local market evidence.

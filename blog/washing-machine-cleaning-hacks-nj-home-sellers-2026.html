8 Household Items NJ Home Sellers Can Clean in the Washing Machine

You've wiped down the counters and mopped the floors — but these eight items are sitting in your home right now collecting dust, odors, and buyer skepticism. The good news: most of them go straight in the washer.

The items buyers notice that sellers overlook

In 15 years and more than 500 closed transactions across Union, Morris, Essex, Hudson, and Middlesex counties, I've walked through thousands of NJ homes. Here's what I know: buyers are emotional decision-makers. They can't always explain why a house didn't feel right — they just know it didn't. More often than sellers realize, the culprit isn't a cracked tile or a dated kitchen. It's a dingy throw pillow on the couch, a musty-smelling curtain, or a bathroom mat that's seen better days.

The great news? None of these are expensive fixes. Many of them take about 45 minutes in your washing machine. Before you list your NJ home, run through this list.

Before you start: Always check the care label. Most items listed below are machine-washable, but fabric type, fill material, and embellishments can change that. When in doubt, use cold water and a gentle cycle, and air dry rather than machine dry to avoid shrinkage.

1

Throw Pillows and Decorative Pillow Covers

Throw pillows are staging tools — they should look fresh and inviting, not flat and faded. Removable covers can almost always go in the washer. The pillow inserts themselves (especially down or poly-fill) can often go in on a gentle cycle with a couple of tennis balls to keep them fluffy. This one small step makes your living room look staged without spending a dime on new pillows. In NJ homes priced between $500K–$800K — the heart of the Morris and Union County markets — buyers expect that kind of attention to detail.

2

Curtains and Window Panels

Curtains are the first thing buyers scan when they walk into a room. Dirty, wrinkled, or faded window panels make an entire room feel neglected — even if everything else is spotless. Most polyester and cotton curtains are machine washable. Wash on cold, gentle cycle, and hang immediately to prevent wrinkles. If they're sheer or delicate, a cool tumble dry for 10 minutes then air dry works beautifully. The result: windows look intentional, light flows better, and the room photographs like a magazine shoot.

3

Fabric Shower Curtains

The bathroom is the most scrutinized room after the kitchen in any NJ showing. A fabric shower curtain with mildew stains or soap scum buildup is a red flag buyers won't forget. Toss it in the washer with hot water and a cup of white vinegar. It comes out looking nearly new. If it's beyond saving, a clean replacement from HomeGoods costs $20 and the ROI is enormous. A clean, crisp shower curtain tells buyers the whole home has been cared for.

Photo via Unsplash — A fresh, well-staged bathroom makes buyers feel confident about the whole property.

4

Bathroom and Kitchen Rugs / Bath Mats

Every NJ buyer steps on that bath mat. A plush, clean rug underfoot creates comfort. A gray, dingy one creates doubt. Most bath mats and small area rugs are washer-safe — hot cycle for heavy-duty cleaning, then air dry or low tumble. Rubber-backed mats should air dry to preserve the backing. If they don't come clean, replace them. A fresh bath mat costs less than $15 and is one of the cheapest investments a seller can make in buyer confidence.

5

Throw Blankets and Sofa Throws

A neatly folded throw blanket draped over a couch arm is classic staging. A throw that smells like pets or feels stiff from dust? That's a buyer repellent. Wash on gentle, cold cycle with a mild detergent. Wool or cashmere throws may need dry cleaning — check the label. Most fleece, cotton, and acrylic throws come out beautifully. Fold them neatly after washing and drape them over a corner of the sofa for that magazine-cover look that photographing NJ homes well demands.

6

Reusable Grocery Bags and Totes

This one surprises sellers. Reusable bags hanging on your pantry hooks or sitting in a mudroom basket can carry odors — produce residue, spilled liquids, general kitchen smells. Buyers open pantries and mudrooms constantly. Toss those bags in the washer before showings. It takes two minutes. Mudrooms in Summit, Scotch Plains, and Westfield homes are huge selling points — keep yours smelling clean and organized. This is the kind of detail that makes buyers say "this family really took care of the place."

7

Pet Beds and Soft Crates

If you have pets, you need to be twice as proactive about odor control than non-pet sellers. Pet beds — even if your dog is the cleanest on the block — carry pet smell that buyers with allergies detect immediately. Most fabric pet beds are machine washable. Wash with hot water, unscented detergent, and dry on high. If the pet bed isn't salvageable, put it in the garage during showings. I've seen buyers in Essex County walk out of otherwise beautiful homes the moment they smelled pets. Don't let that be your story.

8

Slipcovers and Furniture Covers

If you have slipcovers on sofas, chairs, or ottomans, they should be washed before the first showing and between showings if possible. Most cotton and polyester slipcovers are machine-washable. They photograph better when freshly laundered — the fabric lays flatter and the color looks true. For a home in a competitive market like Montclair, Cranford, or East Brunswick, that extra sharpness in the listing photos alone is worth the 45-minute wash cycle.

The bottom line for NJ sellers

First impressions in real estate aren't just about what buyers see — they're about what they smell, what they touch, and how a space makes them feel. Buyers touring NJ homes at $600,000, $800,000, or $1.2 million are making emotional decisions backed by financial logic. You want their emotion to be "this is the one" — not "I wonder what else they neglected."

None of the items on this list cost you more than an hour and a little detergent. But the combined effect — a home that looks, smells, and feels genuinely cared for — supports your asking price, shortens your time on market, and gives buyers the confidence to make a strong offer.

Frequently asked questions

Does cleaning help NJ homes sell for more money?

A spotlessly clean, fresh-smelling home creates a stronger first impression, reduces buyer objections, and supports your asking price. In competitive NJ markets like Union and Morris County, presentation details absolutely matter at the negotiating table.

What household items can I machine-wash before a home showing?

Most pillows, throw blankets, curtains, fabric shower curtains, area rug bath mats, reusable bags, slipcovers, and some window valances can go in a standard front- or top-loader on a gentle cycle. Always check the care label first.

How far in advance should I start cleaning before listing my NJ home?

Start deep cleaning at least two to three weeks before the first showing. That gives you time to tackle overlooked items like pillows and curtains, address any odor issues, and do touch-up cleaning between showings.

Do NJ buyers really notice details like pillows and curtains?

Yes. Buyers touring homes in Summit, Westfield, Chatham, and similar NJ markets are often looking at multiple properties in one afternoon. A home that smells fresh and looks genuinely clean — including soft furnishings — stands out and gives buyers more confidence.

Should I hire a professional cleaner before listing in NJ?

A professional deep clean is one of the highest-ROI steps you can take before listing. It covers what washing machine hacks cannot: grout lines, baseboards, appliance interiors, and light fixtures. Think of it as an investment, not an expense.

How does cleanliness affect my NJ home's sale price?

Buyers mentally discount a dirty home even if they can't articulate why. In a competitive offer situation, a well-maintained, clean property gives buyers confidence in how the home was cared for overall — and that confidence translates directly to offer strength.

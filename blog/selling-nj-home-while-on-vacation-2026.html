Going on Vacation While Your New Jersey Home Is Listed for Sale

Last updated  ·  August 2026

Quick answer: Yes, you can go on vacation while your New Jersey home is listed. The two things that actually decide how it goes are how showing access is handled while you are gone and how the house holds up unattended in August humidity.

By Jorge Ramirez | August 2026

Every August I have some version of the same conversation. A seller has a trip on the calendar that was booked long before the house went on the market, and now they are quietly wondering whether they have to cancel it. Usually they have already talked themselves into believing they do.

You almost never do. A listed home does not require you to sit in it. What it requires is that the house stays easy to show and easy to like while you are somewhere else. Those are two different problems, and most sellers only plan for the first one.

Start with how access will actually work

Before anything else, get clear on how a buyer's agent gets in the door while you are away. In most of New Jersey that means an electronic lockbox, and it usually works fine. What causes trouble is the gap between what the listing says and what is really true. If your listing asks for a full day of notice because you have a dog and a home office, and then you leave for a week without changing that, agents keep hitting the same friction for no reason.

Before you go, decide and put in writing: the notice window while you are traveling, whether showings can be back to back, whether the alarm gets disarmed for the week or stays on with a code shared, and who has the authority to approve a same-day request. That last one matters more than people expect. Some of the best showings come from a buyer who saw three homes that morning, had a cancellation, and wants to squeeze in a fourth. If nobody can say yes for four days, that buyer sees something else.

Time zones are worth a thought too. If you are two or three hours behind and only checking your phone at night, you have effectively taken yourself out of the decision loop during the exact hours buyers are out looking.

The part nobody plans for: what humidity does to a closed-up house

Here is the failure I see most often, and it has nothing to do with showings. Someone turns the air conditioning way up or off entirely to save money for the week, closes every window, and comes home to a house that smells like a basement.

New Jersey summers are humid, and a sealed house with no air moving through it is a very good environment for that smell to develop. It settles into carpet, upholstery, closets, and anywhere air is already stagnant. Buyers who walk in on day four of that will not be able to name what is wrong. They will just describe the house as older or as needing work, and price their offer accordingly.

Set the thermostat to hold a reasonable temperature rather than shutting the system off. Run the dehumidifier in the basement if you have one, and make sure it drains somewhere it cannot overflow, not into a bucket that fills on day two. Leave interior doors and closet doors open so air actually circulates instead of pooling.

Then handle the water sources. Run a hot empty cycle in the washer and leave the door and the detergent drawer open. Front-load machines in particular hold moisture in the rubber gasket, and a sealed door for ten days in August is how that turns into a musty smell and a visibly grimy seal, which is exactly the kind of detail a buyer photographs. Empty the dishwasher and prop it open. Empty every trash can including the small ones in bathrooms. Pour a little water down any floor drain or rarely used bathroom drain so the trap does not dry out and let sewer gas back into the room.

Leave the house in a state it can hold

Your home is going to be shown without you there to tidy up between appointments, so it needs to be in a condition that survives strangers walking through it repeatedly. That is a different standard than day-to-day clean.

Clear the counters completely rather than mostly. Take the perishable food out of the refrigerator, because buyers do open it and a forgotten container is a memorable impression for the wrong reasons. Put away anything with your name, mail, prescriptions, or documents on it. Move jewelry, spare keys, and small valuables somewhere that is not a nightstand. Take the mail and package deliveries off the table by putting them on hold, since a pile of boxes on the porch tells anyone driving by that nobody is home.

The outside matters just as much. Arrange lawn care for the full stretch you are gone, not just the day before you leave, and put exterior lights on timers. A yard that is visibly a week overgrown reads as a house nobody is caring for, and that idea is hard to walk back.

If you have plants you are worried about, this is a good week to simplify. A few large plants clustered on a tray with water usually outlast a scattered collection of small pots that each need attention, and the house looks less cluttered for it. Pets should go with you or stay with someone. A crated dog and an active showing schedule is not a fair setup for anyone involved.

Two things to check before you board

Call your insurance carrier if the house will be empty for an extended period. Most standard policies treat a home as vacant only after a fairly long stretch, and a normal vacation does not come close. But if your trip is longer, or the house is already unoccupied because you have moved out, it is worth a direct question rather than an assumption.

Then decide who your local person is. Someone should be able to walk in if there is a storm, a leak, or an alarm. In practice that is often a neighbor, a family member, or your agent. What matters is that the answer exists before you need it, not after.

Go on the trip

What does your summer usually look like? If a week away is something you have counted on for years, the sale is not a good reason to give it up. Homes go under contract while sellers are on beaches all the time.

The version of this that goes badly is almost always the one where nobody had the conversation in advance. Somebody assumed the listing instructions still applied, or assumed the thermostat did not matter, or assumed a same-day request would just get handled.

If you have a trip coming up and your house is on the market, or you are weighing whether to list before or after you travel, I am glad to talk it through and help you sort out what actually needs to happen before you leave. No pressure to list anything, and no obligation on your end. Sometimes the useful answer is that the timing is fine and you should stop worrying about it. Frequently Asked Questions

Can I go on vacation while my New Jersey home is listed for sale?

Yes. What matters is that showing access is set up so agents can get in without waiting on you, and that the house is left in a condition it can hold for a week or more without anyone tidying between appointments. Sellers travel during active listings regularly and homes go under contract while they are away.

Should I turn off the air conditioning while I am away to save money?

Setting the system to hold a reasonable temperature is generally the better call than shutting it off. A sealed New Jersey house in August humidity can develop a musty smell that settles into carpet, closets, and upholstery, and buyers tend to read that smell as a sign the home has problems even when it does not.

How do buyers get in to see my home while I am traveling?

Typically through an electronic lockbox that records who entered and when. Before you leave, confirm the notice window in the listing instructions matches what is actually workable, and decide who can approve a same-day showing request on your behalf.

What should I do about pets and plants while showings continue?

Pets should travel with you or stay with someone else. A crated animal and an active showing schedule is stressful for the pet and awkward for buyers walking through. For plants, cluster them and set them up to go a stretch without attention, or move the fussier ones out of the way entirely so the house shows less cluttered.

Does my homeowners insurance change if the house is empty while it is listed?

A normal vacation typically does not trigger anything, since most policies define vacancy after a considerably longer period. If your trip is long, or you have already moved out and the home sits unoccupied, call your carrier and ask directly rather than assuming your coverage carries over unchanged.

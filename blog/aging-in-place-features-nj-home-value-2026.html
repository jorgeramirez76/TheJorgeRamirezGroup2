10 Aging-in-Place Features NJ Buyers Look For — And Why They Add Home Value

First-floor bedrooms, curbless showers, lever handles. These used to be filed under "senior" features. Today they're what downsizers, multigenerational families, and long-term buyers across NJ ask me about first.

Why aging-in-place features matter to NJ buyers of every age

Two kinds of buyers keep coming up in my conversations across Union, Morris, Essex, Hudson, and Middlesex counties. The first is the longtime homeowner in Westfield or Livingston who wants to sell the four-bedroom colonial and buy something they'll never have to leave. The second is the family in Edison or Woodbridge buying a home where a parent will eventually move in.

Both are working from the same list. A home that already has these features, or can easily accept them, sells to a wider pool than one with a steep front stoop and every bedroom upstairs. NJ's housing stock is old and vertical, which is exactly why these features stand out.

None of this means turning your home into a medical suite. Done well, the same design choices read as modern and comfortable to a 35-year-old buyer. Here's what the pros put on the list, and what each one means for value.

Jorge's observation: The two questions I hear most from downsizing buyers are "Is there a full bathroom on the first floor?" and "How many steps to get in?" A home that answers yes and zero has already beaten half the listings they toured that weekend.

The 10 aging-in-place features on every pro's list

  1. A no-step or low-step entry Even one step at the front door or from the garage matters. A level garage entry or a gently graded front walk is on nearly every downsizer's must-have list.
    The first thing downsizers check
  2. A bedroom and full bath on the main level The single most valuable aging-in-place feature in NJ, where most homes put every bedroom upstairs. A first-floor suite, or a room next to a full bath that could become one, widens your buyer pool more than any other item here.
    Highest impact on buyer pool
  3. A curbless, walk-in shower No tub wall to climb over, a built-in bench, and a handheld sprayer. To buyers of any age it reads as a spa upgrade, so it works in a listing photo as well as it works at 80.
    Reads as luxury, functions as safety
  4. Grab bars — or blocking in the walls for them If you're renovating a bathroom, have the contractor add wood blocking behind the tile now. It costs almost nothing during the job and a lot afterward. Well-chosen grab bars double as towel bars and match the other fixtures.
    Cost: low during a renovation, high after
  5. Lever handles and rocker switches Lever door handles, lever faucets, and rocker light switches are easy with a full hand and easier with an arthritic one. It's the cheapest item on this list and updates an older home's look at the same time.
    Cost: $15–$30 per door
  6. Wider doorways and hallways Doorways around 36 inches let a walker or wheelchair pass. In an existing home, offset hinges can add a couple of inches of clearance without reframing the opening.
    Ask your contractor about offset hinges
  7. Layered, bright lighting More light at the entry, on the stairs, in the kitchen, and along the path from bedroom to bathroom, with motion-sensor night lights. Better lighting is also what makes listing photos look expensive.
    Impact: safety now, photos at listing
  8. Non-slip flooring with no thresholds Continuous flooring without raised transitions between rooms, and a textured tile or vinyl in bathrooms. Fewer trip points, and a cleaner, more modern look that every buyer responds to.
    Continuous flooring reads modern to every buyer
  9. Storage you can reach Pull-out shelving in lower cabinets, drawers instead of doors, and a pantry where nothing important lives above shoulder height. These kitchen retrofits are among the most affordable on the list.
    Cost: roughly $100–$300 per cabinet retrofit
  10. Main-level laundry Carrying laundry to a basement is the daily task buyers planning to stay put most want to eliminate. A first-floor laundry closet is a strong selling point in older NJ homes.
    Frequently on downsizer must-have lists

What this means if you're selling — or buying — in NJ

If you already have these features, lead with them. A first-floor suite, a level entry, or a walk-in shower belongs in the first three lines of the listing description, not buried after the granite counters. I market these homes to downsizers in the same town and to multigenerational buyers coming from Hoboken, Jersey City, and the city, and they consistently draw a deeper pool.

If you're renovating before a sale, prioritize what's cheap now and expensive later: blocking for grab bars, a curbless shower if you're already redoing the bath, lever hardware, and better lighting. Skip anything that reads clinical.

If you're buying, ask what it would take to add these features later. A ranch in Scotch Plains or a split-level in Springfield with a first-floor bath and a garage entry can serve you for decades. A center-hall colonial with a steep stoop may be the home you love now and the home you have to sell in fifteen years.

Frequently asked questions

Do aging-in-place features increase a NJ home's value?

In my experience they widen the buyer pool, which is what protects value. A first-floor bedroom and full bath, a level entry, and a walk-in shower appeal to downsizers, multigenerational families, and buyers planning to stay for decades. A larger pool of motivated buyers is what supports a strong price.

What is the most important aging-in-place feature in a home?

A bedroom and full bathroom on the main level. In NJ, where most homes put every bedroom upstairs, it's the feature that most often makes or breaks a home for a downsizing buyer.

Will aging-in-place upgrades make my home look institutional?

Not if you choose well. Curbless showers, lever handles, layered lighting, continuous flooring, and drawer storage all read as modern design. Pick grab bars that match your other fixtures and they pass as towel bars.

Which aging-in-place upgrades are worth doing before selling?

The ones that are cheap now and expensive later: blocking for grab bars during any bathroom work, lever door and faucet hardware, brighter lighting, and a curbless shower if the bathroom is already being redone. Bigger changes like adding a first-floor suite should be weighed against your specific market and timeline.

Are there NJ programs that help seniors stay in their homes?

New Jersey offers property tax relief programs for eligible senior homeowners, including Senior Freeze and Stay NJ. Eligibility rules and benefit amounts change, so check the NJ Division of Taxation for current details. Combined with an accessible home, those programs make staying put more realistic for many NJ homeowners.

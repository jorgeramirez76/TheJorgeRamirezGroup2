Sherwin-Williams’ 2027 Color of the Year: How NJ Sellers Should Use a Bold Paint Color

House Outlook’s newsletter this week linked to Domino’s write-up of Sherwin-Williams’ 2027 Color of the Year, described as deeply refreshing and best paired with warm woods, creamy neutrals or even other saturated colors so the result feels layered rather than loud. Every year a color announcement sends a few sellers to the paint store. Here is how I tell them to use it, and when to walk past it.

The tip House Outlook sent this week

This week’s House Outlook newsletter pointed to a Domino piece on Sherwin-Williams’ 2027 Color of the Year. I am not going to describe the swatch for you, because a paint chip on a screen never matches a wall in a north-facing Chatham dining room. What matters for a seller is the direction: a deep, saturated shade meant to be layered with warm wood, creamy neutrals and brass, not splashed across every wall.

What a color of the year actually does to a listing

A color-of-the-year announcement is a forecast, not an instruction. Buyers do not walk into a Westfield colonial looking for a specific swatch. They respond to rooms that feel finished, bright and easy to picture themselves in, which is why most of the listings I prepare still sell on a warm white; the white paint guide explains which ones. A bold color earns its place in a listing only where it adds depth to a photograph without making the buyer calculate a repaint.

Where a deep color works in a NJ listing

  1. One room with a door. A dining room, a powder room, a study. Not the open main floor of a Summit or Millburn house, where the color will follow the buyer from the front door to the kitchen.
  2. Below the chair rail, or on a built-in. Wainscoting, a bookcase, a kitchen island. The color-capping guide covers how a partial application reads in photos.
  3. Paired the way the newsletter describes. Warm wood floors, creamy trim, brass or black hardware. Against cool gray walls, a saturated color goes flat and dated fast.
  4. Tested in New Jersey light. A north-facing room in a Morristown colonial, a Hoboken condo with one window and a Montclair bedroom with afternoon sun will each show the same can of paint three different ways. Paint a two-foot square and look at it morning and evening.
  5. With the trim and ceilings left consistent. One accent, one trim white throughout. Buyers notice when the trim changes color from room to room, even if they cannot say why.

Jorge’s tip: If the repaint is for the sale, saturated colors go in the smallest, most photographed rooms. A buyer in Scotch Plains remembers a navy powder room as a detail. A buyer who walks through a navy family room is already pricing the primer.

Where to leave it alone

The whole main floor, the bedrooms, and any kitchen whose cabinets are already dated. Those rooms need light, not drama, and a trend color in a room that also needs work reads as a distraction. Skip it anywhere you would be painting in the last two weeks before photos; a rushed accent wall with visible roller marks is worse than the beige it replaced. The paint-colors-that-hurt guide lists the shades that cost sellers, and the regretted trends guide covers what last year’s announcements look like now.

For buyers: do not let a paint color change your offer

A trendy accent wall is a weekend and two gallons. It is not a reason to walk away from a house in Cranford or Edison that works in every other way. A saturated color on every wall of a big house is a real repaint budget, and that belongs in your negotiation as a line item, not as a reaction. Look past the color at the trim, the floors and the light; those are what you are buying.

Questions about paint colors, trends and selling a NJ home

Should I paint with the Sherwin-Williams 2027 Color of the Year before listing my NJ home?

Only in one contained room, and only if the rest of the house is already in warm, neutral shape. A color of the year is a design forecast, not a selling tool. Most New Jersey listings I prepare sell on a warm white with one or two well-placed accents.

Do accent walls help or hurt a home sale?

A single accent in a dining room, powder room or study, paired with warm neutrals, usually photographs well and gives a listing a memorable detail. An accent wall in the main living space or the primary bedroom more often makes buyers add a repaint to their mental budget.

What paint colors do NJ buyers respond to?

Warm whites and soft, warm neutrals on the main floor and bedrooms, consistent trim throughout, and depth added through one contained saturated room or a painted built-in. Cool grays and a different color in every room are the two things buyers comment on most.

Is it worth repainting a whole house before selling?

Usually only the rooms that need it: anything scuffed, dark, cool gray or strongly personal. A full repaint is rarely required, and the money is often better spent on repairs and lighting. Decide room by room during a pre-listing walk-through.

As a buyer, should a bold paint color change my offer?

One accent wall should not; it is a weekend fix. A saturated color across most of a large house is a real repaint cost and can be a negotiation line item, but it should never be the reason you pass on a house whose layout, light and condition work.

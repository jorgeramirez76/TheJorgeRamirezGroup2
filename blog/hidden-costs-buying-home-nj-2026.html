Hidden Costs of Buying a Home in NJ: What First-Time Buyers Almost Always Miss in 2026

You've got your down payment saved and your pre-approval in hand. But in New Jersey, the purchase price is only the beginning. Here's the full picture of what it actually costs to buy a home here — before, at, and after closing.

Why NJ buyers get surprised — and how to avoid it

I've been selling homes across New Jersey for over 15 years. The buyers who feel worst after closing aren't the ones who paid too much for the house — they're the ones who had no idea how much they'd need beyond the down payment. New Jersey is not an inexpensive place to transact, and if your agent isn't walking you through the full picture early in the process, you're going to be scrambling at the closing table.

This guide covers every cost category I review with buyers before they make an offer. Not to scare anyone — NJ is a great place to own real estate and property values here reflect that — but to make sure you walk into this with eyes open and your finances lined up. A buyer who runs out of reserves at closing is a buyer in trouble, regardless of how good the deal was on the house.

I serve buyers across Union, Morris, Essex, Hudson, and Middlesex counties. The numbers below are based on what I actually see at closings, not generic national estimates.

Jorge's reserve rule: Separate your down payment from your closing cost reserve and your post-closing emergency fund before you start making offers. You need all three. Buyers who commingle these funds discover the problem at the worst possible moment — usually five days before closing.

Before closing: the costs that start at offer

Home inspection

A thorough home inspection by a licensed NJ inspector is the most important money you'll spend in the home buying process. A quality inspector who knows the age and construction patterns of NJ homes will run every system, test every outlet, and document what the next buyer would eventually find. Do not skip this.

Typical cost: $450–$700 depending on home size and age

Radon testing

NJ has significant radon activity in many counties, particularly in Morris, Somerset, and parts of Union. A radon test runs about $150–$200 and is almost always done at the same time as the home inspection. If levels come back elevated, remediation systems typically cost $800–$2,000 — and that's a legitimate inspection negotiation item with the seller.

Typical cost: $150–$200

Appraisal

Your mortgage lender will require an appraisal to confirm the home's value supports the loan amount. The buyer typically pays for this upfront. In NJ's market, appraisals on single-family homes run $600–$900. Condo appraisals are often higher due to the complexity of analyzing HOA financials and comparable units.

Typical cost: $600–$900

At closing: the NJ-specific fees buyers forget

Attorney fees — required in NJ

New Jersey is an attorney state for real estate transactions. Both buyer and seller are required to have their own attorneys. Your attorney reviews the contract, manages the title search, coordinates the closing, and protects your interests throughout. This isn't optional — it's state practice. Budget for it.

Typical cost: $1,500–$2,500

Title insurance (owner's policy)

Your lender requires their own title policy. Owner's title insurance — which protects your equity — is optional but consistently worth it in NJ. Title issues from unpaid liens, boundary disputes, or clerical errors in historical records don't show up until they become expensive problems. A one-time premium at closing is cheap insurance.

Typical cost: $1,800–$3,500 depending on purchase price

Mansion tax (purchases over $1 million)

This one catches buyers off guard every single time. If your purchase price is $1,000,000 or more, you owe the state of NJ 1% of the total purchase price at closing. On a $1.2 million home, that's $12,000 due at closing — above and beyond your other costs. In many Union and Essex County markets, this affects a significant share of transactions.

Cost: 1% of purchase price on homes $1M+

Mortgage origination and lender fees

Your lender charges origination fees, points (if applicable), underwriting fees, and various administrative costs. These vary by lender but typically total 0.5–1.5% of the loan amount. On a $500,000 mortgage, that's $2,500–$7,500 in lender fees alone. Compare Loan Estimates from multiple lenders — the rate matters, but so do the fees.

Typical cost: 0.5–1.5% of loan amount

Prepaid property taxes and homeowner's insurance

At closing, you'll prepay several months of property taxes and homeowner's insurance into your escrow account. In NJ, where property taxes are among the highest in the country, this prepaid amount can be significant — often $4,000–$10,000 depending on the town and closing date. This is money that goes into your escrow account, not lost — but you need it available at closing.

Typical cost: $4,000–$10,000+

The real number: total cash needed to close in NJ

On a $700,000 home with 20% down ($140,000 down payment), here's what buyers typically need in total:

Down payment: $140,000

Closing costs (2–4%): $14,000–$28,000

Inspections & appraisal: $1,200–$1,600

Attorney: $1,500–$2,500

Post-closing reserve: $10,000–$20,000

Total liquid funds needed: approximately $167,000–$192,000+

These are estimates. Your specific situation will vary based on lender, town, purchase price, and property condition. Always review your Loan Estimate carefully.

After closing: the expenses that surprise new NJ homeowners

The transaction closes, you get the keys, and then the real costs begin. First-time buyers in NJ are often shocked by what the first year of homeownership costs beyond the mortgage payment.

  • Property taxes: The statewide average effective rate is around 2.1–2.4%, but rates vary dramatically by town. That $700,000 home in Summit might carry $18,000–$22,000 in annual property taxes. Know the actual tax bill for any home you're serious about — not the estimate on Zillow.
  • Homeowner's insurance: Budget $1,500–$3,000 per year for a NJ single-family home. Homes in flood zones (check FEMA maps for Middlesex and Hudson County properties especially) require separate flood insurance, which can run $1,000–$4,000 per year depending on zone designation.
  • HOA fees (for condos and planned communities): Monthly HOA fees in NJ condo buildings range from $300 to $800+. Always review the HOA financials — specifically the reserve fund. An underfunded reserve means special assessments are coming. I've seen buyers get hit with $10,000–$30,000 special assessments within two years of purchase on buildings that looked fine from the outside.
  • Deferred maintenance you didn't negotiate: Even with a thorough inspection, first-year homeowners in older NJ homes typically spend $3,000–$8,000 on items they knew about but didn't address at closing. Budget for it. It's not a surprise if you plan for it.
  • Moving costs: Moving within NJ or from NYC? Professional movers for a 3–4 bedroom house typically run $3,000–$7,000. This is money many buyers forget to account for until the week of closing.

Why knowing this actually protects your investment

I'm not sharing this to discourage anyone from buying in NJ. Real estate in this state is one of the most reliable wealth-building tools available — 500+ home sales over 15 years have shown me that clearly. But the buyers who build real wealth through real estate are the ones who entered transactions fully informed, kept their reserves intact, and weren't forced to make bad decisions because they ran out of cash after closing.

Frequently asked questions

What are typical closing costs for a buyer in New Jersey?

NJ buyer closing costs typically run 2–4% of the purchase price. On a $600,000 home, that's $12,000–$24,000 in addition to your down payment. The biggest components are mortgage origination fees, title insurance, attorney fees, home inspection, appraisal, and prepaid property taxes and insurance. NJ requires both a buyer and seller attorney at closing — a cost unique to this state.

How high are property taxes in New Jersey?

NJ has among the highest property taxes in the country. The statewide average effective rate is approximately 2.1–2.4%, but it varies significantly by town. In Union County towns like Summit and Westfield, annual property taxes on a $700,000 home often run $15,000–$22,000. Always verify the actual current tax bill, not the online estimate, for any specific property.

Do NJ home buyers pay a transfer tax?

Generally, the realty transfer fee is paid by the seller in NJ. However, buyers of properties over $1 million do pay a 1% mansion tax. On a $1.2 million purchase, that's $12,000 due at closing — a line item many buyers don't budget for until they see their closing disclosure.

What should NJ buyers budget for after closing?

After closing, NJ buyers should have a reserve of at least $10,000–$20,000 for immediate expenses: repairs from the inspection, appliance or system needs, moving costs, and the first year's homeowner's insurance premium. First-time buyers in older NJ homes should budget toward the higher end of that range.

Is title insurance required for NJ home buyers?

Your lender requires lender's title insurance. Owner's title insurance, which protects your equity, is optional but strongly recommended in NJ. A one-time premium at closing protects you from chain-of-title issues that could otherwise result in significant legal costs down the road.

What is the NJ mansion tax and when does it apply?

The NJ mansion tax is a 1% transfer fee paid by the buyer on residential purchases of $1,000,000 or more. It applies to the entire purchase price. On a $1.1 million purchase, the mansion tax is $11,000 — a significant closing cost that catches buyers off guard in higher-end markets like Summit, Chatham, Montclair, and Hoboken.

What a Pest Problem Costs NJ Sellers

In fifteen years selling homes across Union, Essex, Morris, and Middlesex counties, I've watched pest issues derail more transactions than almost anything else on the inspection report. Not because they're always expensive to fix — sometimes they're not — but because of what they signal to buyers.

When a buyer's inspector finds evidence of termites, active rodents, or carpenter ant damage, the buyer's first thought is: what else haven't they caught? That's the trust problem. And in a market where buyers have a 7-day attorney review period and a full inspection contingency, that uncertainty gives them every reason to walk or renegotiate hard.

The math is simple: a $500 pre-listing pest inspection and $800–$2,000 in treatment, if needed, is almost always cheaper than a $10,000–$25,000 price concession negotiated after the buyer's inspector finds something. In NJ, wood-destroying insect (WDI) reports are standard in residential transactions. The pest question is not whether buyers will look — it's whether you've already dealt with it.

Here are the six early warning signs that are easy to miss but mean you need a professional assessment before you list.

1 Wood That Sounds Hollow When You Tap It

This is the classic termite tell. Grab a screwdriver handle or your knuckle and tap along your window frames, baseboards, door frames, exposed beams, and flooring. Sound solid and dense? Good. Sound oddly hollow, papery, or like there's empty space behind it? That's a red flag. Subterranean termites eat wood from the inside out, leaving a paper-thin outer shell that can literally collapse under your finger if the damage is extensive. The outside of the wood may look completely normal while the interior is gone. In NJ, subterranean termites are especially common in older wood-frame homes throughout Essex and Union counties.

2 Wood That Looks Blistered or Warped (Without a Leak)

If you see wood that appears blistered, slightly warped, or damaged in a way that resembles water damage — but there's no corresponding leak, roof issue, or plumbing problem in the area — termites can create exactly that appearance. They tunnel close to the surface and the resulting pressure causes the wood to buckle or blister. This often shows up on windowsills, the lower sections of exterior door frames, and the wood trim around basement windows. If you're attributing warped wood to "old age" without a clear cause, get it checked.

3 Scratching or Scurrying Sounds Inside Walls at Night

Pests are most active at night, and rodents in particular tend to move around after dark when the house is quiet. Scratching, scurrying, or light thumping sounds coming from inside walls, ceilings, or floors is almost always an animal — mice, rats, or squirrels are the most common culprits in NJ homes. Mice can enter through gaps as small as a dime. In older homes with stone foundations and wood framing, entry points are often around utility penetrations, chimney bases, and garage door frames. A rodent problem caught by a buyer's inspector during inspection is a serious negotiating issue. Find it first.

4 A Musty, Earthy, or Oily Smell in Certain Areas

Different pests produce distinct smells. A musty, earthy odor in a closet, under a bathroom vanity, or in the basement can indicate mice or rats — they leave urine trails along their travel routes and nest in insulation and wall cavities. Cockroaches produce a distinctive oily, slightly sweet smell in heavy infestations. Carpenter ants don't smell much, but a large colony in a water-damaged wall section can produce a faint musty odor from the disturbed wood. If a room or space smells off in a way you've normalized over years of living there, pay attention. A buyer walking in cold will notice it immediately.

5 Small Holes in Fabric, Sweaters, or Stored Clothing

Finding small, irregular holes in wool sweaters, natural-fiber blankets, or stored clothing in closets or cedar chests is a sign of clothes moths or carpet beetles — both common in NJ homes, especially those with older wool carpets or stored natural fiber items. These pests don't threaten the structure, but they're still an issue buyers and inspectors flag. More importantly, they indicate that storage areas in your home have been undisturbed and uninspected for long enough for an infestation to establish itself. That's a maintenance signal buyers read clearly.

6 Sawdust-Like Frass Near Windowsills or Baseboards

Termites and carpenter ants both produce "frass" — fine material that looks like sawdust or coffee grounds — as they tunnel through wood. Termites produce a pellet-like frass that they push out of kick-out holes in infested wood. Carpenter ants push out coarse sawdust-like shavings mixed with insect parts. Finding either near windowsills, door frames, baseboards, or around wood beams is a direct sign of an active infestation that needs professional treatment. Don't sweep it up and forget about it. Have a pest professional assess it before listing.

NJ seller's bottom line: Get a pre-listing WDI (wood-destroying insect) inspection 60–90 days before going to market. Remediate any findings, obtain documentation, and go into your transaction with a clean pest report in hand. It removes a major negotiating lever from the buyer's side and sends the right signal about how the home has been maintained.

What NJ Home Sellers Need to Know About Pest Disclosure

New Jersey sellers are required to disclose known material defects. A past or current pest infestation and any structural damage caused by pests falls under that requirement. If you know about it and don't disclose it, you have potential post-closing liability — and NJ buyers' attorneys look for exactly this kind of thing in due diligence.

The smart play, and the one I walk every seller through, is to find the issue yourself before it goes on the market. Get the inspection, get the treatment if needed, and have documentation ready when buyer's counsel asks. "Yes, we had a termite spot treatment in the garage three years ago, here's the certificate and a current WDI report showing no active infestation" is a very different conversation than "we didn't know."

Buyers in Summit, Chatham, Westfield, and Cranford are sophisticated. They hire inspectors who are thorough. If something is there to find, it gets found. The sellers who come out ahead are the ones who found it first, handled it professionally, and documented it cleanly.

Frequently Asked Questions

What are the early signs of a termite problem in an NJ home?

Early termite signs in a New Jersey home include wood that sounds hollow when tapped (termites eat from the inside out), wood that looks slightly blistered or warped like water damage, small piles of sawdust-like frass near windowsills or baseboard joints, and discarded wings near windows in spring during swarming season. Termites are extremely common in older NJ homes — particularly in Essex, Union, and Morris counties.

How much does a pest problem reduce home value in New Jersey?

An active termite infestation or significant rodent problem discovered during a home inspection in New Jersey typically costs the seller $5,000–$25,000+ in price reductions or remediation concessions, depending on the extent of damage. Some buyers will walk entirely. Treating the problem before listing and providing documentation of treatment is almost always cheaper than negotiating a credit at contract.

Do NJ home sellers need to disclose a pest problem?

Yes. New Jersey sellers are required to disclose known material defects including past or current pest infestations and structural damage caused by pests. Failing to disclose a known termite or rodent problem can result in post-closing legal liability. If you discover a pest issue before listing, remediate it and disclose the treatment history — buyers and their attorneys will find it in inspection anyway.

How long before selling should I get a pest inspection in NJ?

Get a pest inspection 60–90 days before your planned listing date. This gives you time to remediate any findings, obtain treatment certification, and have evidence of a clean bill of health ready for buyers. Many NJ listing agents recommend the pre-listing pest inspection as standard practice — it eliminates a major inspection surprise and positions the home more confidently on the market.

What pests are most common in NJ homes being sold?

The most common pest issues NJ home inspectors find are subterranean termites (particularly in older wood-frame homes throughout Essex, Union, and Morris counties), carpenter ants (common in homes with moisture issues or older wooden decks and siding), mice and rats (especially in homes near wooded areas or with older foundations), and pantry pests. Wood-destroying insect (WDI) reports are standard in most NJ real estate transactions.

11 Things You Can Clean in the Dishwasher, and the Showing-Prep Shortcut NJ Sellers Should Steal

House Outlook’s newsletter this week is about the things a dishwasher can clean that are not dishes. I read lists like that with one question: what does this do for a house that is about to be photographed and shown? Quite a bit, it turns out. If you are listing in Westfield, Cranford or Edison this fall, the dishwasher is the one appliance that cleans the details buyers touch while you are busy with everything else.

The House Outlook idea

Most people picture a dishwasher as a plate-and-glass machine. The newsletter’s point is that the hot water, the detergent and the long cycle will clean plenty of other hard, heat-tolerant things in the house, and that running a load of them is faster than scrubbing each one by hand. I agree, with a seller’s caveat I will get to: the dishwasher is a tool for the week before photos, not a place to put anything you would hate to replace.

The eleven that earn their spot in a listed house

I have organized these by what a buyer or inspector actually notices during a showing in Summit or Scotch Plains, because that is the only reason to spend the time.

  1. Range hood grease filters. Buyers look at the stove. The metal mesh above it is either clean or coated, and coated reads as neglect. Top rack, hot cycle, no heated dry.
  2. Stove burner grates and knobs. Enameled cast iron grates and plastic knobs come out clean. Check the manual first; some grates are hand wash only, and bare cast iron never goes in.
  3. The microwave turntable. The first thing a buyer sees when they open the microwave door, and they do open it.
  4. Refrigerator shelves and drawer bins. Buyers open the fridge too. Glass shelves and plastic bins on the top rack, after they have come to room temperature so the glass does not crack.
  5. Sink strainers and drain stoppers. A clean, dry sink is the whole point of the kitchen details post, and the strainer is the part people forget.
  6. Dish brushes, sponges and scrubbers. Run them every load during showing week so the sink area never smells.
  7. Pet bowls and mats. Stainless and silicone, not plastic that has gone cloudy. Odor is the issue; see the pets and showings guide.
  8. Toothbrush holders, soap dishes and bathroom cups. Ceramic and plastic, top rack. These are the germ magnets buyers notice without saying so.
  9. Plastic and painted-metal register covers. Dusty supply registers make buyers ask about the HVAC. Unscrew, wash, dry fully before reinstalling.
  10. Light switch plates and outlet covers. Plastic only, screws removed, and nothing with a dimmer or electronics attached. A grimy switch plate at the kitchen entry is the first surface a buyer touches.
  11. Kids’ hard plastic toys and bins. Mesh bag, top rack. Clean toys in a lidded bin photograph as a playroom instead of a mess.

Jorge’s tip: After the grease loads, run the dishwasher empty on its hottest cycle with a cleaner, and pull and rinse the filter at the bottom. Buyers open dishwashers, and an inspector will run it. The dishwasher cleaning post covers that step, and it matters more after you have put a hood filter through it.

What never goes in, and why a seller should care

Wood of any kind, including cutting boards and knife blocks. Bare cast iron, carbon steel and nonstick pans. Brass, bronze or plated cabinet hardware, which will spot and strip. Crystal, anything with a painted or printed finish, anything with adhesive, and anything with a cord. The seller’s reason is simple: a warped board or a stripped cabinet pull is a repair you did not need the week before photos. If you are not sure, hand wash it. Working it into showing week

One load an evening, the week before the photographer comes. Monday is the stove: hood filters, grates, knobs, turntable. Tuesday is the refrigerator, after you have thinned out what is in it. Wednesday is the bathrooms. Thursday is registers and switch plates, room by room, with the screws in a labeled cup. Friday is the empty hot cycle. By the weekend every hard surface a buyer touches has been through hot water, and you spent the time on the parts of the kitchen a dishwasher cannot reach.

Two rules once the house is live. Never run the dishwasher during a showing; the noise and the steam are a distraction in a Hoboken condo and a question mark in a Chatham colonial. And empty it before every appointment, because buyers open it, and a full rack says the owners were not expecting company.

For buyers: what the dishwasher tells you

Open it. A clean filter, a dry gasket and no odor tell you the kitchen has been maintained. Then let your inspector run a full cycle; a dishwasher that leaks at the door or will not drain is a small repair, but it belongs on the list before attorney review ends, not after closing. What this does for the sale

None of this changes an appraisal. It changes the twenty small impressions a buyer collects walking through a kitchen in Union or Morris County, and those impressions decide whether they come back for a second look. A clean hood filter is not a selling point anyone mentions. A greasy one is a flaw everyone remembers.

Questions about dishwasher cleaning and showing prep

Can I really put range hood filters and stove grates in the dishwasher?

Metal mesh hood filters almost always can, on the top rack with a hot cycle and no heated dry. Enameled grates and plastic knobs usually can as well, but check the manual, because some grates are hand wash only and bare cast iron never goes in. When in doubt, hand wash; a damaged part is a repair you do not need before photos.

Why do refrigerator bins and the microwave turntable matter to a buyer?

Because buyers open the refrigerator and the microwave during showings, and inspectors test both. A clean interior reads as a maintained kitchen. Let glass shelves reach room temperature before they go in the dishwasher so they do not crack, and dry everything before it goes back.

What should never go in the dishwasher before listing?

Wood, bare cast iron, carbon steel, nonstick cookware, brass or plated cabinet hardware, crystal, anything painted or printed, anything with adhesive, and anything with a cord. Each of those becomes a replacement or a repair the week you least want one.

Should I run the dishwasher during a showing so the kitchen looks lived in?

No. Noise and steam distract buyers and raise questions. Run loads in the evening, empty the machine before every appointment, and leave the door closed. A full rack tells buyers the owners were not expecting company.

What should a buyer check on the dishwasher at a NJ showing?

Open it and look at the filter, the gasket and the interior, and notice whether it smells. Then have your inspector run a full cycle to check for leaks and drainage. A dishwasher problem is a small repair, but it belongs in the inspection negotiation rather than on your list after closing.

Choosing White Paint Before Listing an NJ Home

Test the color against the room you have before buying paint for the whole house.

Discuss Your Home’s Value Talk to Jorge
Written by Jorge Ramirez, NJ real estate license #1754604 · Keller Williams Premier Properties
Published: September 12, 2026 · Updated: September 15, 2026

Decide whether the room needs paint

Start with condition. Identify marks, damage and areas where a repair needs finishing. A sound existing color may not need replacement simply because you are selling. Compare a touch-up, one-room repaint and broader repaint by scope, disruption and written cost; this guide does not predict a resale return.

Identify the existing product and finish if possible. A color name alone does not guarantee that a touch-up will match an older coating. Ask the paint supplier about the surface and product before committing.

Compare samples with fixed materials

Place samples near the floor, counters, tile and trim that will remain. Evaluate one wall area at a time with the surrounding finishes visible. A small isolated swatch cannot show every interaction in a furnished room.

Benjamin Moore lists White Dove OC-17, Chantilly Lace OC-65 and Swiss Coffee OC-45 among its white paint options. These are examples to compare, not a ranked recommendation or a promise of how they will look in your home. Manufacturer’s white-color guide.

Look at the sample more than once

Check samples in daylight and under the lamps you normally use, at different times. The manufacturer recommends reviewing color in the room’s actual lighting; movable sample boards can help compare locations. Follow the sample product’s directions, and remember that a color sample is not necessarily a demonstration of the final finish. Benjamin Moore: choosing paint samples.

  • Label each sample with the brand, color and code.
  • Record how it looks beside the fixed finishes in each room.
  • Check both brighter and shaded areas before choosing.
  • Save the final product, sheen and color information for future maintenance.

Set the preparation scope before work begins

Do not conceal moisture damage or an unresolved surface problem with paint. Identify the cause and get an appropriate repair assessment. For a home built before 1978, painted surfaces may contain lead. Before disturbing them, consult EPA’s lead-safe renovation guidance and consider a lead-safe certified contractor.

Request a quote specifying rooms, preparation, products, coats as appropriate to the product and surface, protection of belongings and cleanup. Check the manufacturer’s drying and ventilation instructions. A listing deadline should not replace those instructions.

Photograph the finished room honestly

Review photographs for accurate color rather than digitally turning every wall bright white. Keep the paint record for the buyer if useful. If the existing finish is in good condition and repainting would strain the preparation budget, discuss other priorities in a seller consultation.

Common questions

What is the best white paint for every NJ home?

There is no single choice established here. Compare samples with the home’s fixed finishes and actual light before selecting a product and color.

Should I repaint every room before selling?

Base the scope on condition, budget and the sale plan. Get a written quote and resolve underlying damage before deciding.

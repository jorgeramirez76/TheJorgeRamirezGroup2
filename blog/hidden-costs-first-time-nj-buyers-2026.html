Hidden Costs First-Time NJ Homebuyers Don't Budget For

Published  ·  August 2026

Short answer

Beyond the down payment and mortgage, NJ first-time buyers should budget for: closing costs (2–3.5% of purchase price), attorney fees ($1,200–$2,500), home inspection and related specialists ($500–$1,500), property taxes (often $10,000–$20,000+ per year in commuter towns), Mansion Tax (1% on homes over $1M), flood zone insurance if applicable, first-year maintenance reserve (1% of purchase price), and moving costs. Failing to plan for these turns a manageable purchase into a financial shock.

NJ has some of the highest property taxes and transaction costs in the country. Every first-time buyer deserves an honest rundown of what homeownership actually costs here — before they're under contract.

Why NJ Is Different From Other States

Buying a home in New Jersey is not the same as buying a home in North Carolina, Texas, or Florida. NJ has the highest average property taxes in the country. It has mandatory attorney representation in transactions. It has a Mansion Tax that triggers at $1 million. It has a real estate market where many towns require specific insurance products — flood insurance in particular — that buyers from lower-risk states have never thought about.

I have sat with first-time buyers who had their down payment ready and their mortgage pre-approved, and watched the reality of NJ transaction costs land on them like a second mortgage. It is not a comfortable conversation to have mid-contract. So I am having it here, before you start touring homes.

These are the costs NJ first-time buyers routinely underestimate. Every number below is a real 2026 figure from the markets I sell in — Union, Essex, Morris, Middlesex, Hudson, and Somerset counties.

1. NJ Property Taxes: The Biggest Ongoing Surprise

New Jersey has the highest property tax rate in the country by several measures. The statewide average tax bill is approximately $10,000–$11,000 per year. In the NJ commuter towns that first-time buyers are competing for, the numbers are significantly higher.

To put it in practical terms: a home in Summit with a $1,300,000 price tag typically carries $18,000–$22,000 in annual property taxes. That is $1,500–$1,833 per month added to your mortgage payment before your lender even calculates the PITI. A home in Chatham at $1,000,000 might carry $14,000–$16,000 per year. Maplewood at $750,000 runs $12,000–$15,000. Even at the more affordable end — Rahway, Linden, Bloomfield — property taxes run $8,000–$11,000 per year.

Your mortgage lender will include your estimated property taxes in your monthly payment calculation as an escrow. But buyers who first encounter these numbers at pre-approval often recalibrate their budget entirely — or they should. The rule I give every first-time buyer: find out the actual tax bill on any house you're seriously considering before you fall in love with it. The listing's tax figure is usually on the MLS sheet. Verify it on the town's tax assessor portal.

What to budget for property taxes in NJ commuter towns

Under $600K purchase price: Budget $8,000–$12,000 annually ($667–$1,000/month added to mortgage).
$600K–$900K: Budget $11,000–$16,000 annually ($917–$1,333/month).
$900K–$1.3M: Budget $15,000–$22,000 annually ($1,250–$1,833/month).
These ranges vary by town. Always verify the actual tax bill before making an offer.

2. Closing Costs: More Than Most Buyers Plan For

NJ closing costs for buyers run 2–3.5% of the purchase price. On a $700,000 home, you're looking at $14,000–$24,500 in closing costs on top of your down payment. On a $1,000,000 home, that's $20,000–$35,000. Where does it go?

  • Lender origination and processing fees: $3,000–$6,000 depending on your loan amount and lender
  • Title insurance (owner's and lender's policy): $2,500–$5,000
  • Title search and examination: $500–$1,000
  • Attorney fees: $1,200–$2,500 (see below — this is NJ-specific)
  • Home inspection: $400–$700 for a general inspection, more with specialist add-ons
  • Appraisal: $500–$900
  • Prepaid property tax escrow: 2–3 months of taxes collected at closing
  • Homeowner's insurance first-year premium: $1,200–$3,000 depending on home value and coverage
  • Recording fees and state transfer taxes: $500–$2,000

3. Attorney Fees: NJ's Non-Negotiable Transaction Cost

New Jersey is an attorney state. That means both buyers and sellers are typically represented by their own licensed NJ real estate attorneys in every transaction. This is not optional in practice — it is how NJ real estate works, and it protects you in ways that matter. The attorney review period (3 business days after signing the contract) is one of the few moments in a transaction where a buyer can walk away without penalty. Your attorney manages that window.

Expect to pay $1,200–$2,500 for attorney representation in a standard residential NJ transaction. Some attorneys charge flat fees; others bill hourly. Clarify the fee structure upfront. This cost is paid at or before closing. First-time buyers from states where attorneys are not involved in real estate transactions are often surprised by this — plan for it.

4. The NJ Mansion Tax: The Cost That Appears at $1 Million

New Jersey imposes a buyer-paid transfer tax of 1% on all residential properties purchased at $1,000,000 or more. This is called the Mansion Tax. It is a line item on your closing statement that first-time buyers buying in the $900K–$1.1M range sometimes don't see coming.

On a $1,000,000 purchase: $10,000 additional at closing. On a $1,200,000 purchase: $12,000. This is on top of all other closing costs and is due at closing day. If you're buying in Summit, Chatham, Westfield, Millburn, or Montclair — where the median home price is above or near $1 million — budget for this from the start.

5. Flood Zone Insurance: The NJ-Specific Risk Most Buyers Underestimate

Many first-time NJ buyers don't ask about flood zones. I ask for them. In NJ, particularly in Middlesex, Union, Essex, and Hudson counties, a meaningful number of homes sit in FEMA-designated flood zones. If your mortgage lender's flood zone determination comes back positive, you will be required to carry flood insurance as a condition of your loan. This is not optional.

Flood insurance through the National Flood Insurance Program (NFIP) runs $1,000–$3,500 per year for most NJ suburban properties in moderate flood zones. For properties in higher-risk zones, premiums can be significantly more. This is a separate policy from your homeowner's insurance — it does not replace it, it adds to it.

Before you make an offer on a home in a NJ town near a river, reservoir, or low-lying area, ask your agent to pull the FEMA flood map for that specific property. Knowing the flood zone designation before you're under contract lets you factor the insurance cost into your offer and your ongoing budget.

Towns where flood zones appear more frequently: Bound Brook, South Bound Brook, Manville, Linden (near Arthur Kill), portions of Elizabeth, Metuchen low-lying areas, portions of Cranford near the Rahway River, Millburn near the Rahway River floodplain, and portions of Parsippany near the Rockaway River. This is not an exhaustive list — check individual properties on FEMA's Flood Map Service Center.

6. The First-Year Maintenance Reserve

Every home needs maintenance. New homes need less. Older homes need more. NJ's housing stock in the best commuter towns — Summit, Westfield, Chatham, Maplewood — skews toward homes built between 1920 and 1970. These are beautiful, character-rich homes. They are also homes where the water heater, the roof, the gutters, the windows, and the mechanicals are all on their own replacement timelines that you just inherited.

The standard financial planning recommendation is to budget 1% of the purchase price annually for home maintenance and repairs. On a $700,000 home, that is $7,000 per year. On a $1,000,000 home, $10,000. First-time buyers often absorb this in year one without planning for it — the furnace goes in November, the hot water heater in March, the roof in May — and end up carrying high-interest debt to cover expenses they should have anticipated. Keep that reserve liquid from the day you close.

7. Moving Costs: Higher Than You Think in NJ

Local NJ moves for a 3–4 bedroom home typically run $1,500–$3,500 for a local move and $4,000–$10,000+ for a long-distance move. These numbers have risen significantly since 2020. Full-service packing and moving for a furnished home moving from New York City to a NJ suburb can run $6,000–$12,000 depending on volume and distance. Factor this into your closing month budget — you will be spending on inspections, attorneys, closing costs, and moving all in the same 30–45 day window.

The Full Budget Picture for NJ First-Time Buyers

Let me pull all of this together for a buyer purchasing a $750,000 home in Cranford, Fanwood, or Bloomfield in 2026. Your actual costs beyond the down payment and first mortgage payment:

  • Closing costs (2.5%): ~$18,750
  • Attorney fees: ~$1,500
  • Home inspection and specialist inspections: ~$800
  • First-year property taxes (ongoing, built into escrow): ~$11,000/year (~$917/month added to payment)
  • First-year homeowner's insurance: ~$1,800
  • Moving costs: ~$2,500
  • First-year maintenance reserve (1%): ~$7,500
  • Total non-mortgage, non-down-payment costs in Year 1: ~$32,000–$44,000

This is why I tell first-time buyers to have at least 5–8% of the purchase price in liquid reserves beyond their down payment. In NJ, the transaction is more expensive and the ongoing cost structure is more demanding than in most markets. Plan for it. The buyers who are prepared for these numbers are the ones who feel confident on closing day instead of surprised.

Frequently Asked Questions

What are the closing costs when buying a home in New Jersey?

NJ closing costs for buyers typically run 2–3.5% of the purchase price. On a $700,000 home, expect $14,000–$24,500. This includes lender fees, title insurance, attorney fees, home inspection, appraisal, prepaid property taxes, first-year homeowner's insurance, and recording fees. Homes at or above $1M also trigger a 1% Mansion Tax buyer-paid at closing.

How high are property taxes in New Jersey?

New Jersey has the highest average property taxes in the country. The statewide average is $10,000–$11,000 per year in 2026. In commuter towns, taxes are significantly higher: Summit averages $18,000–$22,000, Chatham $14,000–$18,000, Westfield $14,000–$18,000. Build the actual tax bill into your monthly payment before you fall in love with a specific home.

Do I need an attorney to buy a home in New Jersey?

Yes, attorney representation is standard practice in NJ residential transactions — both buyers and sellers hire their own attorneys. Attorney fees run $1,200–$2,500 for a standard residential closing. The attorney manages the 3-business-day attorney review period after contract signing, which is one of the only moments you can exit without penalty.

What is the NJ Mansion Tax and who pays it?

The NJ Mansion Tax is a 1% buyer-paid tax on residential properties purchased at $1,000,000 or more. On a $1,200,000 purchase, that is $12,000 due at closing. If you're buying in Summit, Chatham, Westfield, Millburn, or similar premium towns where medians are at or above $1M, include this in your closing cost estimates.

Does New Jersey have a first-time homebuyer program?

Yes. The NJ Housing and Mortgage Finance Agency (NJHMFA) offers a First-Time Homebuyer Mortgage Program with competitive rates and up to $10,000 in down payment assistance through the DPA program. Income and purchase price limits apply. In more affordable NJ towns — Rahway, Linden, Bloomfield, Metuchen — NJHMFA programs can be a meaningful resource.

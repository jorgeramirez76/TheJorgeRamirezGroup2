Three numbers that are related—but not interchangeable

The New Jersey Division of Taxation separates the assessment, the rate used to calculate the levy, and the bill issued for the parcel. Keeping those concepts separate prevents misleading comparisons.

1

Assessed value

The local assessor assigns taxable value as of the statutory valuation date. It is not automatically the asking price, sale price, or a current appraisal.

2

General tax rate

The town's general tax rate is the multiplier used with taxable assessed value. The State publishes this separately from the effective rate.

3

Property-specific tax bill

The bill is tied to the parcel and billing year. Confirm it with the municipal tax collector, including any adjustments or added assessments.

How to verify taxes for a specific NJ property

Use this sequence before comparing homes, estimating ownership costs, or publishing a property-specific figure.

  1. Identify the parcel. Get the municipality, block, lot, and qualifier from the deed, assessment notice, tax bill, or official municipal record.
  2. Check the State Property Explorer. It can show land and improvement assessment, classification, and prior-year taxes. The State notes that ownership changes after the annual filing may not yet be reflected.
  3. Confirm the current record locally. Ask the municipal tax assessor about the property record card and assessment. Ask the municipal tax collector for the current bill, payment status, and any updated billing information.
  4. Use the correct rate source. The Division of Taxation publishes general and effective rates by county and municipality. The general rate—not the effective rate—is the rate used to compute a bill.
  5. Check for property-specific changes. Ask whether a recent improvement, subdivision, exemption change, added assessment, or omitted assessment could affect the record.
For a purchase: a lender's escrow estimate, a listing field, or a prior owner's payment is not a substitute for the municipality's current parcel record. Review the current official information with the appropriate real estate, lending, tax, and legal professionals.

General rate, effective rate, and equalization

Each figure has a different job. The official statistical page is the right place to select the applicable year and municipality.

Before considering an assessment appeal

New Jersey's official A-1 instructions are explicit: only the assessment—not the amount of tax—can be appealed. The taxpayer carries the burden of supporting the requested taxable value.

  1. Read the current assessment notice and property record card. Check the parcel details and the valuation date that applies to the tax year.
  2. Review the current official instructions. Use the County Board of Taxation and the State's current A-1 petition and comparable-sales form; do not reuse an old packet.
  3. Confirm the deadline and filing method. County calendars, bulk-mailing dates, revaluation status, notice dates, office hours, and electronic-filing procedures can affect what applies.
  4. Prepare relevant evidence. The current form explains acceptable comparable-sales information, service requirements, fees, and hearing procedures. An appeal outcome is never guaranteed.

Deadline checkpoint: verify before relying on a date

The State's current A-1 instructions describe an April 1 deadline—or 45 days from completion of the assessment-notice bulk mailing, whichever is later—for counties on the traditional calendar. They describe May 1 where a municipal-wide revaluation or reassessment was implemented.

For Burlington, Gloucester, and Monmouth Counties, the instructions describe January 15—or 45 days from the bulk mailing, whichever is later. A change-of-assessment notice can also carry a separate 45-day period.

Do not rely on this page for a filing deadline. Verify the deadline directly with the county board, check the current notice and official form, and obtain legal or tax advice when needed.

New Jersey property tax FAQ

What is the difference between assessed value, tax rate, and tax bill?

Assessed value is the local assessor's taxable value for a parcel. The general tax rate is the multiplier used with that assessed value. The property-specific tax bill is the amount billed for that parcel and should be confirmed with the municipal tax collector.

Where can I find a current New Jersey property tax bill?

Start with the municipality's tax collector or official tax-payment portal. The State Property Explorer can help identify parcel information and prior-year taxes, but the State warns that ownership changes after the annual filing may not yet appear.

Can I appeal the amount of my New Jersey property tax bill?

The State's appeal form says only the assessment—not the amount of tax—can be appealed. An owner must support a claim about the property's taxable value under the applicable standard and county procedure.

What is the property tax appeal deadline in New Jersey?

Deadlines depend on the county calendar, the assessment notice, and whether a municipality completed a revaluation or reassessment. Verify the deadline directly with the county board and use the current official form before filing.

Should I use the effective tax rate to calculate a parcel's bill?

No. The Division of Taxation says the effective tax rate is a statistical comparison measure and is not used to compute a tax bill. Use the applicable general tax rate and current assessed value, then confirm the actual bill with the municipality.

Where should I check New Jersey property tax relief programs?

Use the Division of Taxation's official Property Tax Relief portal. Program funding, eligibility, application rules, and deadlines can change, so rely on the current State page rather than an undated summary.

Why Curb Appeal Matters More Than Ever in NJ

I've been selling homes in Union, Essex, Morris, Middlesex, and Hudson counties for over 15 years. And one thing I've seen never changes: the first photo determines whether a buyer schedules a showing. In 2026, with buyers starting their search on Zillow, Realtor.com, and Google before they ever call an agent, your exterior is your first showing.

I've watched identical floor plans sell $15,000–$20,000 apart based entirely on exterior presentation. Same school district. Same square footage. Different first impression. The seller who spent $1,200 on curb appeal got the higher number. The seller who didn't lost the equivalent of two years of HOA dues in the first five minutes of the buyer's decision.

The good news: you don't need a full exterior renovation. You need the right six projects, done well, before the photographer shows up.

The NJ listing math: In Summit, Westfield, Chatham, and Maplewood — the towns I work in most — well-presented homes are going to contract in 7–14 days with multiple offers. Poorly presented homes sit 45–60 days and take price cuts. Curb appeal is the difference between those two timelines.

The 6 Curb Appeal Projects Worth Your Money

1. Power Wash Everything

Cost: $150–$400 ROI: Exceptional

This is the single most underrated presale project in NJ real estate. Fifteen years of black mold streaks on a vinyl exterior. An algae-green driveway. A walkway that looks gray when it should look white. Power washing fixes all of it for under $400.

The driveway, front walkway, front steps, exterior siding, and the porch floor — all of it needs to be hit before the photographer comes. A professional power washing company in Union or Morris County typically runs $200–$350 for the full exterior package. Do it yourself for $40 if you rent the equipment, but pay attention to siding type — some older vinyl and wood siding can be damaged by too much pressure.

This is the project where sellers consistently underestimate the before-and-after gap. The listing photos will look like a different house. In NJ's market, that matters more than any single interior upgrade at this price point.

2. Repaint or Replace the Front Door

Cost: $125–$350 ROI: High

The front door is the focal point of every exterior listing photo. Every photographer shoots it. Every buyer looks at it. And in NJ's older housing stock — Colonials, Tudors, and center-halls that were built in the 1940s through 1970s — the original door color is usually whatever the previous owner chose in 2003 and never updated.

I recommend three colors: navy blue, charcoal black, or deep red. These photograph cleanly, feel welcoming, and appeal to the broadest buyer pool across every NJ commuter town I work in. A fresh coat of exterior paint from a professional painter runs $125–$250 for the door alone. New hardware while you're at it — a brushed nickel or matte black handle set costs $80–$150 at Home Depot and makes the whole entry feel intentional.

Avoid trendy colors. Purple, bright yellow, and coral can look great on Instagram and terrible in a rain-soaked listing photo on a gray November morning in Morris County. Classic colors win every time in this market.

3. Landscape the Front: Mow, Edge, Mulch, Plant

Cost: $200–$600 ROI: High

A manicured lawn signals something important to buyers: this home has been taken care of. It's not about perfect landscaping — it's about the absence of neglect. Overgrown shrubs, bare mulch beds, patchy grass, and weeds climbing through the front walkway tell buyers that deferred maintenance is the theme. They carry that assumption into the house with them.

The minimum: mow the lawn, edge the walkway and driveway, pull visible weeds, and put down fresh mulch in all front beds. Mulch is transformative. It costs $40–$80 for a yard delivered in NJ, and it makes beds look intentional instead of abandoned. Add two or three seasonal planters with color at the front steps. In July, that's black-eyed Susans, petunias, or geraniums. Budget $50–$100 for plants at a local nursery in Summit, Westfield, or Morristown.

If your shrubs are covering the first-floor windows, cut them back. Buyers need to see the house. Overgrown foundation plantings add age and subtract light — neither helps your sale price.

4. Update the Exterior Light Fixtures

Cost: $150–$450 ROI: Good

Brass fixtures from 1988. A half-broken porch light with a yellowed plastic globe. This is the thing sellers overlook and buyers notice immediately — often without consciously registering it. Old, mismatched, or broken exterior lighting makes a home feel forgotten.

New matte black or brushed nickel fixtures are available at Home Depot and Lowe's for $50–$120 each. Replace the porch light, any garage-mounted fixtures, and any path or entry lights. The whole project runs $150–$450 for parts and a few hours of work. If you're not comfortable with electrical, an electrician in NJ charges $75–$125 an hour and this is a 1–2 hour job per fixture.

Coordinated exterior lighting doesn't just look better in daylight photos — it photographs well in twilight shots, which are one of the most clicked listing photo types in NJ's high-end market.

5. New House Numbers, Mailbox, and Door Hardware

Cost: $75–$200 ROI: Very High (for cost)

This is the cheapest upgrade on this list and one of the most impactful per dollar. Faded or missing house numbers. A mailbox that leans or is starting to rust. A door knocker from a previous decade. These small details send a signal about how the property has been maintained.

New brushed nickel or matte black house numbers cost $30–$60 at Home Depot. A new post mailbox runs $60–$150 depending on style. Match the finish across all metal exterior elements — numbers, mailbox, door hardware, and light fixtures — and the whole exterior starts to feel cohesive. That cohesion photographs well and reads as "move-in ready" to buyers.

I've had buyers comment specifically on these details in feedback. Nobody comments on the things they don't notice. They only notice when something is wrong.

6. Clean or Paint the Exterior (Targeted Touch-Ups)

Cost: $300–$2,500 depending on scope ROI: Good to Excellent

Full exterior paint is one of the highest-dollar curb appeal projects, but it's not always necessary. The first question is: does the paint look bad in photos, or just in person? NJ listing photographers are skilled at compensating for mild fading. But genuinely peeling, chipping, or dramatically faded paint photographs badly regardless of who shoots it.

If the home needs paint, do it. A professional exterior paint job on a 2,000–2,500 sq ft Colonial in Union or Morris County runs $3,500–$6,500 for the full exterior. If you're tight on budget, prioritize the front elevation — what the camera sees. Touch up the trim, shutters, and fascia boards at minimum. Shutters in particular are frequently overlooked and frequently peeling. New shutters can be purchased and installed for $300–$600 and make a front elevation look sharp in photos.

Choose neutral, timeless colors. Greige, warm white, soft gray, and sage green are all selling well in NJ's 2026 market. Stay away from color combinations that felt trendy in 2018 — buyers see dated color as deferred taste, which bleeds into their offer price.

The Projects That Don't Pay Off (Save Your Money)

As important as knowing what to do: knowing what to skip.

Full driveway replacement: Power washing fixes 90% of driveway appearance problems. Asphalt resurfacing or replacement runs $2,000–$8,000 and almost never returns dollar-for-dollar. If the driveway is structurally compromised, disclose it and price accordingly. Buyers see through a fresh surface over a bad base — it becomes an inspection issue.

Inground pool installation: Not a presale project. Ever. A pool costs $60,000–$100,000+ in NJ and narrows your buyer pool significantly because it adds insurance costs and maintenance obligations. Pools add value in certain markets and price ranges but not on a 90-day timeline before closing.

New front walkway when the old one is just dirty: Pressure wash it first. A clean concrete walkway is fine. A new paver walkway at $3,000–$6,000 is a nice upgrade, but not one that adds its cost to your sale price in most NJ markets.

Elaborate landscape redesign: Buyers want maintenance, not theater. A full landscape redesign at $5,000–$15,000 is not a presale investment. Clean, edged, mulched, and trimmed is the goal. Plants from 2026 at a nursery are indistinguishable from plants installed last fall in listing photos.

Timing: When to Do Curb Appeal Work Before Listing

The sequence matters. Here's how I walk sellers through the timeline:

  • 6–8 weeks before listing: Decision point on exterior paint, shutters, major landscape work. Schedule contractors early — good painters in Union and Morris County book 3–5 weeks out in the spring and summer market.
  • 2–3 weeks before listing: Power washing, new fixtures, house numbers, mailbox, front door paint. These can all be done quickly but need time to dry and cure before photography.
  • 1 week before listing: Final lawn mow, edge, mulch top-off, planter installation. Everything fresh for the photographer.
  • Day before photography: Walk the exterior with me. I'll spot the things you've stopped seeing because you've lived there — the garden hose coiled on the side of the house, the extension cord running to the garage, the recycling bin visible from the street. These are the things buyers see.

Frequently Asked Questions: Curb Appeal and NJ Home Sales

What curb appeal projects add the most value when selling a home in NJ?

In New Jersey's competitive market, the highest-ROI curb appeal projects are: power washing the driveway and exterior ($150–$400), repainting the front door ($125–$250), fresh mulch and edging ($200–$500), and new exterior light fixtures ($150–$400). Together these cost under $1,500 and routinely push offers up $10,000–$25,000 in Union, Essex, and Morris county markets.

Does curb appeal really affect sale price in New Jersey?

Yes, significantly. Studies consistently show curb appeal accounts for 7–14% of a home's perceived value. In NJ commuter towns where buyers scroll listings online first, a great hero photo can mean 40% more clicks — which translates directly to more showings and stronger offers. I've watched identical floor plans sell $15,000 apart based entirely on exterior presentation.

What is the best front door color to sell a home in NJ?

In NJ's suburban markets, classic colors outperform trendy ones. Navy blue, charcoal black, and deep red consistently photograph well and appeal to the broadest buyer pool. Avoid purple, orange, or ultra-bright colors — they photograph poorly and narrow your buyer pool. The goal is a door that looks intentional and welcoming in the listing photo on a gray NJ morning.

How much should I spend on curb appeal before selling my NJ home?

For most NJ homes in the $600K–$1.5M range, a curb appeal budget of $1,000–$3,000 covers the highest-impact items. Power wash ($200–$400), front door paint ($150–$250), mulch and edging ($300–$600), light fixtures ($200–$400), and new house numbers ($50–$150). Beyond $3,000, you're into diminishing returns unless the driveway needs repaving or the exterior paint is genuinely failing.

Should I replace my driveway before selling my NJ home?

Not unless it's severely cracked or heaved. Power washing cures 90% of driveway appearance problems for under $300. Asphalt resurfacing rarely returns dollar-for-dollar in NJ's market. If the driveway is structurally bad, disclose it and price accordingly — buyers will use it against you in inspection negotiations regardless of how new the surface looks.

Trash Can Odors Before a Showing: The Afternoon Fix for NJ Sellers

House Outlook’s newsletter this week opened with a home hack for garbage odors that stay in the bin after the bag is gone. I read it as a listing agent, because the kitchen trash can is the one smell a buyer meets before they see a single countertop. Here is the fix I give sellers, what keeps it working while the house is listed, and when the smell is not the trash at all.

The tip House Outlook sent this week

This week’s House Outlook newsletter led with a short piece on persistent garbage odors, the kind that linger in the can even after you have taken the bag to the curb. Their point is that the smell lives in the bin itself, not the bag, and that a simple treatment left to work for a few hours clears it. That matches what I see in listing prep every week.

Why a trash can smell costs a seller more than it should

Buyers smell a house before they see it. In my experience a faint sour note at the kitchen doorway does more damage than a scuffed floor, because the buyer cannot locate it and starts wondering what else is hiding. In a Westfield or Summit colonial that means a mental note about the whole kitchen. In a Hoboken or Jersey City condo, where the can sits a few feet from the living room, it colors the entire showing. The fix costs nothing but an afternoon, which is why I put it on every pre-listing checklist.

The afternoon fix, step by step

  1. Take the can outside and wash it properly. Hot water, dish soap and a splash of white vinegar. Scrub the bottom, the rim and the underside of the lid, including the hinge, where drips collect and nobody looks.
  2. Let it dry completely in the sun. A damp bin is a smelly bin. Give it a few hours; UV light helps and the drying is what most people skip.
  3. Deodorize the empty can. Spread a generous layer of baking soda across the bottom and let it sit for several hours, then wipe or vacuum it out. For a plastic can that has absorbed odor, repeat once. This is the slow, boring version of the hack, and it works.
  4. Line the bottom under the bag. A few sheets of newspaper or paper towel under the liner catch the drips that cause the next smell. Swap them weekly while the house is listed.
  5. Fix the lid and the location. A lid that closes fully, and a spot away from the dishwasher and oven, where heat cooks whatever is inside. If the can lives in a cabinet, wipe the cabinet floor too.

Jorge’s tip: On showing days the bag goes out before you leave, every time, even half full. Empty the small bathroom and office cans as well. In a Hoboken or Jersey City building, walk the bag to the chute room before the appointment rather than after. A buyer should open the cabinet, see a clean liner and smell nothing.

Keeping it gone while the house is listed

Once the can is clean, a two-minute routine holds it. Bag out twice a week, a quick wipe of the rim with the counter spray you already use, and a fresh pinch of baking soda under the new liner. If you have pets, the pets and showings guide covers the litter and food-bowl smells that pile onto a trash odor, and the cooking-while-listed guide explains which dinners to skip the night before an appointment.

When the smell is not the trash

If the kitchen still smells after the can is clean and dry, the trash was never the source. The usual culprits are a garbage disposal that needs a scrub, a slow drain, a dishwasher filter, or something that has been leaking under or behind an appliance for months. The slow drain guide and the under-appliance guide walk through each one. Do not mask it with a plug-in scent; buyers in Cranford, Scotch Plains and Montclair read strong air freshener as a cover-up, and a home inspector will find the real cause anyway.

Questions about kitchen odors, showings and selling a NJ home

Does a trash can smell really affect a home sale?

In my experience, yes. Buyers notice odor before anything visual, and a smell they cannot place makes them suspicious of the whole kitchen. It will not change an appraisal, but it changes how carefully a buyer looks at everything else and how they remember the house afterward.

What is the fastest way to get odor out of a plastic trash can?

Wash it with hot water, dish soap and a little white vinegar, dry it fully in the sun, then leave a thick layer of baking soda in the bottom for several hours before wiping it out. If the plastic has absorbed the smell, repeat the baking soda step once more.

Should I buy a new trash can before listing?

Only if the old one is cracked, stained or missing a lid that closes. A clean, dry can with a tight lid and a liner looks the same in a showing as a new one. Spend the money on a can that fits inside a cabinet if yours currently sits in the open.

Where should the kitchen trash can be during showings?

Out of sight if possible, inside a cabinet or pantry, with the bag taken out before every appointment. If it has to stay visible, keep it against a wall away from the oven and dishwasher, lid closed, liner fresh.

What if the kitchen still smells after I clean the trash can?

Then the can was not the source. Check the garbage disposal, the sink drain, the dishwasher filter and the floor under and behind the refrigerator and dishwasher for an old leak. A persistent smell belongs on your repair list, not under an air freshener.

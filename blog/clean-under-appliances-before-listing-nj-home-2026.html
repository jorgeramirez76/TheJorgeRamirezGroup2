The Dust Under Your Appliances: The Pre-Listing Cleaning Step NJ Sellers Skip and Inspectors Do Not

House Outlook’s under-appliance dusting trick is a sock pulled over a bent wire hanger. It is a good hack. For a seller, the real lesson is what lives under the refrigerator, the range and the dryer, and who is going to find it first.

The hack, and why it is only the start

House Outlook’s trick works: straighten a wire hanger, bend the end to fit the gap under the refrigerator, pull an old sock over it, and sweep. Wire beats plastic because you can shape it to the opening. Microfiber grabs more than cotton. It clears the first few inches of dust and pet hair in a minute, and it is what I tell sellers in Westfield and Scotch Plains to do before every showing while the house is on the market.

Before the listing goes live, though, the hanger is not enough. The appliances have to come out. Not because buyers pull the refrigerator forward during a showing, although some do, but because a home inspector in Summit or Montclair will, and so will the buyer’s moving crew on the day you are supposed to hand over a clean house. What is under there becomes a conversation, and you want it to be a short one.

What is actually under and behind each appliance

  1. The refrigerator. Dust-caked condenser coils, which make the unit run hot and loud. A drip pan that can grow mold and smell. The water line to the ice maker, which is the most common source of a hidden kitchen floor leak. Pull it out, vacuum the coils with a brush attachment, wash the pan and look at the floor.
  2. The range. Grease and crumbs that attract mice and roaches, especially in older Elizabeth, Union and Bloomfield houses. Behind a gas range, check that the flexible connector is not kinked and that the anti-tip bracket is installed; inspectors look for both. Degrease the sides of the cabinets while it is out.
  3. The dishwasher. The kick plate hides a drain line and a small puddle if the door seal has been leaking. Remove the plate, look for staining on the subfloor, and run a cycle before you close it up.
  4. The washer and dryer. Lint under and behind the dryer is a fire hazard, and lint in the vent is what inspectors flag most often. Pull the dryer, vacuum the cabinet and the duct, and check that the vent hood outside opens. The dryer vent guide covers it in detail.
  5. The microwave and the hood. The grease filter above the range is the one most buyers open. Soak it in hot water and dish soap, or replace it. Both are cheap.

Jorge’s tip: Put a towel down and walk the refrigerator out on its wheels with a helper, then photograph the floor before you clean it. Sellers in Cranford and Chatham have found old water damage from the ice-maker line this way, and finding it yourself means you can fix it and disclose it on your terms instead of hearing about it from the buyer’s inspector two weeks before closing.

Why this matters more when the house is for sale

Three reasons. First, odor. A sour drip pan or old grease under a range is a smell buyers notice at the door and cannot name, and my guide to home odors that kill sales explains how that lands. Second, pests. Crumbs under the stove are the first thing a pest inspector in Morristown or Millburn looks for, and the pest warning signs guide lists what else they check. Third, disclosure. The floor under an appliance is where a hidden leak shows up first, and in New Jersey a known material defect has to be disclosed. Finding it before you list is cheaper than finding it in attorney review.

There is also a quieter benefit. Clean coils make the refrigerator run quieter and cooler. Buyers who open the refrigerator during a showing, and most do, judge the appliance by sound and smell. The refrigerator guide covers what else they learn from it.

The pre-listing routine

Do the pull-outs once, two or three weeks before photos, in this order: refrigerator, range, dishwasher kick plate, dryer. Budget an afternoon with a helper. Vacuum, degrease, inspect the floor, fix or note what you find. Then the hanger-and-sock pass under each appliance every few days until closing, and after any showing with kids or dogs. If you are listing an older house in Maplewood or Edison with appliances that have not moved in a decade, do the pull-outs before the pre-listing inspection so the report reflects the house you are actually selling.

For buyers: pull the refrigerator out

During the inspection in Hoboken, Jersey City or Woodbridge, ask the inspector to pull the refrigerator and look at the floor and the water line. Open the dishwasher kick plate. Run the dryer and check the outside vent flap. Dust and crumbs are cosmetic; staining, soft flooring and a crushed dryer duct are not, and all three are negotiable or at least disclosable before you close.

Questions about cleaning under appliances before selling a NJ home

Do home inspectors look under and behind appliances?

Many do, especially behind the range for the gas connector and anti-tip bracket, behind the dryer for the vent, and under the dishwasher kick plate for leaks. Pulling the refrigerator varies by inspector, but buyers’ movers will see that floor on closing day either way.

How do I clean under a refrigerator without moving it?

Straighten a wire hanger, bend the end to fit the gap, pull a microfiber cloth or old sock over it and sweep in and out. It handles dust and pet hair between showings. For coils, the drip pan and the floor, the refrigerator has to come out at least once before listing.

Is lint behind the dryer really a fire risk?

Yes. Lint is highly flammable, and a dryer cabinet or duct packed with it is one of the most common findings in a home inspection. Pull the dryer, vacuum the cabinet and the duct, and confirm the exterior vent flap opens when the dryer runs.

What if I find water damage under the refrigerator or dishwasher?

Photograph it, find the source, repair it and keep the receipts. In New Jersey a known material defect has to be disclosed, and a documented repair with an invoice reads very differently to a buyer than a stain discovered during inspection.

How often should I clean under appliances while the house is listed?

Do the full pull-out once, a few weeks before photos. After that, the hanger-and-cloth pass under each appliance every few days and after showings with children or pets is enough to keep the kitchen and laundry room from smelling or attracting pests.

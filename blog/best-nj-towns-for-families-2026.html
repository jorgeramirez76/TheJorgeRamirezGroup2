How to use this guide

A research framework, not a recommendation

A place that fits one buyer may not fit another. Start by writing down the travel windows, housing type, municipal services, property constraints and public resources that matter to your decision. Then verify each item for the exact address instead of relying on a townwide label.

A note about the legacy search phrase

People who search for “best NJ towns for families” deserve objective information, not a list that decides where they should live. This updated resource preserves that useful destination while asking every reader to compare official records and personal priorities for themselves.

Equal-housing approach

Housing choices should not be guided by protected characteristics or demographic proxies. The U.S. Department of Housing and Urban Development explains federal rights and obligations, and the New Jersey Division on Civil Rights explains state protections and steering. This page therefore uses neutral, public sources and leaves the decision to the reader.

Use the same categories

Six objective checks for every address

Municipal identity

Match the deed, tax record and mailing address to the legal municipality. Similar place names can point to different governments, assessors and zoning rules.

Public-school research

Ask the responsible district to confirm current assignment and registration for the address. Read the NJ School Performance Reports with their methodology and reporting-year notes.

Transit research

Use the NJ TRANSIT trip planner for the dates and times that matter. Check transfers, service alerts and walking or driving connections instead of assuming a fixed trip.

Station and parking details

Use the official station and parking search, then confirm permit rules, accessibility and availability with the named operator.

Property tax and assessment

Read the municipality’s current parcel record together with the state’s local property-tax explanation and year-specific statistics. A townwide figure is not a tax bill for a particular home.

Land, building and civic fit

Review zoning, permits, flood information, survey conditions, public facilities and the route to the places you expect to use. Confirm planned work with the responsible agency and qualified professionals.

Basking Ridge is a community within Bernards Township. Use the township—not the community label—for the assessor, zoning and municipal record. The district and station sources answer different questions.

Chatham Borough and Chatham Township

Address-first decision checklist

Turn town research into a property decision

Before a showing

  • Write down the exact travel windows and routes you need to test.
  • Confirm the legal municipality and collect the current parcel record.
  • Open the district and state reports for the address under review.
  • Map municipal offices, public facilities and routine destinations you expect to use.

Before an offer

  • Review the survey, zoning, permits, flood information and planned-use questions.
  • Ask the district to confirm current school assignment in writing when material.
  • Check the transit itinerary, alerts, accessibility and parking rules again.
  • Compare the current tax record with transaction documents and professional advice.

During due diligence

  • Use qualified inspection, legal, title, insurance and lending professionals as appropriate.
  • Resolve differences among the deed, listing fields, tax record and survey.
  • Ask the responsible agency about open permits, approvals or address-specific constraints.
  • Document source dates because service, assignments and public records can change.

For a seller

  • Confirm the legal municipality and district before publishing listing fields.
  • Organize surveys, permits, approvals, disclosures and tax records.
  • Describe public resources and transit factually without promising future conditions.
  • Link buyers to responsible agencies for facts that require current verification.

Verify current taxes, assessment data, zoning, permits, flood conditions, transit service, parking and school assignment directly for the specific address before relying on them.

Source method

What these links can—and cannot—tell you

Municipal and Census pages establish jurisdiction and offer public data; they do not decide personal fit. District and NJDOE pages support school research; they do not guarantee assignment or future programs. NJ TRANSIT pages support current trip planning; they do not promise future schedules. State and municipal tax files require the correct year and parcel.

Census sources are included for consistent geography and neutral housing or travel research. Do not use protected-class demographic data to direct a housing decision. All source links in this guide were accessed August 26, 2026.

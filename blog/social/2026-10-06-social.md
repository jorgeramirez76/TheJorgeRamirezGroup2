### Facebook Post (100-150 words)

House Outlook's newsletter this week included a roundup of six simple ways to reduce noise in a home without changing how it looks. If you are selling near the train in Westfield, Cranford, Summit or Chatham, or a block off Route 22 in Scotch Plains, that list is a pre-listing checklist.

Here is what most sellers get wrong: they strip the rugs to show off the hardwood, pull the curtains down for light, and then the front room echoes. A buyer standing quietly at the window hears the train twice as loud as you ever did.

Rugs with pads, lined curtains left open, new weatherstripping on the doors, caulk around the street-side windows, a full bookcase on the loud wall, and fix the house's own rattles and squeaks. Cheap, fast, and no white noise machine required.

## Article 2: Staging the Guest Room: What NJ Buyers Want to See in a Spare Bedroom

**Live URL:** https://thejorgeramirezgroup.com/blog/guest-room-staging-spare-bedroom-nj-buyers-2026
**Image:** https://thejorgeramirezgroup.com/images/properties/06-bedroom.webp
**Instagram image:** https://thejorgeramirezgroup.com/images/properties/06-bedroom.webp

### Facebook Post (100-150 words)

House Outlook shared a Real Simple list this week of the essentials for a welcoming guest room: a comfortable bed, a lamp you can reach from the pillow, somewhere to put a suitcase, empty hangers, a clear surface.

Read it as a seller and it is a staging checklist. In most Westfield, Cranford and Scotch Plains colonials the fourth bedroom is the small one that quietly became storage: the treadmill, the holiday bins, the ironing board. Buyers count bedrooms with their eyes, and a room full of boxes does not count.

A plainly made bed, one nightstand and lamp, a closet taken down to a third full, and the storage out of the room. Keep it that way for the whole listing, because the buyers who liked the photos come back to that room on the second visit.

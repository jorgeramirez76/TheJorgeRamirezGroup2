### Facebook Post (100-150 words)

House Outlook's cold-snap advice is simple: keep the thermostat at 55°F or higher, let a faucet drip, open the cabinets under the sinks. Good advice for a house with people in it.

I read it for the houses nobody is living in. Every winter in New Jersey I see vacant listings, estates waiting on paperwork, and sellers who already moved and turned the heat down to save money. One January night in the teens, one pipe in an exterior wall, and the buyer's final walkthrough finds a ceiling on the floor.

The checklist: heat on and left alone, cabinets open, exterior-wall faucets dripping, hoses off and spigots drained, foam on the exposed pipes, a tag on the main shutoff, and someone checking the house if nobody sleeps there. ## Article 2: Mow, Don't Bag: What to Do With Fallen Leaves Before Listing a NJ Home This Fall

**Live URL:** https://thejorgeramirezgroup.com/blog/fallen-leaves-mow-mulch-before-listing-nj-home-sellers-2026
**Image:** https://thejorgeramirezgroup.com/images/properties/08-exterior.webp (crop to 1200×630)
**Instagram image:** https://thejorgeramirezgroup.com/images/properties/08-exterior.webp (crop to 1080×1080)

### Facebook Post (100-150 words)

House Outlook's advice this week: put the leaf blower away and get the mower out. Run over the leaves until the pieces are about the size of a dime and let them feed the lawn. Whole leaves form a mat that smothers the grass; shredded leaves disappear into it.

I agree, and a seller has a different calendar than a gardener. A listing in Westfield or Chatham in late October drops leaves every day for six weeks, and every showing and every photo happens inside that window.

The routine: mow weekly with the bag off, sweep the walk and steps before every showing, keep leaves off the foundation and out of the window wells, clean the gutters after the main drop, follow your town's curb rules exactly, and shoot the exterior photos the day after a cleanup. ## Article 3: 7 Fire Hazards You Should Never Put in the Trash When Clearing Out a NJ Home Before a Sale

**Live URL:** https://thejorgeramirezgroup.com/blog/fire-hazards-never-toss-in-garbage-nj-home-sellers-2026

### Facebook Post (100-150 words)

House Outlook published a list of things that should never go in the garbage because they start fires, in the can, at the curb or in the truck. Lithium-ion batteries and aerosol cans lead it; a garbage truck is a compactor, and a compactor crushes whatever is inside.

I read it as a seller's article. The week before a New Jersey house is listed is when the garage, the basement and the junk drawer get emptied into contractor bags without anyone looking: old phones, half-full spray cans, the grill's propane tank, pool shock, stain-soaked rags, fireplace ash that is still warm.

Every county I work in runs a household hazardous waste program. Put one labeled box by the garage door on day one and write the next collection date on it. Full guide, with where each item goes: thejorgeramirezgroup.com/blog/fire-hazards-never-toss-in-garbage-nj-home-sellers-2026

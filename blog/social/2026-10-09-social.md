**Live URL:** https://thejorgeramirezgroup.com/blog/trash-can-odors-before-showings-nj-home-sellers-2026
**Image:** https://thejorgeramirezgroup.com/images/editorial/nj-home-valuation-review-2026-1280.webp

### Facebook Post (100-150 words)

House Outlook's newsletter this morning led with a hack for garbage odors that stay in the can after the bag is gone. I read it the way I read everything now, as a New Jersey listing agent, and here is the thing: the kitchen trash can is the one smell a buyer meets before they see a single countertop.

The fix takes an afternoon and costs nothing. Wash the can outside with hot water, dish soap and a splash of vinegar. Dry it fully in the sun. Spread a thick layer of baking soda in the bottom and leave it for a few hours. Line under the bag with newspaper. Then bag out before every showing, every time.

If the kitchen still smells after that, the trash was never the source. Check the disposal, the drain and under the appliances before a buyer's inspector does.

**Live URL:** https://thejorgeramirezgroup.com/blog/gutter-guards-fall-nj-home-sellers-buyers-2026
**Image:** https://thejorgeramirezgroup.com/images/blog-hero/families-2026-1280.webp

### Facebook Post (100-150 words)

House Outlook's newsletter this week pushed gutter guards as a fall project. The item was sponsored, but the question under it is real in the towns I work in. Westfield, Montclair, Chatham and Millburn drop a season of oak and maple leaves in about three weeks, and a clogged gutter overflows straight at the foundation.

In my experience the one question that stops a buyer cold is water in the basement, and most of it starts at the roof edge.

My order of operations: clean the gutters, run a hose and watch where the water goes. Fix pitch, extensions and grading first. Then decide on guards based on how many trees are over the roof, get written quotes, and keep the invoice and the warranty. Present them as proof of maintenance, never as a reason for price.

### Facebook Post (100-150 words)

House Outlook's newsletter this week linked to Domino's coverage of Sherwin-Williams' 2027 Color of the Year: a deep, refreshing shade meant to be layered with warm woods and creamy neutrals rather than splashed on every wall.

Every year a color announcement sends a few sellers to the paint store. Here is how I tell them to use it. One room with a door: the dining room, the powder room, the study. Below the chair rail or on a built-in. Paired with warm wood and brass, never with cool gray. Tested on a two-foot square in your actual light, morning and evening.

And where to leave it alone: the whole main floor, the bedrooms, and any kitchen that already needs work. Most of my listings in Westfield, Summit and Montclair still sell on a warm white.

## Post 2: The Cabinet Hardware Trick That Keeps NJ Homes Show-Ready

**Facebook Post (100–150 words):**
Here's something I check on every pre-listing walkthrough in NJ: the kitchen and bathroom hardware. Loose drawer pulls and wobbly cabinet knobs are one of the most common buyer turnoffs — and one of the easiest to fix.

The trick: before you tighten any loose hardware, put one drop of clear nail polish on the screw threads. Drive the screw back in while the polish is still wet. It acts like a light threadlocker and keeps the hardware tight through every showing, every open house, every buyer visit.

While you're at it — are your finishes consistent? Mixed chrome, nickel, and oil-rubbed bronze in the same kitchen is another thing I flag. One consistent finish is a $150–$300 upgrade that buyers notice immediately.

## Post 3: How to Fix Window Screen Tears Before Your NJ Home Showing

**Facebook Post (100–150 words):**
Fall is NJ real estate season — and buyers are walking homes with fresh eyes and open windows. That means window screens are very much visible.

A torn screen might seem trivial, but it's one of those small deferred maintenance signals that buyers connect to bigger questions about the home. The fix for small holes (under 1/8 inch) is literally 5 minutes with a bottle of clear nail polish. Two coats, nearly invisible, stops the tear from spreading.

Larger screens that are badly damaged? Replace them. A full DIY re-screening kit runs $20–$30. Professional replacement in NJ is $40–$80 per window. Either way, it's a small cost compared to what a "deferred maintenance" impression costs you at the negotiating table.

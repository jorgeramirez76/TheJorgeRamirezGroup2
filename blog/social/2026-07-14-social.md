## POST 1: 6 Early Warning Signs of a Pest Problem NJ Homeowners Should Know

### Facebook Post
Here's something I've seen blow up more NJ home deals than almost anything else: a pest inspection report that catches sellers completely off guard.

Termite frass behind the baseboard. Mouse droppings behind the refrigerator. Hollow-sounding wood in the basement window frames. These things don't appear the day before closing — they've been building for months or years. The sellers just didn't know what they were looking at.

The early signs are there if you know where to look:
🐭 Scratching sounds in walls at night (rodents, or carpenter ants in wood)
💩 Droppings in cabinet corners, pantries, or along basement baseboards
🪵 Hollow sound when you tap window frames or baseboards (termites eat from inside out)
🪲 Tiny sawdust piles near wood trim (frass from drywood termites or carpenter ants)
🏚️ Grease marks along baseboards where rodents travel the same paths daily
💧 Moisture problems — damp basements, clogged gutters — that invite everything above

I walk every listing through a pre-market checklist. Sellers who catch and fix pest issues before listing consistently close with fewer credits and cleaner contracts.

## POST 2: Why Your NJ Home Is Always Dusty — And What It Costs at Resale

### Facebook Post
If your NJ home is always dusty no matter how often you clean, your HVAC system is working against you. And that matters more than you think when you're selling.

Here's what's actually happening in most NJ homes:

The HVAC filter hasn't been changed in 6+ months → it's recirculating dust instead of capturing it. The ductwork is 20–30 years old with leaky joints → it's pulling attic dust into your living space. Old carpet → acting as a dust reservoir, releasing particles with every step. Gaps around aging windows and doors → outdoor pollen and particulates coming in continuously.

According to ENERGY STAR, the average home loses 30% of its conditioned air through duct leaks. That air is replaced by whatever's in your attic.

The real estate math: buyers touring a dusty home offer less. They notice it before they even look at the disclosures. A $40 MERV-13 filter and a $150 HVAC tune-up are some of the best pre-listing investments you can make.

## POST 3: 12 Design Mistakes Making Your NJ Home Look Cheaper Than It Is

### Facebook Post
Buyers make up their minds in the first 90 seconds. Here are 12 design mistakes I see NJ sellers make every week that quietly cost them money before a single inspection finding is even on the table:

❌ Accent walls — screams 2009, buyers see a paint project they have to redo
❌ Wrong-sized furniture — makes rooms feel disconnected or cramped
❌ Builder-grade light fixtures — the boob light in every bedroom has got to go
❌ Cluttered countertops — buyers can't picture their own life through your stuff
❌ Cheap/dated cabinet hardware — $10 in new pulls completely changes a kitchen photo
❌ Drastic flooring transitions — laminate to tile to carpet in one lap of the house
❌ Hollow-core interior doors — they sound cheap because they are
❌ Outdated window treatments — vertical blinds in 2026 are a showing filter
❌ DIY paint jobs with visible brush marks and lap lines
❌ Zero curb appeal — buyers drive by before scheduling showings
❌ Personal photos and memorabilia everywhere — declutter aggressively
❌ Overhead lighting only, no layered lighting — makes every room look flat in photos

Most of these fixes cost $200–$500 each. Addressing 4–6 of them before listing consistently puts $5,000–$20,000 more at the closing table.

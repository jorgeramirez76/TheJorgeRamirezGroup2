## POST 1: The Dollar Bill Oven Test — What NJ Home Sellers Need to Know

### Facebook (100-150 words)
Got 30 seconds? Here's a simple test that tells you something important about your NJ home.

Take a dollar bill. Open your oven door, put the bill halfway inside, and close it. Try to pull the bill out.

If it slides right out — no resistance — your oven door gasket is worn. Heat is escaping every time you use it.

Alone, a $20 part doesn't affect your sale price. But a kitchen with deferred maintenance tells buyers a story: *"What else hasn't been kept up?"* And in a competitive NJ market (Summit, Westfield, Chatham, Cranford), that doubt costs you at the negotiating table.

## POST 2: Things in Your NJ Home That Could Be Worth Real Money

### Facebook (100-150 words)
Before you load up for the donation center, take 30 minutes with a specific checklist.

NJ's older homes — Summit Colonials, Westfield Victorians, the Chatham ranch your family has owned for 40 years — frequently hold items people have stopped noticing. Items with real market value.

Sterling silver with a "925" stamp on the bottom. Original oil paintings with a signature in the corner. Hand-cut crystal in original boxes. First editions with a number line that includes "1."

I'm not an appraiser. But I've walked through enough pre-sale home clearouts to know that people routinely donate or trash items worth hundreds — sometimes thousands — of dollars.

Know what you have before you decide where it goes.

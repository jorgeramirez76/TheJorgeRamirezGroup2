## Article 1: Before You Turn On the Heat: The Furnace Check Every NJ Seller and Buyer Should Make This Month

### Facebook Post (100-150 words)

House Outlook’s reminder this week: before the first cold night, replace the furnace filter. Cheapest thing you can do for a heating system, and the one most people skip.

If your house is listed, it matters more. The first time the heat runs this fall will probably be during a showing, and the first person to pull that filter will be the buyer’s inspector. A gray, bowed, year-old filter shapes how they read the rest of the report. A clean one with the date written on the frame does the opposite.

The twenty-minute check: new filter, dated. Run the heat once with the windows cracked so the burnt-dust smell is gone before buyers arrive. Clear three feet around the furnace. Test the CO and smoke alarms. Fresh thermostat batteries. Book a service if it has been more than a year, and keep the receipt. ## Article 2: 12 Things You Should Never Store in a NJ Basement, and What Buyers Think When They Find Them

**Live URL:** https://thejorgeramirezgroup.com/blog/things-never-store-in-basement-nj-home-sellers-2026

**Instagram image:** https://thejorgeramirezgroup.com/images/oil-tank-sweep-nj-home-inspection-2026-1280.webp (crop to 1080×1080)

### Facebook Post (100-150 words)

House Outlook published a list this week of things you should never store in a basement: paper, fabric, food, paint, fuel, electronics on the floor, firewood, wine, irreplaceable documents. Most of it comes down to one fact: basements are cooler, damper and more variable than the rest of the house.

In New Jersey that is not a theory. Nearly every house I sell, from the colonials in Maplewood to the split-levels in Scotch Plains, has a basement, and what is stored down there is the first clue a buyer reads about moisture.

Cardboard is the tell. Buyers and inspectors look at the bottom two inches of every box, because cardboard records water like a tide line. Lidded totes on shelving, a dehumidifier on a drain hose, bright bulbs and clear paths to the furnace, panel and sump. If the basement has taken water, that is a disclosure conversation, not a storage one. Full guide: thejorgeramirezgroup.com/blog/things-never-store-in-basement-nj-home-sellers-2026

## Article 3: What Pro Cleaners Use for a Streak-Free Stovetop Before Listing Photos: A NJ Seller’s Guide

**Live URL:** https://thejorgeramirezgroup.com/blog/streak-free-stovetop-listing-photos-nj-home-sellers-2026
**Image:** https://thejorgeramirezgroup.com/images/nj-fsbo-selling-plan-2026-1280.webp (crop to 1200×630)
**Instagram image:** https://thejorgeramirezgroup.com/images/nj-fsbo-selling-plan-2026-1280.webp (crop to 1080×1080)

### Facebook Post (100-150 words)

House Outlook asked professional cleaners how they get a stovetop streak-free, and the honest answer is that there is no one product. Glass-ceramic, stainless, enamel and gas grates each want a different method, and the wrong one scratches the surface for good.

Why it matters when you are selling: the range sits in the middle of the most-viewed kitchen photo, and a streaky cooktop reads as dirty in a photo even when it is clean in person. Buyers who tour three houses on a Saturday remember the kitchens by their ranges.

Glass: cooktop cleaner, razor scraper nearly flat, two microfiber cloths, never ammonia or a scrub pad. Stainless: dish soap, with the grain, dry with the grain. Grates: soak, soft brush, dry the caps before relighting. Then take everything off it before the photographer comes.

## POST 2: Home Safety Red Flags NJ Buyers Find at Inspection

### Facebook Post (130 words)
NJ home inspections are where deals go to die — and it's almost always a safety item that does it.

The warning signs buyers' inspectors find most often in NJ's older homes:
🔴 Federal Pacific or Zinsco electrical panels (extremely common in pre-1985 homes)
🔴 A fishy smell near outlets or the electrical panel
🔴 Warm light switches or outlets
🔴 Yellow pilot light on gas appliances
🔴 Clogged dryer vent
🔴 Smoke detectors past their 10-year replacement date

When the buyer's inspector finds these, they become credit demands. When YOU find them first and fix them, they become selling points.

Pre-listing inspection: $500. Fixing the top safety items: $2,000–$5,000. Avoided credit demands: $10,000–$20,000.

## POST 3: 8 Renovation Decisions NJ Homeowners Regret

### Facebook Post (140 words)
The renovation decisions NJ sellers regret most — from someone who's walked through these conversations hundreds of times.

The ones I see cost sellers money:
❌ Half-done renovations (new counters, old cabinets — doesn't read as "updated")
❌ Full kitchen gut done for resale (returns 50–65 cents on the dollar in NJ)
❌ Over-improving for the neighborhood ceiling
❌ Renovating to personal taste right before listing
❌ Installing a pool before selling (never pencils out in NJ)
❌ Spending on visible items while ignoring aging HVAC and electrical

The moves that actually work in NJ's market: fresh paint, refinished floors, new light fixtures, updated cabinet faces. Cosmetic, targeted, and priced for the buyer pool — not your taste.

## POST 1: The One Flooring Decision That Makes NJ Homes Feel Larger

### Facebook (100–150 words)
Did you know the flooring in your NJ home might be making it feel 20% smaller than it actually is?

Most NJ homes have 3–4 different flooring materials on the main level — tile in the foyer, hardwood in the living room, laminate in the kitchen — separated by metal threshold strips. Every one of those transitions is a visual stop sign that tells the buyer's brain: this room is over.

Run one consistent material through all connected spaces and everything changes. Your brain processes it as one continuous zone instead of a series of small rooms — and buyers feel more spacious in the home before they even know why.

Before you list, walk your main sightline from the front door and count the transitions. That number is costing you.

## POST 3: The Design Mistake That Makes NJ Rooms Feel Flat

### Facebook (100–150 words)
Here's why some NJ homes feel great when you walk in and others feel forgettable — even at the same price point.

It's not the square footage. It's not even the finishes. It's visual layering. When everything in a room sits at the same height — sofa at 30 inches, coffee table at 18, console at 30 — there's nothing for the eye to do. The room reads as flat and static. Buyers walk through it quickly.

A tall lamp in the corner. Artwork above sofa height. A floor plant that reaches 5 feet. Stacked books on the coffee table. These are the layers that make a room feel composed and alive — that make buyers stop, look around, and imagine living there.

The fix is four layers of visual height:

1️⃣ Ground level — area rug anchoring the furniture grouping
2️⃣ Furniture level — sofa, chairs, consoles (most sellers nail this one)
3️⃣ Eye level — artwork or a mirror above sofa height (most sellers miss this)
4️⃣ Upper level — tall floor lamp, tall plant, shelving that reaches toward the ceiling (almost everyone skips this)

When all four layers are present, the eye travels dynamically through the room and buyers stay longer. Buyers who stay longer write better offers.

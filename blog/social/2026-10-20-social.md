## Article 1: 6 Paint Colors to Avoid in a Small Room Before Listing a NJ Home, and What to Use Instead

**Instagram image:** https://thejorgeramirezgroup.com/images/properties/04-brick-stone.webp (crop to 1080×1080)

### Facebook Post (100-150 words)

House Outlook published a list today of six paint colors that make a small room feel smaller: stark cool white, bright red and electric yellow lead it. I would add charcoal or navy in a one-window room, the grays that turn icy blue under north light, and semi-gloss on any wall.

Why it matters here: most houses I sell in Union and Middlesex County have a bedroom that measures ten by ten or less. The second bedroom in a Cranford cape, the third in a Scotch Plains split, the back room in a Hoboken condo. Buyers do not measure those rooms. They look at the photo and decide whether a crib or a desk fits.

The fix is one warm white through every small room, matte or eggshell, ceiling included. And if the walls are already a clean neutral, leave them alone. Full guide: thejorgeramirezgroup.com/blog/small-room-paint-colors-avoid-nj-home-sellers-2026

## Article 2: 9 Things You Should Never Store in a NJ Attic, and What Buyers and Inspectors See When You Do

**Live URL:** https://thejorgeramirezgroup.com/blog/things-never-store-in-attic-nj-home-sellers-2026
**Image:** https://thejorgeramirezgroup.com/images/properties/02-staircase.webp (crop to 1200×630)
**Instagram image:** https://thejorgeramirezgroup.com/images/properties/02-staircase.webp (crop to 1080×1080)

### Facebook Post (100-150 words)

House Outlook listed nine things that do not belong in an attic: paper, wood furniture, electronics and vinyl records among them, because a summer at 130 degrees and a winter below freezing ruins them all.

I care about the other half of the problem. In a New Jersey sale, the buyer’s inspector goes into the attic. In a 1920s colonial in Westfield or Montclair that means the pull-down ladder. In a cape in Cranford or Union it means the knee-wall doors. The inspector is looking at the roof deck, the bathroom fan vents, the insulation depth and for mouse droppings. Boxes stacked on the joists block all of that, and “unable to inspect” on a report worries buyers more than a repair would.

Clear it, deck it, leave a path to the hatch. Full list: thejorgeramirezgroup.com/blog/things-never-store-in-attic-nj-home-sellers-2026

## Article 3: 9 Forgotten Filters in Your Home: The Cheap Maintenance That Shows Up in a NJ Inspection

**Live URL:** https://thejorgeramirezgroup.com/blog/forgotten-household-filters-clean-replace-nj-home-sellers-2026
**Image:** https://thejorgeramirezgroup.com/images/properties/05-kitchen.webp (crop to 1200×630)
**Instagram image:** https://thejorgeramirezgroup.com/images/properties/05-kitchen.webp (crop to 1080×1080)

### Facebook Post (100-150 words)

House Outlook rounded up nine household filters most people forget: the furnace, range hood, dishwasher, washing machine, vacuum, refrigerator water, dryer vent and humidifier. It reads like a maintenance article. I read it as a selling article.

When a buyer’s inspector in Morristown or Summit opens the furnace door, the filter is the first thing in hand. Clean and dated, they move on. Gray and bowed, the report recommends a service call and the buyer starts asking the age of every system. Buyers in Edison and Woodbridge open the dishwasher at showings and smell the filter before they see it.

Replace the furnace filter and date it, clear the dryer vent to the outside, degrease the hood screens, rinse the dishwasher filter, and put the receipts in a folder on the counter. Full guide: thejorgeramirezgroup.com/blog/forgotten-household-filters-clean-replace-nj-home-sellers-2026

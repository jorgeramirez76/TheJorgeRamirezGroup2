## Article 1: Fresh Flowers for Showings: How to Cut Stems to Fit Any Vase in a NJ Listing

**Live URL:** https://thejorgeramirezgroup.com/blog/fresh-flowers-vase-staging-nj-home-showings-2026
**Image:** https://thejorgeramirezgroup.com/images/properties/07-dining.webp
**Instagram image:** https://thejorgeramirezgroup.com/images/properties/07-dining.webp

### Facebook Post (100-150 words)

A vase of fresh flowers is the cheapest staging move there is, and the one most New Jersey sellers get wrong. House Outlook explained that a good arrangement starts with the vase, then every stem gets cut to a length that suits it, at an angle so it keeps drinking.

The seller’s version: pick the vase first. Tall vase, blooms about one and a half times its height. Low bowl, cut short so the heads sit just above the rim. Strip the leaves below the waterline and change the water every other day.

Then put one arrangement in each of five spots: kitchen island, dining table, entry console, primary nightstand, primary bath. Nothing scented, no lilies on the quartz, and buy them the morning of the photo shoot, not the week before. A wilting vase does more harm than an empty table.

## Article 2: Decluttering Small Spaces Before You Sell: 7 Pro Tricks for NJ Condo and Small-Home Sellers

**Live URL:** https://thejorgeramirezgroup.com/blog/declutter-small-spaces-nj-condo-sellers-2026
**Image:** https://thejorgeramirezgroup.com/images/properties/03-shingle-porch.webp
**Instagram image:** https://thejorgeramirezgroup.com/images/properties/03-shingle-porch.webp

### Facebook Post (100-150 words)

Small homes have fewer surfaces to clean and a bigger clutter problem, because every item is in the photo. House Outlook pointed readers to the professional organizers’ tricks for decluttering small spaces, and they are the right starting point for anyone selling a condo in Hoboken or Jersey City or a two-bedroom in Cranford.

The seller’s edition: declutter by zone, not room. Go vertical, then leave the top third of every shelf empty. Make furniture do two jobs, but do not stuff the ottoman, because buyers open it. Clear every surface to one object. Build a drop zone at the door. Edit closets to two-thirds full. And move a third of what you own out of the home entirely.

One warning from Jorge: do not empty the rooms. An empty small room has no scale. Keep the right-sized furniture and let it prove the room works.

## Article 3: The Dust Under Your Appliances: The Pre-Listing Cleaning Step NJ Sellers Skip and Inspectors Do Not

**Live URL:** https://thejorgeramirezgroup.com/blog/clean-under-appliances-before-listing-nj-home-2026
**Image:** https://thejorgeramirezgroup.com/images/editorial/nj-home-valuation-review-2026-1280.webp
**Instagram image:** https://thejorgeramirezgroup.com/images/editorial/nj-home-valuation-review-2026-1280.webp

### Facebook Post (100-150 words)

House Outlook shared a smart hack this week: straighten a wire hanger, bend the end, pull a sock over it and sweep the dust out from under the refrigerator and stove. It works, and it is what I tell sellers to do between showings.

Before the listing goes live, though, the appliances have to come out. Under the refrigerator: dust-caked coils, a drip pan that smells, and the ice-maker line that causes most hidden kitchen floor leaks. Behind the range: grease and crumbs that attract pests, plus the gas connector and anti-tip bracket inspectors check. Under the dishwasher kick plate: the puddle from a leaking seal. Behind the dryer: lint, which is a fire hazard and the most common inspection finding.

Pull them once, a few weeks before photos. Photograph the floor. Fix what you find and disclose it on your terms, not the buyer’s inspector’s.

## Article 1: Do This Once a Year to Save Your NJ Home From Rot

**URL:** https://thejorgeramirezgroup.com/blog/save-home-from-rot-nj-sellers-2026

---

### Facebook Post

Here's the cheapest annual maintenance task NJ homeowners skip every year — and the one that costs them the most when they sell.

Once a year, walk around your home with a tube of exterior caulk and inspect every place where two materials meet: around windows, door surrounds, where siding meets brick, along siding seams, around every exterior pipe penetration.

NJ winters destroy exterior caulk. Freeze-thaw cycles crack it, shrink it, and pull it away from surfaces. A gap the size of a credit card lets water in. That water gets into your wall cavity. It causes wood rot. It causes mold. By the time a buyer's inspector finds it, you're handing over a $2,000 credit for what would have been a $10 tube of caulk and an afternoon.

What to look for:
✅ Gaps between caulk and window frame (all four sides)
✅ Door surrounds, especially the top and sides
✅ Where trim meets masonry or brick
✅ Around every dryer vent, gas line, and hose bib
✅ Where the foundation meets the sill plate above it
✅ Along horizontal siding seams at corners

Caulk is $8–$12 at any hardware store. This takes 2–3 hours. Do it this spring, every spring.

## Article 2: Why Closing Your Bedroom Door at Night Could Save Your Life

**URL:** https://thejorgeramirezgroup.com/blog/close-bedroom-door-fire-safety-nj-2026

---

### Facebook Post

This is the fire safety tip most NJ homeowners have never heard — and it costs nothing.

Close your bedroom door before you go to sleep.

Here's why this matters, backed by actual fire safety research:

🔥 FEMA reports that 51% of fatal house fires occur between 10pm and 6am — while most people are asleep.

🌡️ The Fire Safety Research Institute tested conditions during actual structure fires. Rooms with open doors reached approximately 1,000°F. Rooms with closed doors stayed around 100°F.

That's a 900-degree temperature difference created by a single interior door.

A closed door also significantly reduces carbon monoxide concentration — giving sleeping occupants more time to wake up, call 911, and escape before conditions become unsurvivable.

This matters especially in NJ. A significant percentage of homes in Union, Essex, Morris, and Middlesex counties were built before 1980 — before modern fire-resistant building standards. Those homes have open stairwells, older electrical systems, and construction that doesn't slow fire the way newer homes do.

Two seconds. Every night. Could save your family.

## Article 3: 10 Lifesaving Home Safety Tips NJ Homeowners Probably Don't Know

**URL:** https://thejorgeramirezgroup.com/blog/lifesaving-home-safety-tips-nj-2026

---

### Facebook Post

As a full-time NJ real estate agent since 2017, I've seen what goes wrong in houses. Not just the cosmetic stuff — the stuff that's actually dangerous. Here are the lesser-known safety hazards I see hiding in plain sight in NJ homes:

🚫 **Water on a grease fire** — NEVER do it. Use a lid to smother it. Water causes a burning oil explosion.

🔵 **Yellow pilot light on your furnace or water heater** — should be blue. Yellow means incomplete combustion and possible carbon monoxide. Leave the house and call your gas utility.

💧 **Rotten egg smell in tap water** — stop drinking it. Hydrogen sulfide or bacteria. Get it tested before using it again.

🧴 **Mixing bleach + ammonia** — produces chloramine gas. Mixing bleach + vinegar = chlorine gas. Never use these in the same space.

🚪 **Closed bedroom door at night** — 900°F temperature difference in a fire. FEMA says 51% of fatal fires happen while you sleep.

🔌 **GFCI outlets fail silently** — press the Test button monthly. If it doesn't cut power, replace it before it's near water.

🌀 **Dryer vents** — lint accumulation in the duct is a fire hazard. Have it cleaned professionally every 1–2 years.

🏠 **Annual exterior caulk walk** — NJ freeze-thaw cycles destroy caulk. Water gets in. Wood rot follows.

⚡ **Gas and water shutoffs** — know where they are BEFORE an emergency. Show every adult in your household.

### Facebook Post (100-150 words)

House Outlook’s newsletter this week led with the one habit that keeps a washing machine from growing mold: leave the door open after every load. Simple. For a New Jersey seller it matters more than the machine deserves.

I have walked buyers through hundreds of basements in Cranford, Westfield and Scotch Plains. The laundry area is where they stop talking and start smelling. A musty front-loader does not stay a washer problem in a buyer’s mind. It becomes a question about the whole basement, and then the foundation walls, and then the floor by the utility sink.

The routine: door and detergent drawer open, wipe the gasket dry, less detergent, a monthly cleaning cycle, clear the drain filter, never leave wet laundry overnight. ## Article 2: The No. 1 Lighting Mistake That Makes a NJ Home Feel Cold to Buyers

**Live URL:** https://thejorgeramirezgroup.com/blog/lighting-mistake-cold-stark-rooms-nj-home-sellers-2026
**Image:** https://thejorgeramirezgroup.com/images/properties/07-dining.webp (crop to 1200×630)

### Facebook Post (100-150 words)

House Outlook’s newsletter this week linked a Martha Stewart piece on the number one lighting mistake designers wish homeowners would avoid. If your home feels cold or stark, the problem might not be the furniture or the paint. It is one overhead fixture doing all the work.

I see it constantly in prewar colonials in Westfield, Cranford and Maplewood: one ceiling light per bedroom, none in the living room, and a cool bright bulb in each. The house is in good shape and still feels like a waiting room. By mid-October, after-work showings happen in the dark, so that is the house buyers remember.

The fix costs less than a paint job: two more light sources per room, every bulb matched to one warm color, lamps in the corners, bedside lights in the primary, dimmers where a switch swap allows, and everything on for every showing. House Outlook linked the designers’ number one lighting mistake this week, and I see it in half the colonials I walk through. The fix for NJ sellers:
✔ Three layers per room: ceiling, lamps, one accent
✔ Every bulb the same warm color, spares in the closet
✔ A lamp in the far corner makes the room read bigger
✔ Bedside lamps on, ceiling light off, for the primary bedroom photo
✔ Every light on before every showing, closets included
✘ Recessed lighting installed three weeks before listing

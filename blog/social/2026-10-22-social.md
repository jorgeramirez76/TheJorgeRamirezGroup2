### Facebook Post (100-150 words)

House Outlook linked a countertop stain guide this week, and the point is simple: match the cleaner to the surface. What lifts a coffee ring from laminate will dull marble, and the pad that saves butcher block scratches quartz for good.

I read it as a seller's checklist. At a showing in Westfield or Summit, the buyer walks to the island, puts a hand on it and looks down. A ring by the sink is the first thing they see, and a buyer who decides the counters need replacing has already subtracted a renovation from your price.

Clean what cleans. Reseal granite that absorbs water. Send etched marble and cracked slabs to a pro and put the rest on the written repair list. And never hide a burn under a cutting board; the inspector lifts it. Full guide: thejorgeramirezgroup.com/blog/countertop-stains-before-listing-nj-home-sellers-2026

### Facebook Post (100-150 words)

House Outlook linked a designer list of the colors that make a room look more expensive: warm neutrals, cream, deep green, chocolate brown, charcoal, ochre and slate blue. Good list. A listing isn't a design magazine, though.

Here's how I sort it for a seller. The quiet colors go on the walls and trim, where they stay with the house: warm neutral walls, cream trim. The saturated ones go into things that leave with you: a throw, two pillows, a runner. Deep green earns one wall or a powder room. Chocolate brown stays in the furniture. Charcoal goes on an island or a fireplace, not a family room.

A buyer in Montclair expects character. A buyer touring new construction in Edison expects clean and bright. Paint for the buyer you have. Full guide: thejorgeramirezgroup.com/blog/colors-make-room-look-expensive-nj-home-sellers-2026

## Article 3: The Handover Drawer: What NJ Sellers Should Leave for the Buyer, and What Buyers Should Stock in Week One

### Facebook Post (100-150 words)

House Outlook's article on what belongs in a junk drawer is about tape, scissors and a flashlight. I read it as a closing checklist.

Sellers: pick one kitchen drawer, empty it, and label it for the buyer. Every key, labeled. Garage remotes and the alarm code. Manuals for everything that stays. Paint colors and a touch-up jar. A one-line map to the water, gas and electrical shutoffs. Filter sizes. The plumber's number. Trash day. None of it is in the contract. All of it gets talked about for years.

Buyers: assume the drawer is empty and pack your own week-one kit, then change the locks and the codes before anything else. Full guide: thejorgeramirezgroup.com/blog/handover-drawer-what-nj-sellers-leave-buyers-2026

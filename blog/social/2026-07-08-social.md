### Instagram Caption
6 laundry habits that are slowly damaging your NJ home — and costing you at inspection:

🔴 Too much detergent (more suds ≠ cleaner clothes — it leaves residue in the drum and pump)
🔴 Overloading the washer (stresses the drum bearings — repairs run $200–$400)
🔴 Never cleaning the drum (mildew smell transfers to clothes and signals deferred maintenance)
🔴 Fabric softener on towels (coats fibers — towels stop absorbing after a few months)
🔴 Leaving wet clothes in the washer (mold grows in 8–12 hours)
🔴 Cleaning only the lint screen (the duct beyond it is where lint actually accumulates — and where dryer fires start)

That last one is the one inspectors flag. A clogged dryer duct is a documented fire hazard and gives buyers real negotiating leverage.

## POST 3: Keeping Your NJ Home Cool in Summer
**Blog:** cool-home-summer-sleep-nj-2026.html

### Facebook Post (100–150 words)
Summer is NJ's peak selling season — and a hot, stuffy home at a showing loses buyers before they reach the primary bedroom. I walk into homes every July where the AC is running but the house still feels warm: dirty coils, a filter that hasn't been changed, a system that's 20 years old and running at 60 percent capacity. Buyers notice all of it. An AC tune-up costs $100–$200 and produces a service receipt that removes one of buyers' biggest negotiating points. Set the thermostat to 72°F before showings, run ceiling fans counterclockwise, and open shaded windows 15 minutes before buyers arrive. These things cost nothing.

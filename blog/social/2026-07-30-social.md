## POST 1: 11 Things In Your NJ Home That Could Be Worth Real Money Before You Sell

---

### Facebook Post

Before you start packing, donating, and decluttering for your NJ home sale — stop and do this first.

I've watched sellers accidentally give away real money during the pre-listing process. A box of original pressing vinyl left at the curb. A sterling silver tea service donated because they assumed it was plated. Cast iron pans tossed because they "looked old."

Here are 11 things worth checking before anything leaves your house:

🎵 Original vinyl records (1950s–1970s pressings can be worth $50–$500+)
🥄 Sterling silver — look for "925" or "Sterling" hallmarks
🖼️ Original artwork — regional oil paintings can carry real value
🔮 Fine crystal — Waterford, Baccarat, Heisey
📚 First edition books — especially mid-20th century fiction
⌚ Vintage watches — Rolex, Omega, Longines
🪑 Antique furniture with maker's marks (Stickley, Duncan Phyfe)
🪙 Pre-1965 silver coins and old paper currency
📷 Vintage cameras — Leica, Rollei, early Nikon
🍳 Cast iron cookware — Griswold and Wagner pans fetch $75–$200+
🧸 Vintage toys and board games in original packaging

## POST 2: 6 Early Warning Signs of a Pest Problem NJ Home Sellers Can't Ignore

---

### Facebook Post

Here's a stat that sticks with me: a pest problem found at inspection costs NJ sellers $10,000–$25,000+ in concessions. Sometimes the buyer walks entirely.

What makes it worse is that the signs are usually there weeks or months before the inspector finds them. Most sellers just don't know what to look for.

The 6 early warning signs every NJ seller should check:

🪵 Hollow-sounding wood when you tap it (termites eat from the inside out)
🌊 Wood that looks blistered or warped — without a water leak nearby
🐭 Scratching or scurrying in walls at night (mice and rats are nocturnal)
👃 Musty or oily smell in closets or under vanities
🧣 Small holes in wool sweaters or stored clothing (clothes moths)
🪛 Sawdust-like frass near windowsills or baseboard joints (termites or carpenter ants)

In NJ — especially in older wood-frame homes in Essex, Union, and Morris counties — termites and carpenter ants are extremely common. The sellers who handle it before listing come out ahead. The ones who find out during inspection are negotiating from a weak position.

Get a pre-listing WDI inspection 60–90 days before you plan to list. It's $150–$300 and almost always worth it.

## POST 3: 6 Outdated Laundry Habits That Signal Deferred Maintenance to NJ Home Buyers

---

### Facebook Post

Most NJ sellers spend three weekends on the kitchen and walk right past the laundry room. Big mistake.

The laundry room tells buyers exactly how you've maintained the home. A clean, functioning space says you pay attention. A musty drum, mold on the gasket, a clogged lint trap, and detergent residue caked in the dispenser drawer? That says something else entirely.

The 6 habits that cause visible damage buyers notice:

1️⃣ Too much detergent — residue feeds mold, causes that musty drum smell
2️⃣ Always washing in hot water — breaks down gaskets and seals faster
3️⃣ Fabric softener every load — waxy buildup clogs the dispenser, reduces towel absorbency
4️⃣ Overloading the washer — strains bearings, causes grinding noise buyers hear on test cycles
5️⃣ Leaving wet clothes sitting in the machine — direct path to drum mold and permanent odor
6️⃣ Ignoring the lint trap and dryer vent — fire hazard AND an inspection red flag

Before listing, run a cleaning cycle, clean the gasket with diluted bleach, clear the dispenser drawer, and get the dryer vent checked. Two hours. Real difference in buyer perception.

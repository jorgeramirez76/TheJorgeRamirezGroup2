## Article 2: Small Closets in Older NJ Homes: How to Make Them Show Bigger to Buyers

**Live URL:** https://thejorgeramirezgroup.com/blog/small-closets-show-bigger-nj-home-sellers-2026
**Image:** https://images.unsplash.com/photo-1558591710-4b4a1ae0f04d?w=1200&h=630&fit=crop
**Instagram image:** https://images.unsplash.com/photo-1558591710-4b4a1ae0f04d?w=1080&h=1080&fit=crop

### Facebook Post (100-150 words)

The 1920s colonial in Westfield. The Tudor in Maplewood. The Cape in Cranford. Gorgeous houses, tiny closets. And buyers open every single one.

Here's what I tell sellers: the size of the closet is fixed. What the buyer sees when they open it is not. A half-empty closet in an older home reads as "enough." A packed closet of the exact same size reads as "we'll need an addition." Same closet, two very different offers.

Before you list: get every closet to half full (you're moving anyway, start packing), switch to one style of slim hanger, hook a double rod over the existing one, add a motion-sensor puck light, and deal with any musty smell. Then do the hall, linen, and coat closets too, because buyers open those on the way out.

## Article 3: Bathroom Products to Toss Before Your NJ Home Hits the Market

**Live URL:** https://thejorgeramirezgroup.com/blog/bathroom-products-toss-before-listing-nj-2026
**Image:** https://images.unsplash.com/photo-1584622650111-993a426fbf0a?w=1200&h=630&fit=crop
**Instagram image:** https://images.unsplash.com/photo-1584622650111-993a426fbf0a?w=1080&h=1080&fit=crop

### Facebook Post (100-150 words)

Every agent knows it and most sellers don't want to believe it: buyers open the medicine cabinet.

They open the vanity, too, and the linen closet. Some are checking storage, some are checking for leaks, some are just curious. Whatever is in there becomes part of the story they tell themselves about your house. I've watched a buyer in Scotch Plains cool on a perfectly good colonial because the bathroom had prescription bottles on the shelf, a rusty razor by the tub, and a loofah that had seen better years. None of it was a defect. All of it was a distraction.

Before you list, toss: expired meds (use a pharmacy or police drop box, don't flush), old makeup, anything that stays wet in the shower, worn-out razors and toothbrushes, mildewed curtains and faded towels, and everything on the counter but one soap and a hand towel.

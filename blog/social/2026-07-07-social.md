## Post 2: Best Fan Placement for Home Cooling in NJ
**Blog:** `best-fan-placement-home-cooling-nj-2026.html`

### Facebook (100-150 words)
Most NJ homeowners have their ceiling fans spinning the wrong direction right now — and it's costing them on their electric bill and on how comfortable their home feels during summer showings.

The fix takes 30 seconds: there's a small switch on the motor housing of every ceiling fan. Flip it so the blades spin counterclockwise (in summer). That creates a downward airflow that makes a room feel up to 4°F cooler without touching the thermostat.

For sellers: a home that's hot and stuffy at a showing loses buyers fast. Run fans correctly, set the AC to 72°F, and open the shaded windows 15 minutes before buyers arrive. It costs almost nothing and completely changes how buyers experience the space.

## Post 3: The Viral Grout Cleaning Hack That Destroys Tile
**Blog:** `viral-grout-cleaning-hack-tile-damage-nj-2026.html`

### Facebook (100-150 words)
That toilet bowl cleaner grout hack you've seen all over social media? It makes grout look white for a few weeks. Then it starts crumbling.

Here's the chemistry: toilet bowl cleaners use hydrochloric acid or high-concentration bleach — both of which dissolve the mineral structure of cement grout over time. Repeated use means cracking, crumbling, and pitted grout lines. Same problem with vinegar, which has a pH low enough to erode grout sealer and make staining worse in the long run.

For NJ sellers: damaged grout in bathrooms is one of the most common inspection findings I see. Buyers use it to negotiate $3,000–$6,000 in credits. Regrouting a shower runs $500–$1,500. That math is easy.

### Instagram Caption
That toilet bowl cleaner grout hack? Stop.

It works because of acid or bleach — both of which dissolve the mineral structure of cement grout with repeated use. Your grout looks white for 2-3 weeks, then starts crumbling at the seams. Same issue with vinegar (pH ~2) and the baking soda + vinegar combo that just neutralizes into salty water.

What actually works: 2 parts baking soda + 1 part 3% hydrogen peroxide. Paste on the grout. 10-15 minutes. Scrub. Rinse. That's it.

For NJ sellers: damaged grout in a bathroom is a $3,000–$6,000 inspection credit waiting to happen. Regrouting costs $500–$1,500. Easy math.

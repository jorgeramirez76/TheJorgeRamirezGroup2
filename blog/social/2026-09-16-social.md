## Article 1: Wi-Fi Dead Zones Before an NJ Home Sale: What to Check and Document

**Live URL:** https://thejorgeramirezgroup.com/blog/wifi-dead-zones-nj-home-sale-checklist-2026
**Image:** https://thejorgeramirezgroup.com/images/properties/04-brick-stone.webp
**Instagram image:** https://thejorgeramirezgroup.com/images/properties/04-brick-stone.webp

### Facebook Post (100-150 words)

Buyers who work from home ask about internet the way earlier buyers asked about the furnace. On showings in Summit, Chatham and Montclair, I've watched people open a laptop in the room they plan to use as an office and check the signal before they look at the closet.

Most dead zones aren't the service. They're the router in a basement corner, plaster-and-lath walls in a pre-war Westfield colonial, a brick chimney through the middle of the house, or a mirror and a radiator between the router and the back bedroom.

Before you list: walk each room with a speed-test app, move the router somewhere central and open, and put together one page with the providers at your address (the FCC broadband map is the place to check), where the jacks are, and which equipment stays. Accurate beats impressive here.

## Article 2: Cleaning and Storing Outdoor Furniture Before Fall Listing Photos in NJ

**Live URL:** https://thejorgeramirezgroup.com/blog/outdoor-furniture-cleaning-fall-listing-nj-2026
**Image:** https://thejorgeramirezgroup.com/images/blog-hero/families-2026-1280.webp
**Instagram image:** https://thejorgeramirezgroup.com/images/blog-hero/families-2026-1280.webp

### Facebook Post (100-150 words)

A September or October listing in Morristown, Chatham or Scotch Plains still gets photos of the deck, the patio and the porch, and buyers walk that route on nearly every showing. A summer of pollen, grill smoke and rain shows on the furniture more than you notice from the kitchen window.

Green film on a white resin chair, rust bleeding from an iron table, a faded umbrella and a pile of mismatched cushions all read as neglect in a photo, even when the house is well kept.

The fix is an afternoon: check the care label and clean by material, keep one clean seating group for the frame, store everything faded or broken before the first frost, and decide in writing whether the patio set and grill go with the house. Buyers ask. Have the answer ready.

## Article 3: Floating Furniture: Testing a Layout Before Listing Your NJ Home

**Live URL:** https://thejorgeramirezgroup.com/blog/floating-furniture-layout-nj-home-sellers-2026
**Image:** https://thejorgeramirezgroup.com/images/properties/06-bedroom.webp
**Instagram image:** https://thejorgeramirezgroup.com/images/properties/06-bedroom.webp

### Facebook Post (100-150 words)

Almost every house I walk through before listing has the same layout: every piece of furniture pushed against a wall. In a long, narrow living room in a Westfield or Cranford colonial, that turns the middle of the room into a hallway.

Floating furniture means pulling a few pieces into the room. The sofa comes forward with a walkway behind it, two chairs face it across a table, and the room suddenly has a front and a back. It costs nothing and takes ten minutes to undo.

Here's the test: stand in the doorway where the photographer will stand, take a phone picture, move the furniture, take the same picture again. Compare. Then walk the buyer's route through the room without touching anything. Keep whichever version is better. Some rooms improve. Some don't. You'll know by the end of the weekend.

Preparing a NJ home for listing photos? Try floating the furniture in one room.

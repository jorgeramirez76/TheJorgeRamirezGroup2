## Article 1: Slow Drains Before Listing: The Easy Fix and What a Clog Tells NJ Buyers

**Live URL:** https://thejorgeramirezgroup.com/blog/slow-drain-fix-before-listing-nj-home-sellers-2026
**Image:** https://thejorgeramirezgroup.com/images/properties/05-kitchen.webp
**Instagram image:** https://thejorgeramirezgroup.com/images/properties/05-kitchen.webp

### Facebook Post (100-150 words)

A slow drain is one of the few defects a buyer can find in a ten-minute showing. They run the faucet in a Westfield or Maplewood bathroom, the water sits, and they stop thinking "hair in the trap" and start thinking "old pipes."

House Outlook sent out an easy fix this week for minor clogs, and it is the one I give sellers: a kettle of hot water and dish soap for a greasy kitchen line, pull the stopper and fish out the hair in the bathroom, then baking soda and vinegar for the film. No chemical cleaners in an older NJ home, ever. They eat cast iron and make the plumber's job dangerous.

If two drains lag or a tub gurgles when the toilet flushes, get a sewer scope before the buyer does.

## Article 2: Should You Remove a Tree Stump Before Selling Your NJ Home?

**Live URL:** https://thejorgeramirezgroup.com/blog/remove-tree-stump-before-selling-nj-home-2026
**Image:** https://thejorgeramirezgroup.com/images/properties/08-exterior.webp

### Facebook Post (100-150 words)

To the owner, a tree stump is "eventually." To the buyer, it is the first thing in the listing photo and the first question at the curb: why did the tree come down? Storm, disease, roots in the sewer line. None of those answers helps you sell a house in Westfield or Cranford.

House Outlook's newsletter linked a piece this week on whether stumps are worth removing. From the selling side the answer is yes, and grinding beats digging for almost everyone. A rotting stump near the foundation is exactly what termites and carpenter ants want, and the wood-destroying-insect inspector most NJ buyers hire will name it. Suckers, heaved walkways and a hidden mower hazard finish the list.

Call 811, grind it a month before the photographer, fill and seed, and the buyer never knows there was a tree.

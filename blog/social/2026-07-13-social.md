## POST 2: Why Your NJ Home Feels Colder Than It Should

### Facebook Post
If your NJ home feels colder than it should with the heat running — you're not imagining it, and it's probably costing you at resale.

Most NJ homes were built before modern energy codes. Attic insulation at R-11 when current code calls for R-49. Single-pane windows replaced decades ago with double-panes that have since lost their seal. Ductwork running through unconditioned spaces, leaking conditioned air before it reaches the registers.

Buyers touring homes in winter notice the temperature inconsistency. They feel the draft along the floor. They see the fogged window glass. And they discount their offer before they've finished the first floor.

The good news: most of these fixes cost $200–$4,000 and can prevent $5,000–$10,000 in post-inspection buyer credits.

## POST 3: How to Lower Utility Bills in Your NJ Home

### Facebook Post
NJ utility rates are among the highest in the US. PSE&G and JCP&L rates combined with older housing stock that was never insulated properly means a lot of NJ homeowners are paying $350–$500/month in peak heating and cooling seasons for no good reason.

The fixes aren't complicated:
✅ Annual HVAC tune-up: $75–$150, saves 10–15% efficiency
✅ Smart thermostat: $150–$250, saves $100–$200/year
✅ Attic insulation top-off: $1,500–$3,500, 15–25% energy reduction
✅ LED conversion throughout: under $100 in bulbs, $150–$300/year savings

And here's the real estate angle no one talks about: buyers ask about utility bills. A home with $300/month bills vs $200/month is a $1,200/year difference that shows up in offers. Energy improvements don't just save you money while you live there — they help you sell for more when you're ready.

## Article 1: Stop Storing Loose Batteries in the Junk Drawer: A Small Fire Risk to Fix Before Listing a NJ Home

### Facebook Post (100-150 words)

House Outlook’s newsletter this week flagged something most of us have never thought about: loose batteries in the junk drawer. A 9-volt has both terminals on the same end, so a key ring or a coin can bridge them, the battery heats up, and a drawer full of paper and tape is the wrong place for that to happen.

For a New Jersey seller there is a second reason to fix it. Before a sale closes, the local fire official inspects your smoke alarms, carbon monoxide alarms and fire extinguisher. A chirping alarm running on whatever was in that drawer is a common reason sellers fail the first visit with closing approaching.

Twenty-minute fix: sort by type, tape every 9-volt terminal, test the rest, take rechargeables to a drop-off bin, store the keepers in a divided case, and put fresh batteries in every alarm today. Full guide: thejorgeramirezgroup.com/blog/loose-batteries-junk-drawer-fire-risk-nj-home-sellers-2026

### Facebook Post (100-150 words)

House Outlook’s newsletter this week linked a Livingetc piece on the mistakes people make decorating with bright colors. The designers’ point was balance. Mine is about the offer.

A listing photo is seen first as a thumbnail on a phone, and at that size a saturated wall is the only thing in the frame. Buyers scrolling Montclair or Maplewood listings do not see your dining room; they see a red rectangle. The ones who visit add the cost of repainting to their number, and in my experience they overestimate it every time.

The seven mistakes: no neutral anchor, ignoring the floors and tile, bright color in the kitchen or primary bedroom, forgetting which way the room faces, too many brights at once, half-done trim and ceilings, and choosing from the chip. Plus my rule for repaint versus leave it. Full guide: thejorgeramirezgroup.com/blog/bright-color-decorating-mistakes-nj-home-sellers-2026

## Article 3: The 90/90 Rule for Closets: A Fast Wardrobe Edit Before Listing a NJ Home

**Live URL:** https://thejorgeramirezgroup.com/blog/90-90-rule-closet-wardrobe-edit-nj-home-sellers-2026
**Image:** https://thejorgeramirezgroup.com/images/editorial/nj-first-time-buyer-planning-2026-1280.webp (crop to 1200×630)
**Instagram image:** https://thejorgeramirezgroup.com/images/editorial/nj-first-time-buyer-planning-2026-1280.webp (crop to 1080×1080)

### Facebook Post (100-150 words)

House Outlook’s newsletter this week linked the 90/90 rule for simplifying a wardrobe: if you have not worn it in the last 90 days and will not in the next 90, it goes. No sentiment test, just a calendar.

For a New Jersey seller the rule has a second job. Buyers open every closet, fast. A rod packed so tight the hangers cannot move reads as “not enough storage,” and in a pre-war colonial in Westfield or Maplewood that is the objection they carry to the next house. The goal is a rod a little over half full, a clear floor and one tidy row on the shelf.

One adjustment for four real seasons: clothes that fail the test only because it is October get boxed and labeled for the move, not donated. Full guide: thejorgeramirezgroup.com/blog/90-90-rule-closet-wardrobe-edit-nj-home-sellers-2026

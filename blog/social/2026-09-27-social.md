## Article 1: The 3-4-5 Decorating Rule and How to Use It in a NJ Home You Plan to Sell

### Facebook Post (100-150 words)

House Outlook's newsletter featured designer Nancy Cavaliere's 3-4-5 rule this week: three patterns, four period styles and five colors or textures in every room. It is a good recipe for a house you live in. For a house you are about to photograph and sell, I tell New Jersey sellers to run it in reverse.

Count what is already in the room. Above three patterns, four styles or five colors, you are editing, not shopping. Buyers touring a colonial in Westfield or a split-level in Union are trying to picture their own furniture, and listing photos turn a busy room into noise on a phone screen.

The seller's version: two patterns, two or three styles, and five textures in a neutral palette. Wood, linen, wool, metal and a plant fix a flat room without a paint can.

The 3-4-5 rule, seller's edition. 🛋️

Designer Nancy Cavaliere's formula says 3 patterns, 4 styles and 5 colors or textures per room. Great for living in. Too much for listing photos.

Before your NJ home hits the market:
📸 Count patterns, styles and colors in a phone photo of the room, not the room itself
🧵 Two patterns, not three: one on the rug, one on a pillow
🪑 Two or three furniture styles that share a color or material
🪵 Five textures in a neutral palette: wood, linen, wool, metal, a plant
🎨 One warm white, one accent wall, done

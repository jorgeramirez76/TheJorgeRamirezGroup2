## POST 3: How to Declutter Your Closets Before Listing Your NJ Home (And Keep Them That Way)

---

### Facebook Post

Here's something most NJ sellers don't know: buyers don't just glance at your closets. They open them, step inside, and mentally measure whether there's room for their life.

A jammed closet says "this house doesn't have enough storage." An organized closet with breathing room says "there's more space here than I thought."

The four-step closet system I teach every seller I work with:

1. Full-removal pass — everything out, floor clean
2. Reverse-hanger sort — the trick that reveals what you actually wear (and what to donate)
3. Organization that signals capacity — 20% empty, visible floor, color-coordinated
4. Donation bag system — one bag per week until you list, keeps it from filling back up

This takes a weekend. Done right, it adds to buyer perception in a way that's hard to quantify but very easy to feel at the closing table.

### Instagram Caption

Buyers open every closet in your NJ home. Every. Single. One. 🚪

And a packed, overflowing closet — even in a beautiful house — makes them mentally subtract storage space and lower their offer.

Here's the 4-step system I walk sellers through before every listing:

Step 1️⃣ Full-removal pass
Take everything out. Vacuum the floor. Start from zero — not from "let me tidy this up a bit."

Step 2️⃣ Reverse-hanger sort
Hang everything backward. After 3 weeks, anything still reversed goes to donation. No willpower required.

Step 3️⃣ Organize for perceived capacity
Fill the closet only 80%. Visible floor. Color-coordinated. Matching hangers if possible.

Step 4️⃣ Donation bag system
One bag by the closet door. Fill it weekly until you list. Prevents the slow re-fill.

Buyers don't notice effort. They notice the feeling — "this house has room for us."

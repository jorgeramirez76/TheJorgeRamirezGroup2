### Facebook Post (100-150 words)

Modern sofa in a 1920s colonial. Farmhouse table in a Hoboken condo. Her mid-century pieces, his leather and dark wood. Almost every NJ home I list is a mix of styles, whether the owners planned it or not.

Buyers don't consciously judge the mix. They feel whether the house makes sense. When the rooms share a thread, they relax and start picturing their life there. When every room looks like a different owner decorated it, the house feels like "a lot," and that follows them into the offer.

Designers blend styles for a living, and their rules are simple: pick a lead style and give it two thirds of the room, repeat two or three colors everywhere, repeat one material, match scale not era, let the architecture cast the deciding vote, and remove the third and fourth styles entirely. One weekend, almost no money.

## Article 3: How to Clean Your Couch Before NJ Buyers Sit on It

**Live URL:** https://thejorgeramirezgroup.com/blog/clean-couch-before-showings-nj-home-sellers-2026
**Image:** https://images.unsplash.com/photo-1555041469-a586c61ea9bc?w=1200&h=630&fit=crop
**Instagram image:** https://images.unsplash.com/photo-1555041469-a586c61ea9bc?w=1080&h=1080&fit=crop

### Facebook Post (100-150 words)

Here's what happens on almost every showing: buyers walk the house, then drift back to the living room and sit down on the sofa while their agent talks. That's the moment they stop touring and start imagining living there.

If the cushion is firm and the fabric is clean and the room smells like nothing, they relax. If they sink into a collapsed cushion, spot a stain on the arm, or catch a whiff of the dog, they get up fast. And that feeling follows them into the offer.

Sellers deep-clean the kitchen and bathrooms and never think about the couch. Fixing it takes an afternoon: check the cleaning code tag, vacuum under every cushion, baking soda for an hour, spot-treat by code, wash the covers, then flip and plump. If it's past saving, a neutral slipcover costs a fraction of a new sofa.

The sofa is the one piece of furniture buyers actually touch during a NJ showing, and a stained, saggy, or smelly couch drags down the whole room. Before you list: find the cleaning code tag under a cushion (W, S, WS, or X), vacuum everything including under the cushions, deodorize with baking soda, spot-treat by code, wash the covers, and flip and plump. If the cushions have collapsed or the smell lives in the foam, a neutral slipcover is the honest fix.

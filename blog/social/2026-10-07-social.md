## Article 1: Cloudy Shower Doors: What NJ Buyers Notice First

**Live URL:** https://thejorgeramirezgroup.com/blog/shower-door-soap-scum-bathroom-glass-nj-home-sellers-2026
**Image:** https://thejorgeramirezgroup.com/images/editorial/nj-rental-property-transition-2026-1280.webp

### Facebook Post (100-150 words)

House Outlook's newsletter this week included a guide to getting soap scum off shower doors, the kind of buildup that turns clear glass into something that looks frosted. Good housekeeping advice. For anyone about to list a home in New Jersey, it is more than that.

I have stood in a lot of hall bathrooms in Westfield, Cranford and Scotch Plains with a buyer who opened the door, looked at the shower, and closed it without a word. The room is small. The glass is the biggest surface in it. If the glass is cloudy, that is the bathroom, and buyers assume the grout, the caulk and the drain are in the same shape.

Warm vinegar, a non-scratch pad, the bottom third twice, then a squeegee. Replace the stained rubber sweep. Recaulk anything mildewed. And if the glass is still cloudy when it is bone dry, it is etched, and that is a replacement, not a remodel.

## Article 2: 8 Upgrades NJ Sellers Should Skip Before Listing

**Live URL:** https://thejorgeramirezgroup.com/blog/home-upgrades-not-worth-money-before-selling-nj-2026
**Image:** https://thejorgeramirezgroup.com/images/editorial/nj-written-offer-comparison-2026-1280.webp
**Instagram image:** https://thejorgeramirezgroup.com/images/editorial/nj-written-offer-comparison-2026-1280.webp

### Facebook Post (100-150 words)

House Outlook's newsletter this week pointed to a Southern Living piece where designers list eight home upgrades they say are not worth the money. I did not need convincing. Every spring I meet New Jersey sellers who spent the winter on the wrong project.

My test before any pre-listing project: Will a buyer see it in the first ninety seconds? Will an inspector flag the house without it? Does the price ceiling on your street leave room to recover it? Three noes, skip it.

The eight I would skip in Summit, Millburn, Union or Rahway: a kitchen gut done to sell, a luxury appliance package, whole-house smart systems, turning a bedroom into a closet, trend-forward tile, finishing the basement the month before listing, replacing windows that still work, and elaborate landscaping or water features.

Spend it on paint, lighting, inspection repairs, a deep clean and the yard instead.

## Article 3: Restyle a NJ Home for Fall Showings: 7 Easy Changes

**Live URL:** https://thejorgeramirezgroup.com/blog/fall-restyle-nj-home-october-showings-2026
**Image:** https://thejorgeramirezgroup.com/images/properties/02-staircase.webp
**Instagram image:** https://thejorgeramirezgroup.com/images/properties/02-staircase.webp

### Facebook Post (100-150 words)

House Outlook's newsletter this week shared a Real Simple list of easy ways to restyle a home for fall. Textiles, light, a few simplified surfaces. Nothing in it costs much. For a New Jersey seller showing a house in October, it is a staging checklist.

By mid-October the light is lower and buyers who work in the city tour Westfield, Summit, Chatham and Morristown after the train gets in. They walk into your living room as the sun leaves it. The houses that show well at 5:30 are warm and lit. The ones that struggle looked great in June with the same furniture.

Heavier throw and two warm pillows. Three light sources per room on timers, same warm bulbs. One seasonal thing on the mantel, not a display. Rugs back down. Porch swept, one planter of mums. Fresh air, no cinnamon candle. And shoot the exterior photos now, while the trees still have color.

### Facebook Post (100-150 words)

Low-water landscaping is the yard trend of 2026. Will it hurt you when you sell in New Jersey? It depends entirely on how it is done.

In Westfield, Cranford or Chatham, buyers still read a front lawn as a cared-for house. What they do not need is an acre of turf. Keep a tidy lawn panel at the street and the entry, then convert the spots where grass already fails: deep shade, the strip by the driveway, the slope, the corner the sprinkler never reached.

Plant what belongs here. Little bluestem, switchgrass, coneflower, inkberry holly and bayberry handle our summers once they root in. Mulch two to three inches, cut a clean edge, and plant in September, not the week before photos.

Skip gravel over plastic. In our climate it grows weeds and moss, and buyers price the removal.

## Article 2: Color-Capping: The 2026 Paint Trend and How NJ Sellers Should Use It

**Live URL:** https://thejorgeramirezgroup.com/blog/color-capping-paint-trend-nj-home-sellers-2026
**Image:** https://thejorgeramirezgroup.com/images/properties/07-dining.webp
**Instagram image:** https://thejorgeramirezgroup.com/images/properties/07-dining.webp

### Facebook Post (100-150 words)

Color-drenching had its moment. The 2026 paint idea is color-capping: walls in one shade, ceiling and trim in a second, deliberately different one.

Done well, it frames a room. A soft mushroom ceiling over warm white walls finishes a dining room in Summit. A deep green cap in a Montclair Victorian with ten-foot ceilings looks intentional. Done badly, it is a dark lid on an eight-foot bedroom in a Cranford cape.

If your house is going on the market: one room, not the whole house. Keep the cap soft. Cap the trim but leave the ceiling white under nine feet. Leave the kitchen and primary bedroom neutral. And leave original stained wood trim alone, because painting it splits buyers and is not reversible.

Remember that repainting trim is the labor-heavy part of any paint job. Bold trim makes buyers do that math in their offer.

## Article 3: Protecting Your Digital Data While Selling or Buying a Home in NJ

**Live URL:** https://thejorgeramirezgroup.com/blog/protect-digital-data-selling-buying-home-nj-2026
**Image:** https://thejorgeramirezgroup.com/images/editorial/nj-first-time-buyer-planning-2026-1280.webp
**Instagram image:** https://thejorgeramirezgroup.com/images/editorial/nj-first-time-buyer-planning-2026-1280.webp

### Facebook Post (100-150 words)

A home sale moves a lot of personal information: photos of your rooms online for years, strangers in your home office, smart devices tied to your accounts, and closing emails full of bank details.

Sellers: before the photographer comes, clear names, mail, diplomas, calendars, medication and anything on a screen. During showings, lock up laptops, passports, checkbooks and spare keys. Talk to your agent and attorney about whether cameras stay on. Change the Wi-Fi password. Before closing, factory-reset every device that stays and hand the buyer a written list.

Buyers: the biggest risk is wire fraud at closing. Verify wire instructions by phone using a number you already have, never one in the email. Treat any change in instructions as suspicious until you hear a voice confirm it.

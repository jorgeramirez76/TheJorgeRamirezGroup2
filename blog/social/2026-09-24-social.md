## Article 1: The Paper Towel Habit: A Cheaper Way to Keep an NJ Home Show-Ready

**Live URL:** https://thejorgeramirezgroup.com/blog/paper-towel-habit-cheaper-cleaning-nj-home-sellers-2026

### Facebook Post (100-150 words)

The day a house in Westfield or Cranford goes on the market, the cleaning goes from weekly to daily and the paper towel roll disappears twice as fast. I have watched sellers burn through a case in the first two weeks of a listing and still hand me a kitchen with streaks on the refrigerator.

House Outlook ran a piece this week on cutting paper towel spending. For a seller, the money is the small part. Paper towels leave lint on mirrors, streaks on stainless and a cloudy swipe on quartz, and a fat roll on the counter shows up in every listing photo.

The fix is a dozen microfiber cloths, color-coded by job, plus one roll kept in a cabinet for the ugly work. Cheaper every week, and the glass and steel actually photograph.

## Article 2: Houseplants and Home Showings: What a Dying Orchid Tells NJ Buyers

**Live URL:** https://thejorgeramirezgroup.com/blog/houseplants-home-showings-dying-orchid-nj-sellers-2026
**Image:** https://thejorgeramirezgroup.com/images/properties/04-brick-stone.webp
**Instagram image:** https://thejorgeramirezgroup.com/images/properties/04-brick-stone.webp

### Facebook Post (100-150 words)

House Outlook shared a piece this week on the number one reason orchids die. The answer is kindness: too much water, a pot that never drains, a dark corner. That orchid a seller in Maplewood bought to dress the kitchen island is usually the first thing to turn yellow once the house is listed.

Buyers read plants the way they read everything else. A healthy fig in the corner of a Montclair living room says someone pays attention here. A wilted orchid on the counter says the opposite, and buyers in Westfield and Summit carry that impression to the roof and the furnace.

The rule for a listed home: fewer and bigger, nothing on a windowsill, a saucer under everything, and nothing dead or dusty. Then check under every pot for the water rings an inspector will find.

### Google Business Profile Post (80-100 words)

Selling a NJ home this fall? Edit the houseplants two weeks before photos. Keep only what you can keep alive for two months with almost no attention: one floor plant in an empty corner, a small arrangement on the dining table and matched pots at the front door. Move everything off the windowsills, put a saucer and mat under every pot, and remove anything dead, dying or dusty. Then lift each pot and check for water rings before a buyer's inspector does.

## Post 1: 6 Pest Warning Signs in NJ Homes That Sellers Miss
**Blog:** `/blog/pest-warning-signs-nj-homes-2026.html`

### Facebook Post
In my full-time New Jersey real estate work since 2017, I can tell you: pest problems don't announce themselves. They leave clues — and sellers who don't know what to look for find out the hard way when a buyer's inspector does.

The six warning signs I tell every NJ seller to check before listing:

🪵 **Hollow-sounding wood** near foundation sill plates → classic early termite indicator
🐭 **Scratching or scurrying in walls at night** → rodents (and possibly chewed electrical wiring)
👃 **Ammonia smell in basement or under kitchen sink** → mouse urine trails — buyers smell this in seconds
🪣 **Sawdust-like debris near windows or doors** → carpenter ants excavating moisture-damaged wood
🧥 **Small holes in stored wool clothing** → clothes moths in undisturbed closets
💩 **Dark rice-sized droppings in cabinet backs** → rodent activity buyers find when they open drawers

In NJ, a WDI (termite) inspection is standard in almost every sale. Buyers find what sellers miss. Fix it before they do.

## Post 2: 6 House Sounds NJ Buyers and Sellers Can't Ignore
**Blog:** `/blog/house-sounds-nj-home-inspection-2026.html`

### Facebook Post
A quiet house and a well-maintained house are not the same thing. But when your house starts making sounds — certain ones — it's communicating something that will end up in an inspector's report.

Here's what I tell NJ sellers to listen for before they list:

🐀 **Scratching/scurrying in walls or ceiling** → rodents or squirrels; potential wiring damage
🚿 **Banging pipes when water shuts off** → water hammer; often galvanized plumbing in NJ's older homes
⚡ **Clicking or buzzing at the electrical panel** → Federal Pacific/Zinsco panel = major inspection flag in NJ
🌡️ **HVAC banging or persistent clicking** → possible cracked heat exchanger (CO risk — take seriously)
🪠 **Gurgling drains or bubbling toilets** → drain venting issue or aging cast iron sewer line
🏠 **New cracking sounds in walls** → seasonal settlement or moisture-related framing movement

NJ's older housing stock — and most of the inventory in Summit, Maplewood, Chatham, and Montclair is from 1920–1975 — has systems that talk when they're wearing out. Listen before your buyer's inspector does.

## Post 3: Home Odors That Kill NJ Real Estate Deals
**Blog:** `/blog/home-odors-kill-nj-home-sales-2026.html`

### Facebook Post
In my full-time NJ real estate work since 2017, I can tell you the most common thing buyers say when they don't make an offer: "It had a smell." They can't always name it. They don't always connect it to their decision. But they walk out and they don't come back.

The odors that cost NJ sellers the most — and what actually fixes each one:

🐾 **Pet odor** — enzyme cleaners for mild cases; floor refinishing or subfloor replacement for severe. Air fresheners don't fix this.
🚬 **Cigarette smoke** — the most expensive to remediate. Zinsser BIN primer on every surface, carpet replacement, duct cleaning. Budget $4,000–$15,000.
🏚️ **Musty basement** — fix the moisture source first (grading, gutters, drainage), then dehumidify consistently. The smell is mold. You have to address the moisture, not the smell.
🍳 **Cooking odors** — clean the range hood filter (when was the last time?), degrease cabinet walls, ventilate.
🌬️ **Stale closed-house smell** — replace the HVAC filter, open windows before showings, run an air purifier.
🗑️ **Garbage/drain odors** — clean the trash can, flush drains with baking soda, clean the dishwasher filter.

One thing: **don't use air fresheners.** Buyers are trained to be suspicious of strongly scented homes. The goal is neutral, not different.

## Article 1: The Only Washer Setting You Need: Laundry Habits That Protect an NJ Home Before You Sell

**Live URL:** https://thejorgeramirezgroup.com/blog/washer-setting-laundry-habits-nj-home-sellers-2026
**Image:** https://thejorgeramirezgroup.com/images/properties/05-kitchen.webp

### Facebook Post (100-150 words)

A laundry expert made the rounds this week saying you only need one washer setting: normal cycle, cold water. I agree, and not just for the energy bill.

Buyers open the laundry room door in every house I show, from a Westfield colonial to a second-floor closet in an Edison townhouse. So does the inspector. A sour-smelling washer, a lint-packed dryer vent or a puddle under a rubber hose tells them the house has been run casually, and that follows them into the offer.

Before you list: run normal and cold by default, clean the lint screen every load, have the vent cleaned to the outside, swap rubber hoses for braided steel, check the drain pan under an upstairs washer, and decide early whether the washer and dryer stay.

## Article 2: Patio Fruit in Pots: Container Gardens That Help NJ Balconies and Small Yards Show Better

**Live URL:** https://thejorgeramirezgroup.com/blog/container-fruit-garden-nj-patio-balcony-home-value-2026
**Image:** https://thejorgeramirezgroup.com/images/properties/02-staircase.webp
**Instagram image:** https://thejorgeramirezgroup.com/images/properties/02-staircase.webp

### Facebook Post (100-150 words)

Outdoor space sells in New Jersey, even when it is a balcony in Hoboken or a patio the size of a parking spot in Woodbridge. Buyers walk out, stand there for ten seconds and decide whether they would use it.

Three big pots of fruit do more for that decision than new furniture. Everbearing strawberries fruit the first season. Dwarf blueberries turn red in the fall. A potted fig looks established fast and winters in the garage. A Meyer lemon is a balcony plant in summer and a houseplant after frost.

Keep it simple: fewer, larger pots in one material, grouped near the door, on feet or saucers so nothing stains the pavers. Check your association's balcony rules. And decide early whether the pots stay, because buyers ask.

## Article 1: A Free Way to Divide an Open-Concept Space Before You List Your NJ Home

**Live URL:** https://thejorgeramirezgroup.com/blog/divide-open-concept-space-free-nj-home-sellers-2026
**Image:** https://thejorgeramirezgroup.com/images/properties/03-shingle-porch.webp
**Instagram image:** https://thejorgeramirezgroup.com/images/properties/03-shingle-porch.webp

### Facebook Post (100-150 words)

House Outlook shared a designer trick this week for dividing an open-concept space without spending a dollar: set the sofa and the dining table perpendicular to each other instead of parallel. The back of the sofa becomes the edge of the living zone, the table becomes the edge of the dining zone, and you never buy a screen or a bookcase.

I do the same thing before the photographer arrives. One big undefined room photographs smaller than two clear zones, and buyers touring an open plan in Westfield, Edison or Jersey City want to know where the dining table goes in the first ten seconds.

Pull the sofa off the wall, get the front legs of every seat on the rug, put a console behind it, and keep the path to the kitchen clear. Then take the shot from the entry with your phone.

## Article 2: 10 Questions to Ask During a NJ Home Inspection (and What the Answers Mean)

**Live URL:** https://thejorgeramirezgroup.com/blog/questions-to-ask-during-nj-home-inspection-2026
**Image:** https://thejorgeramirezgroup.com/images/editorial/nj-property-tax-research-2026-1280.webp
**Instagram image:** https://thejorgeramirezgroup.com/images/editorial/nj-property-tax-research-2026-1280.webp

### Facebook Post (100-150 words)

House Outlook published a list of questions to ask during a home inspection. Here is the New Jersey version I give my buyers.

The written report shows up two days later as sixty pages of photos. The questions you ask while you are standing in the basement are how you learn which three pages matter. So show up, and ask: How old is the roof and how many layers? Any evidence of water down here? Does the grading move water away from the house? What is the age of the boiler and water heater? Is the panel safe? Any sign of an oil tank? What is the plumbing made of? Any structural movement? Where do the bathroom fans vent? And the one that ranks the whole report: if this were your house, what would you fix first?

## Article 3: 10 Money-Pit Red Flags NJ Home Buyers Can Spot Before They Make an Offer

**Live URL:** https://thejorgeramirezgroup.com/blog/money-pit-red-flags-nj-home-buyers-2026
**Image:** https://thejorgeramirezgroup.com/images/properties/02-staircase.webp
**Instagram image:** https://thejorgeramirezgroup.com/images/properties/02-staircase.webp

### Facebook Post (100-150 words)

House Outlook listed the red flags that scream "money pit" to home buyers. In New Jersey you write the offer, get through attorney review, and then pay for the inspection, so the showing is the free look. Here is what I point out on tours.

Grading that slopes toward the house and downspouts that end at the wall. One freshly painted basement wall and a strong air freshener. Caulk smeared over cracks and extension cords through walls. Stair-step cracks in block. Curling shingles or a second roof layer. Tide marks in the basement. Two-prong outlets everywhere. A fill pipe in the yard with nothing connected to the chimney. A finished basement with no permit. And deferred maintenance everywhere you look.

None of these mean walk away. They mean price it, name the inspection, and ask.

## POST 2: House Handbook for NJ Sellers

### Facebook Post
Quick question: if I called you right now and asked what paint color your living room walls are, would you know the brand, the color name, and the sheen?

Most homeowners I work with cannot answer that. And it's not a big deal — until you're trying to sell and a buyer asks, or you need a touch-up before the first showing.

That's what a house handbook solves. It's a binder (or a Google Drive folder) where you keep all the information your home needs to function: appliance manuals with warranties, paint colors for every room, HVAC service records, contractor contacts, permits for any improvements you've made.

It costs nothing to create. And when you hand one to a buyer at closing, it signals something that changes their entire relationship to the sale: this home was cared for.

## POST 3: Furniture Placement Mistakes That Hurt NJ Home Value

### Facebook Post
Most NJ homes are set up backward. Not the appliances, not the plumbing — the furniture.

Sofas pushed flush against every wall. Oversized sectionals that eat 70% of the floor plan. Seating that faces away from the fireplace. Coffee tables that are 8 inches too tall. Furniture blocking every walking path.

I see this in every price range across Essex, Union, Morris, and Middlesex counties. And it costs sellers money — not because buyers consciously notice it, but because they feel it within 8 seconds of walking through the door. That unsettled feeling becomes a lower offer.

The good news: fixing your furniture arrangement is free. No contractors, no renovations. Pull the sofa 12 inches off the wall. Face the seating toward the fireplace. Remove 30% of the furniture from each room before photos.

I walk sellers through this every week. Full-time Realtor with Keller Williams Premier Properties since 2017. I know what NJ buyers are looking for the moment they step inside.

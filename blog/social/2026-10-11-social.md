### Facebook Post (100-150 words)

House Outlook's newsletter this week linked a guide to getting pet smells out of couches, bedding and the other soft surfaces a dog or cat lives on. For a seller, the stakes are higher than a fresh sofa. You stop smelling your own pet within weeks. A buyer walking into your Westfield colonial or Hoboken condo from fresh air notices in the first few seconds, and from that moment they read every scratch and every rug differently.

The order I use with clients: find the source with a UV flashlight, vacuum every soft surface, baking soda after checking the care code, enzyme cleaner on the actual spots, wash everything removable, pro-clean the big pieces, change the HVAC filter and air the house out. If the smell comes back, it is in the pad or subfloor, and that is a repair conversation, not a cleaning one.

## Article 2: Is Bouclé Out in 2026? What the Debate Means for a NJ Listing

### Facebook Post (100-150 words)

House Outlook's newsletter this week linked a Livingetc piece asking whether bouclé, the nubby looped fabric on half the sofas sold in 2020, is officially uncool in 2026. The designers' verdict: it has not disappeared, we just stopped covering every piece in it.

That is exactly the call I make with sellers before the photographer arrives. One clean bouclé chair in a room with a linen sofa reads as current. A bouclé sofa, bouclé pillows, bouclé stools and a bouclé ottoman in one frame reads to a buyer in Montclair or Summit as a house decorated in 2020, and that quietly dates the kitchen and baths too.

The rule: keep one bouclé piece per room, move the rest, and swap stained bouclé counter stools before the kitchen photo. Condition matters more than the trend.

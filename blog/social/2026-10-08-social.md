### Facebook Post (100-150 words)

House Outlook's newsletter this week opened with a list of seven places mold hides in a home. I read it the way I read everything now, as a New Jersey listing agent, and every spot on that list is one a buyer's inspector goes to first.

Under the bathroom vanity. The lower corners of old window sashes. Behind the washer. The base of the basement walls in a Cranford or Montclair colonial with a stone foundation. The HVAC drip pan. The attic deck above a bathroom fan that never got vented outside. Behind the fridge, where the ice-maker line drips for a year.

You can live with a damp corner. You cannot sell past one. A buyer who finds mold stops thinking about the kitchen and starts thinking about water. Find it first, fix the moisture, keep the invoices, and never paint over it.

### Facebook Post (100-150 words)

House Outlook's newsletter this week linked a Houzz roundup of twenty-five old-fashioned house details worth keeping or reimagining. Dutch doors, dumbwaiters, built-ins, transoms. In Montclair, Maplewood, Westfield and Summit, where so much of the housing stock predates 1940, that is not a nostalgia piece. It is a list of what buyers pay for.

The buyer who picks Glen Ridge over a newer subdivision is picking the house: the trim, the built-in china cabinet, the pocket doors, the inlaid floor. Intact, those details put the house up against the best listings on the street. Ripped out for a generic finish, it competes on price alone.

Keep the built-ins, the transoms, the paneling and the radiators. Give the phone nook and the butler's pantry a new job. And fix the wiring, the oil tank and the sewer line first, because charm covers nothing at an inspection.

## Article 3: 10 DIY Projects That Can Sink Your NJ Home's Resale Value

**Live URL:** https://thejorgeramirezgroup.com/blog/diy-projects-sink-resale-value-nj-home-sellers-2026
**Image:** https://thejorgeramirezgroup.com/images/editorial/nj-housing-comparison-2026-1280.webp
**Instagram image:** https://thejorgeramirezgroup.com/images/editorial/nj-housing-comparison-2026-1280.webp

### Facebook Post (100-150 words)

House Outlook's newsletter this week pointed to a list of ten home projects that can sink your resale value when you do them yourself. I have seen most of them at New Jersey inspections. The owner saved money on a Saturday and gave it back, with interest, at the buyer's inspection or the town's resale inspection.

New Jersey adds a cost the article did not cover: the permit. Most towns, Westfield, Cranford, Union and Woodbridge among them, run a resale inspection, and work done without a permit can surface there, right before closing.

Hand off electrical beyond a fixture swap, plumbing inside walls, structural changes, decks, roofing, wet-area tile, floor refinishing, window replacement, finished basements and gas hookups. Paint, caulk, hardware, nail holes and landscaping are yours. And pull your own permit history before you list.

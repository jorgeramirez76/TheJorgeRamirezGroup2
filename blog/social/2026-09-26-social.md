### Facebook Post (100-150 words)

House Outlook's newsletter linked a piece this week on the one refrigerator shelf that spoils groceries: the door. It swings into a warm kitchen every time it opens, so milk and eggs stored there go bad sooner. Good grocery advice. Better listing advice.

In most New Jersey sales the refrigerator stays with the house, and buyers open it during showings. I have watched buyers in Summit and Maplewood open the fridge before a single cabinet. They notice smell, a torn gasket, frost in the freezer, a noisy compressor and a door packed with jars. Any of that reads as a tired appliance, and they carry the impression into the rest of the house.

Empty it, wash under the crisper drawers, run the dollar bill test on the gasket, vacuum the coils and restock light. One afternoon.

## Article 2: Dryer Balls, Dryer Vents and the Laundry Room: What NJ Buyers and Inspectors Check

**Live URL:** https://thejorgeramirezgroup.com/blog/dryer-balls-dryer-vent-laundry-room-nj-home-sellers-2026
**Image:** https://thejorgeramirezgroup.com/images/properties/01-loft-living.webp
**Instagram image:** https://thejorgeramirezgroup.com/images/properties/01-loft-living.webp

### Facebook Post (100-150 words)

Do wool dryer balls go bad? House Outlook's newsletter linked a piece on it this week. Yes, after two or three years, when they pill, shed or lose their bounce. Until then a hot wash and a high-heat dry brings them back.

The reason a real estate agent cares is what sits behind the dryer. A New Jersey home inspector checks the vent on nearly every house I sell, from a split-level in Union to a colonial in Chatham. Plastic or foil flex duct, a long crushed run to a second-floor laundry room, or an exterior hood packed with lint all get written up as a safety item, and safety items become buyer requests.

Swap the duct for metal, clean the run, confirm the outside flap opens, and drop the dryer sheets. They coat the lint screen and the moisture sensor.

Getting a New Jersey home ready to list? Check the dryer vent before the inspector does. Plastic or foil flex duct, a long run to an upstairs laundry room, and a lint-packed exterior hood all get written up as safety items, and those turn into buyer repair requests. Replace the duct with rigid metal, clean the run to the outside, and confirm the hood flap opens when the dryer runs. Then wash the lint screen and switch from dryer sheets to wool balls.

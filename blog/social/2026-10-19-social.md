## Article 1: The Floor-to-Ceiling Curtain Trick That Makes NJ Rooms Look Taller in Listing Photos, and Where It Backfires

**Live URL:** https://thejorgeramirezgroup.com/blog/floor-to-ceiling-curtains-taller-rooms-nj-home-sellers-2026
**Image:** https://thejorgeramirezgroup.com/images/properties/07-dining.webp (crop to 1200×630)

### Facebook Post (100-150 words)

House Outlook linked a curtain trick this week that I have been recommending to sellers for years: mount the rod just under the ceiling, run it past the window frame on both sides, and let the panels fall to the floor. The eye reads one tall vertical line and the room looks higher and brighter than it is.

That matters in New Jersey, where so many of the splits, capes and ranches in Cranford, Clark, Scotch Plains and Edison have eight-foot ceilings and low windows. Buyers coming from a tall-windowed rental in Hoboken notice the ceiling first. Curtains change what the eye believes for the price of a rod and two panels.

Three places to skip it: over baseboard heat or radiators, in front of original woodwork in an older Maplewood or Montclair colonial, and in kitchens, baths and sunrooms. Full guide: thejorgeramirezgroup.com/blog/floor-to-ceiling-curtains-taller-rooms-nj-home-sellers-2026

## Article 2: Hanging Pictures Straight and at the Right Height: The Wall Art Rules NJ Sellers Should Follow Before Listing Photos

**Live URL:** https://thejorgeramirezgroup.com/blog/hanging-pictures-right-height-wall-art-nj-home-sellers-2026
**Image:** https://thejorgeramirezgroup.com/images/properties/03-shingle-porch.webp (crop to 1200×630)
**Instagram image:** https://thejorgeramirezgroup.com/images/properties/03-shingle-porch.webp (crop to 1080×1080)

### Facebook Post (100-150 words)

House Outlook linked a list of ten tricks for hanging pictures level and centered: paper templates, painter’s tape on the hanger, a real level, art centered at 57 inches. Good advice for anyone. For a seller it is a different job.

Most art in the houses I list in Westfield, Montclair and Morristown hangs too high and too small, and a lone frame floating above a sofa makes the whole room look unfinished in photos. Center it at eye level, drop it to six to eight inches above the furniture, and make it about two thirds the width of what it hangs over.

Then take down the family photos, the diplomas and anything with your name on it, use two hooks so frames stay level, and patch every hole you made. Full guide: thejorgeramirezgroup.com/blog/hanging-pictures-right-height-wall-art-nj-home-sellers-2026

## Article 3: Kitchen Items You Are Storing Wrong, According to Chefs: A Cabinet-by-Cabinet Reset Before NJ Buyers Open the Doors

### Facebook Post (100-150 words)

House Outlook linked a piece on the kitchen items chefs say the rest of us store wrong: knives loose in a drawer, nonstick pans stacked bare, spices cooking over the stove, cast iron put away wet. The article is about protecting your gear. I read it as a seller’s checklist.

Buyers in Summit, Edison and Hoboken open every cabinet and drawer at a showing. A drawer that will not close over a tangle of knives and a cabinet packed to the top shelf tell them the kitchen is small, whatever the square footage.

Knives in a block or insert, pans racked with air around them, spices out of the heat, glassware upright, nothing under the sink that cannot get wet, and visible space on every shelf. One afternoon.

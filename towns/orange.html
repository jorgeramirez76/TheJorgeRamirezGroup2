Town-specific property review

Verify the approved unit count before relying on a multifamily label

A listing description, current layout, or prior use does not by itself establish the legally approved number of units. Send each question to the office that maintains the relevant record and keep the answer tied to the exact property.

Zoning and existing use

Use the City's Zoning Division as an official starting point for the district, permitted-use, dimensional, and application questions that apply to the parcel. Ask what current record or approval supports the represented use.

Planning record versus proposal

Use Planning and Economic Development materials to identify adopted plans, board information, and public planning resources. A proposal, hearing, or planning document is not proof that a particular project was approved or completed.

Permits, certificates, and observed layout

For a property marketed as multifamily, ask the responsible City offices which construction, housing, certificate, fire, or permit records apply. Reconcile the municipal response with title, inspection, appraisal, lender, and attorney review before relying on a unit count.

Buyer and seller evidence

Prepare a City of Orange Township property record before marketing

A seller can reduce avoidable ambiguity by matching the marketing description to the municipal and property documents that actually exist. This workflow does not promise approval, timing, price, or a transaction result.

  • Confirm the City of Orange Township address, block and lot, ownership record, and property type.
  • Verify the represented unit count and use with the responsible City offices rather than relying on an old listing.
  • Collect available permits, certificates, board approvals, surveys, title material, and improvement documentation.
  • Separate current comparable evidence by property type, condition, approved use, location, and date.

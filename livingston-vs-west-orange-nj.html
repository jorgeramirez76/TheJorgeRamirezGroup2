Livingston vs West Orange, NJ: Which Is Right for You?

Two Essex County townships that share a border and almost nothing else. One is a school-district buy at $795,000. The other is the value play at $559,000. Neither has a train station in town.

The Short Answer

Buy in West Orange if you want the most house per dollar in Essex County and a shorter path to a train: median $559,000, roughly 45 minutes to Penn Station. Buy in Livingston if schools are the whole reason you're moving and you'll accept a drive to the station and a $236,000 higher median.

Livingston is a consistently top-ranked district with well-maintained neighborhoods, and buyers pay for that directly. West Orange is where you go when you want square footage, variety in housing stock, and a lower tax bill, and you don't need a 9/10 rating to say yes.

Choose Livingston if…

The school district is the reason you are moving, full stop. Livingston is a consistently top-ranked community with well-maintained neighborhoods and a strong sense of continuity — the kind of town people move into for kindergarten and leave after graduation. The 9/10 district rating is what the $795,000 median is buying, and buyers here know it.

Be honest with yourself about the commute. There is no station in the township. Most Livingston commuters drive 10 to 15 minutes to South Orange, Millburn or Short Hills and park, or ride an NJ Transit bus to Port Authority. That means a car, a parking permit or a daily lot fee, and a trip that the town snapshot puts around 55 minutes to Penn. If you are in the office four or five days a week, add that up honestly before you fall for a house.

Best fit: Families buying for a specific school run, hybrid workers going in two days a week, two-car households, buyers who would rather drive to the train than pay Midtown Direct walk-to-station pricing.

Choose West Orange if…

You want space and you want to stop stretching. At $559,000 median, West Orange offers more square footage per dollar than Essex County's premium markets while still delivering an 8/10 district and a workable commute. It is a big, varied township — housing stock ranges widely by section, which is a feature if you are willing to look and a problem if you expect every street to be the same.

Commute-wise it is the better of the two on paper. West Orange buyers commuting to Manhattan generally use Highland Avenue or Mountain Station on the Midtown Direct line, roughly 45 minutes to Penn, and NJ Transit express bus routes serve the parts of the township farther from a station.

Best fit: First-time buyers and value seekers, young families who want a yard now rather than in five years, buyers who need four bedrooms under $700K, anyone who wants Essex County access without Millburn or Montclair pricing.

How long is the commute from Livingston and West Orange to NYC?

Start with the fact that shapes both answers: neither township has an NJ Transit rail station on the station list of our NJ Transit train map. Everyone in both towns either drives to a station, takes a bus, or both. A buyer who assumes there is a station in town finds out at the first showing.

West Orange comes out roughly ten minutes ahead. Its market snapshot lists ~45 minutes to New York Penn Station, and West Orange commuters typically board at Highland Avenue or Mountain Station on the Midtown Direct line. NJ Transit bus routes to Port Authority cover the parts of the township that are not convenient to either station.

Livingston lists ~55 minutes and is a genuine drive-and-park town: most commuters go 10 to 15 minutes to South Orange, Millburn or Short Hills, or take the bus. That extra leg is not just time — it is a parking spot you have to secure, a second car you probably need, and a schedule that breaks if the lot fills.

Do the arithmetic on cost, not just minutes. Between the monthly rail pass, station parking and the occasional ticket, a full-time NJ commuter typically spends several thousand dollars a year on top of the mortgage. What the NJ-to-NYC commute actually costs breaks that down, and the NYC to NJ commute guide covers the bus and drive-to-station options both towns depend on.

What are the honest trade-offs?

You are paying $236,000 at the median for one point of school rating. That is the whole trade in a sentence: 9/10 versus 8/10, $795,000 versus $559,000. Whether that is worth it depends entirely on how many kids you have, how long they will be in the system, and what you would otherwise spend that money on. For some families it is obviously worth it. For a couple without kids, it plainly is not.

Livingston costs more to hold, too. The 2025 average residential bill was $18,465 in Livingston against $16,162 in West Orange — $2,303 a year, roughly $190 a month, on top of the larger mortgage. The price gap and the tax gap point the same way.

District ratings hide street-level swings. In Livingston, two homes a quarter-mile apart can differ by six figures based on school catchment and how close they sit to the jitney or to Short Hills and South Orange train access. In West Orange the same thing happens around bus express routes and South Orange station access. Get the exact catchment boundary in writing before you offer, in either town.

West Orange is not one market. The 8/10 rating and the $559,000 median describe a large, mixed township. Sections of it look and price nothing like each other. That is why the value is there — and why you should not shop it from a listing site alone.

Neither town is slow. Fourteen days on market in Livingston, sixteen in West Orange. Essex County home prices are up 4.2% year over year in the Q2 2026 county report, with West Orange named among the towns seeing strong activity. Be pre-approved before you tour.

Frequently asked questions

Is Livingston or West Orange more expensive?
Livingston, by $236,000 at the median — $795,000 against $559,000 — plus $2,303 more per year in average property taxes.

Does Livingston have a train station?
No. Livingston commuters drive 10 to 15 minutes to South Orange, Millburn or Short Hills and park, or take an NJ Transit bus to Port Authority. The snapshot figure to Penn is roughly 55 minutes.

Which has the better commute?
West Orange, by about ten minutes. Roughly 45 minutes to Penn using Highland Avenue or Mountain Station on Midtown Direct, against roughly 55 from Livingston.

Are the schools better in Livingston?
Livingston rates 9/10 and West Orange 8/10 on GreatSchools. Both are district averages — verify the specific elementary catchment for any address before you offer.

Which gives more house for the money?
West Orange, clearly. It delivers more square footage per dollar than Essex County's premium markets while keeping local schools and a usable commute.

What will it actually cost me monthly?
Price plus taxes plus commute, not price alone. Run it with the mortgage calculator and the closing costs calculator, and look up any NJ town's average bill in the NJ property tax guide.

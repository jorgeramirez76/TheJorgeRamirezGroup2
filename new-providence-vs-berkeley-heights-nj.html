New Providence vs Berkeley Heights, NJ: Which Is Right for You?

Berkeley Heights costs less on both lines that matter: a $739,000 median against New Providence's $779,000, and a $14,152 average tax bill against $16,228. New Providence buys back three minutes to Penn Station — 47 versus 50 — on the same Gladstone Branch one-seat ride. Both districts rate 9/10.

Who each town is actually for

Berkeley Heights suits the buyer optimizing for total cost of ownership: $40,000 less at closing and roughly $2,076 less every year in property tax, for a district rated exactly the same. New Providence suits the buyer who rides the train daily and wants the shorter trip, the second station, and the faster recent appreciation.

These are neighboring Union County towns on the same branch line with the same 9/10 rating. This is not a good-town-versus-weak-town comparison. It's an argument about three minutes and about $2,000 a year.

Choose New Providence if…

You are on that train five days a week and three minutes each way is worth $40,000 to you. New Providence station is 47 minutes to New York Penn on the Gladstone Branch — a one-seat ride, no Newark transfer — with a $362 monthly pass. The town also carries a second stop at Murray Hill, 49 minutes, which matters a great deal if the house you like sits on that side of town.

It has also been the stronger performer lately: +8.4% year over year through Q1 2026 against Berkeley Heights' +7.6%. Homes go in 11 days on average, and the district feeding New Providence High School rates 9/10. You pay for all of it with the higher median and a $16,228 average tax bill.

Ideal for: Daily rail commuters who want the shortest Gladstone Branch trip and a second station option, and buyers who weight recent appreciation over entry price.

Choose Berkeley Heights if…

You want the same school rating for less money in both directions. Berkeley Heights' median is $739,000 — $40,000 under New Providence — and the average residential tax bill is $14,152, the lowest of any town on this page and about $2,076 a year below its neighbor. The district feeds Governor Livingston High School and rates the same 9/10.

What you give up is three minutes to Penn Station, 50 against 47, and a verified fare: our train map has no monthly pass on file for Berkeley Heights, so budget it off NJ Transit's own numbers rather than mine. Homes average 12 days on market — the most patient market in this comparison, by one day.

Ideal for: Buyers who drive more than they ride, families who want the 9/10 district at the lowest carrying cost, and anyone stretching to reach a Union County commuter town at all.

How do the NYC commutes compare?

Both towns sit on the Gladstone Branch, which splits from the Morris & Essex line at Summit and runs into New York Penn as a one-seat ride. New Providence is 47 minutes, Murray Hill 49, Berkeley Heights 50. The verified monthly rail pass from New Providence is $362; the map carries no fare for Berkeley Heights, so I'm not printing one.

One discrepancy worth knowing about, because it changes the shape of your morning: both community pages on this site list the NJ Transit service as "Raritan Valley," while the station-level train map data puts both towns on the Gladstone Branch. That is not a trivial difference — the Raritan Valley Line requires a transfer at Newark Penn most hours, and the Gladstone Branch does not. The station dataset is the more specific source and it's what I've used here, but confirm your exact train on NJ Transit's timetable before you treat a one-seat ride as a given.

Both towns also have direct I-78 access, and New Providence adds Route 24. If half your week is a drive to a corporate campus rather than a ride into Manhattan, that changes the calculus more than three minutes of rail time does.

What are the honest trade-offs?

The tax gap eats the price gap in about 19 years

Berkeley Heights is $40,000 cheaper to buy and about $2,076 a year cheaper to hold. At that rate the annual saving alone covers the purchase-price difference in roughly nineteen years — and it keeps running after that. If you are buying a long-term family house rather than a five-year stop, that math points one direction. If you're planning to move again inside a decade, the three-minute commute and the stronger appreciation number point the other.

Same rating, different schools

9/10 and 9/10 is a genuine tie on paper. New Providence High School and Governor Livingston are separate schools in separate districts, and a matching district average tells you nothing about program depth or the specific elementary catchment your address feeds. Pull the NJ Department of Education report for both before you let the number stand in for research.

What I could not verify

No monthly rail pass for Berkeley Heights — the station dataset has that field empty, and I would rather leave a cell blank than fill it with a plausible-looking number. And the line-name conflict above is unresolved between two sources on this same site; treat it as a question to ask NJ Transit, not a settled fact.

A median is not a price list

$779,000 and $739,000 are town-wide midpoints. Berkeley Heights is the more spread-out town, so the same dollar often buys more land there — but "often" is not "always," and only side-by-side comps on the streets you'd actually buy on will tell you whether that holds for your budget.

New Providence vs Berkeley Heights: common questions

Which town is cheaper?

Berkeley Heights, on both counts: a $739,000 median against $779,000, and a $14,152 average tax bill against $16,228.

Which has the faster NYC commute?

New Providence, at 47 minutes to New York Penn against Berkeley Heights' 50, both one-seat on the Gladstone Branch. New Providence's Murray Hill station is 49 minutes.

Do they share a school district?

No. New Providence High School and Governor Livingston High School serve separate districts, both rated 9/10.

Which town has appreciated faster?

New Providence, at +8.4% year over year through Q1 2026 against Berkeley Heights' +7.6%, per the train map station dataset.

Maplewood vs Montclair, NJ: Which Is Right for You?

Choose Maplewood if the commute is what you optimize: 33 minutes to Penn Station on Midtown Direct, a $698,000 median, and a $19,378 average tax bill. Choose Montclair if you want the bigger arts-and-dining town and will pay for it — an $875,000 median, a 9/10 district, and $22,487 in taxes.

Who each town is actually for

Maplewood suits the buyer whose day is organized around a train: one seat to Midtown, the lowest carrying cost of the two, and $177,000 less at closing. Montclair suits the buyer who wants the town itself to be the amenity — a deeper downtown, a 9/10 district — and who has run the math on paying roughly $3,100 more in property tax every year, forever.

These are both Essex County towns and both are fast markets. Nine days and ten days. Whichever you pick, you are deciding in a weekend, not a month.

Choose Maplewood if…

The train is the axis your week turns on. Maplewood station is a 33-minute Midtown Direct ride to New York Penn — one seat, no transfer at Newark — and the monthly pass runs $276. At a $698,000 median you're in for $177,000 less than Montclair, and the average residential tax bill is $19,378 against $22,487. That's roughly $3,100 a year back before you've furnished a room.

The trade you're accepting is the district rating: 8/10 here against Montclair's 9/10, feeding Columbia High School. Homes average 10 days on market, so the money you save does not buy you time to think.

Ideal for: Commuters who ride five days a week and want the shortest one-seat trip in this comparison, plus buyers who care more about monthly carrying cost than about a one-point district rating.

Choose Montclair if…

You want a town that functions like a small city rather than a village with a train stop. The $875,000 median buys a 9/10 district feeding Montclair High School and a downtown with restaurant and arts density Maplewood Village doesn't attempt.

You pay for that twice — once at closing and again every year. Montclair's average residential bill is $22,487, up 4.0% from $21,631 in 2024, with $12,912 of it funding schools. And the market is the tighter of the two at 9 days on market: the premium price does not buy a calmer search.

Ideal for: Buyers who want walkable nightlife, arts, and dining as a daily amenity, and families weighting the 9/10 district — with the tax bill priced into the monthly number from day one.

How do the NYC commutes really compare?

Maplewood is the cleaner commute and it isn't close on paper. The NJ train map clocks Maplewood station at 33 minutes to New York Penn on Midtown Direct, with a $276 monthly rail pass. The Maplewood community page rounds the same trip to about 35 minutes; I've used the station-level figure here because that's the one tied to an actual line and stop.

Montclair rides the Montclair-Boonton line. Its community page puts Manhattan at roughly 38 minutes in the market snapshot and 40 minutes in the at-a-glance table — call it high-30s to 40 and verify against a real timetable.

Here's what I won't do: quote you a Montclair monthly pass. Our interactive map covers four lines — Midtown Direct, the Gladstone Branch, the Raritan Valley Line, and the Northeast Corridor — and Montclair-Boonton isn't one of them. I don't have a verified fare for Montclair's stations, so I'm not going to invent one. Price your exact station on NJ Transit before you sign, because a monthly pass is a thirty-year cost, not a rounding error.

One thing the timetable won't tell you: how far the house is from the platform. A seven-minute walk versus a drive-and-park changes the commute more than the two-to-five minute gap between these two towns ever will.

What are the honest trade-offs?

This comparison is closer than the price gap suggests, and there are three things I'd want a buyer to hear before they fall for either town.

The medians hide enormous spread

$698,000 and $875,000 are town-wide medians. In both towns a renovated house on a good street trades well above them and a house needing systems work trades well below. Neither number tells you what the specific home you want will cost. Pull comps within a half-mile and inside the same catchment.

The tax gap compounds and the price gap doesn't

You pay the $177,000 price difference once. You pay the roughly $3,100 tax difference every year, and both towns' bills rose about 4% year over year — Maplewood +4.1%, Montclair +4.0%. Run it out ten years before you decide the Montclair premium is only a down-payment problem.

What I could not verify

No monthly rail fare for Montclair, because our station dataset doesn't cover the Montclair-Boonton line. And a district rating — 8/10 or 9/10 — is a district average, not your school. In Montclair especially, where the district runs a magnet structure, the address-to-school relationship is worth an hour of your own research before you write an offer.

Maplewood vs Montclair: common questions

Is Maplewood or Montclair cheaper?

Maplewood, on both axes. The median is $698,000 against Montclair's $875,000, and the average residential tax bill is $19,378 against $22,487 — about $3,100 a year apart.

Which has the faster NYC commute?

Maplewood. Maplewood station is 33 minutes to New York Penn on Midtown Direct with no transfer and a $276 monthly pass. Montclair's community page puts Manhattan at roughly 38 to 40 minutes on the Montclair-Boonton line.

Which town has better schools?

On district-wide rating, Montclair at 9/10 against Maplewood's 8/10. Maplewood feeds Columbia High School, Montclair feeds Montclair High School. Check the NJ Department of Education report for your exact catchment before you let one point decide a $177,000 question.

How fast do homes sell in each town?

Montclair averages 9 days on market, Maplewood 10. Both are fast enough that you should be pre-approved and ready to tour the weekend a listing hits.

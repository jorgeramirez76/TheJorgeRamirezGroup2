Real Talk

Questions People Actually Ask Before Reaching Out

No fluff. Just honest answers to what's really on your mind.

I'm not sure I'm ready to sell. Is it worth talking to you?

An early conversation can clarify your goals, a current pricing range, possible costs, and the information still needed to plan. There is no obligation to list, and deciding to wait is a valid outcome.

What makes you different from every other agent who says the same thing?

Fair question. My answer is the process: define your priorities, work from current property and comparable-sale information, make the tradeoffs visible, and keep the next decision clear. I've worked full-time at Keller Williams since 2017, and I'll give you a direct explanation of what I see.

How do I decide whether now is the right time to buy?

Mortgage rates, inventory, prices, taxes, and insurance costs change. Start with a current lender estimate and a payment range you are comfortable with, then compare that budget with the properties and locations available now.

If I sell, where am I supposed to go? Everything is expensive.

Plan the destination, budget, and timing before the listing goes live. Depending on your goals, that may mean buying first, selling first, coordinating both contracts, renting temporarily, or waiting; each option has different costs and risks.

Which NJ towns do you work in?

I work with buyers and sellers across Essex, Hudson, Morris, Middlesex, Union, and Somerset counties. I'm based in Summit and focus especially on commuter towns including Summit, Millburn, Chatham, Maplewood, South Orange, Madison, Westfield, and Cranford. Use the community directory to explore the available town guides.

I'm a first-time buyer. Will I feel out of my depth?

First-time buyers can ask questions at every stage. I'll explain the steps, help you compare properties and offer terms, and coordinate with the lender, inspector, attorney, and other professionals you select so you know what happens next.

How do I find out what my NJ home is actually worth?

Start with our free home valuation page to request a free CMA. A comparative market analysis looks at what similar homes in your specific neighborhood have sold for, what's currently under contract, and what's sitting unsold. That local comparison gives you a pricing range you can use to plan. The request is free and carries no obligation.

My home was listed before and didn't sell. Can you help?

Yes. A review can compare the prior pricing history, photos, property description, showing feedback, offer activity, and current market conditions before recommending a new plan. Learn more about the seller process.

What is the best time to sell a home in New Jersey?

There is no universal best month. The right timing depends on your move, property preparation, carrying costs, current competing inventory, recent local sales, and the buyers active when you list. Review current town-level data before choosing a launch date.

How much does it cost to sell a home in New Jersey?

Costs vary by property and agreement. Possible items include brokerage compensation, which is negotiable and not set by law; the New Jersey Realty Transfer Fee; legal fees; mortgage payoffs; municipal requirements; repairs or credits; moving; and taxes. Ask each provider for a current written estimate and consult your attorney or tax adviser about your situation.

How long does it take to sell a home in NJ?

The timeline varies with property preparation, buyer activity, contract terms, attorney review, inspections, financing, appraisal, title work, municipal requirements, and the parties' chosen closing date. Your New Jersey attorney should explain the contract language and legal deadlines that apply to your transaction.

What is my home worth in Summit, Westfield, or Chatham NJ?

A useful pricing review considers recent comparable sales, active and pending competition, property features, condition, location, and current market conditions. Townwide medians and automated estimates do not account for every property difference. Request a current comparative market analysis.

Should I use a realtor or sell my home myself in NJ?

Compare the services, fees, time commitment, marketing access, showing process, negotiation support, and transaction coordination available with each option. New Jersey and federal forms or disclosures may apply depending on the property and transaction, so ask a New Jersey attorney which legal requirements apply. A licensed agent can explain the brokerage services and negotiable compensation offered without promising a particular sale result.

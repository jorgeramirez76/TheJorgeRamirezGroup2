Summit vs New Providence, NJ: Which Is Right for You?

Summit suits buyers who want a small-city downtown, a 10/10 district, and the Midtown Direct main line at a $1,095,000 median. New Providence suits buyers who want 9/10 schools and two stations of their own for $779,000, three minutes further out on the Gladstone Branch and $3,469 lighter in average tax.

Jorge Ramirez · NJ Real Estate License #1754604 · Keller Williams Premier Properties · Updated August 6, 2026

Who is Summit for, and who is New Providence for?

My office is at 488 Springfield Avenue in Summit, so I make this drive constantly. The two towns are neighbors in Union County, three listed minutes apart by rail, and they charge the exact same $362 monthly pass to New York Penn Station. What separates them is $316,000 at the median and about $3,500 a year in average property taxes.

Summit is the buyer who wants a downtown with retail, dining, and office space in walking distance — a small city rather than a suburb — plus a 10/10 district, and who is buying above $1M anyway. New Providence is the buyer who wants a 9/10 district and its own high school, has a budget in the high $700s to high $800s, and correctly notices that the train ride is nearly identical.

Choose Summit if…

You want to live where you can walk to dinner, the train, and an office. Summit's downtown along Springfield Avenue is a genuine mixed-use district — retail, restaurants, and office space, not a strip of storefronts — and the residential streets behind it are established and tree-lined. The district rates 10/10 through Summit High School, and the station is a Midtown Direct stop at 44 listed minutes to New York Penn.

The trade you accept: a $1,095,000 median, a $19,697 average tax bill, and a 10-day market that is one of the fastest in the corridor.

Best fit: Buyers above $1M who want walkability as a daily fact rather than a weekend outing, professionals splitting time between a Manhattan office and a local one, and empty nesters trading a big lot for a short walk.

Choose New Providence if…

You want the same commute economics without the Summit price. New Providence is one stop out on the Gladstone Branch — 47 listed minutes to New York Penn against Summit's 44 — and NJ Transit charges the identical $362 monthly pass. The median is $779,000, roughly $316,000 under Summit, and the average tax bill runs $3,469 a year lighter. The district rates 9/10 and runs through its own New Providence High School.

The trade you accept: a residential town rather than a downtown town, and a school rating one point below Summit's.

Best fit: Families who want a self-contained district and more house for the money, and commuters who realize three minutes is not worth $316,000.

What is the NYC commute like from Summit and New Providence?

This is the strongest argument for New Providence, and most buyers miss it. The Gladstone Branch splits off the Morris & Essex Main Line at Summit — so a New Providence train literally runs through Summit on its way east. That is why the listed times are 44 minutes from Summit and 47 from New Providence, and why NJ Transit's New York Penn Station chart prices both at the same $362 a month.

Three minutes and zero dollars. That is the entire commute penalty for a $316,000 lower median. If someone tells you New Providence is "much further out," check the timetable and the fare chart on my NJ train map before you believe it.

New Providence also has two platforms rather than one — New Providence station at 47 listed minutes and Murray Hill at 49. In a town where most people drive to the station, having two changes which streets are genuinely walk-to-train, and that affects resale. Worth knowing before you fall for a specific house.

How do the schools compare in Summit and New Providence?

Summit Public Schools rates 10/10 on GreatSchools and runs through Summit High School. New Providence Public Schools rates 9/10 and runs through New Providence High School. Both are complete K-12 districts — neither town sends its high schoolers somewhere else, which is not true of every Union County commuter town.

A one-point district gap is narrower than it looks on a search results page, and district averages hide elementary-level variation in both towns. Check the specific catchment for the address you are considering rather than the district-wide number.

What are the honest trade-offs between Summit and New Providence?

Against Summit

You are paying about $316,000 more at the median and about $3,469 more per year in average property taxes for one point of school rating and three minutes of train time. Almost none of that premium is buying you commute. It is buying you the downtown. If your household eats out twice a month and drives everywhere else, you are paying a walkability premium you will not use. Summit also moves in 10 days, so you will be making decisions fast and often against other offers.

Against New Providence

The downtown is not comparable, and no amount of enthusiasm makes it so. If you want a Friday night within walking distance, you will be driving to Summit or Berkeley Heights to get it — which means you have New Providence's tax bill and Summit's parking problem. The 9/10 district is strong but it is not Summit's 10/10, and if you plan to sell in five years, some buyers will price that difference. At 11 days on market, it is barely slower than Summit, so do not expect a relaxed search either.

Against both

Both towns sit in the same fare zone and both are effectively 45-minute towns. If your budget is under $779,000, the answer is probably neither — it is a stop further out on the Gladstone Branch or a different line entirely. Summit vs New Providence: frequently asked questions

Is New Providence cheaper than Summit?

Yes — about $316,000 cheaper at the median, $779,000 against $1,095,000. The average residential property tax bill is $16,228 in New Providence against $19,697 in Summit, a difference of $3,469 a year.

Are Summit and New Providence on the same line?

Summit is on the Morris & Essex Main Line with Midtown Direct service. New Providence is on the Gladstone Branch, which splits from the main line at Summit — so the trains share track east of Summit. Listed times are 44 and 47 minutes to New York Penn, and both stations carry the same $362 monthly pass.

Does New Providence have two train stations?

Yes: New Providence at a listed 47 minutes and Murray Hill at 49. Summit has one station.

Which town has better schools?

Summit, by one point on GreatSchools — 10/10 against 9/10. Both are complete K-12 districts ending at their own high school. Verify the specific elementary catchment before you buy; the district number is an average, not a guarantee about your street.

Which sells faster?

Summit, barely: 10 days typical time on market against New Providence's 11. Treat both as one-weekend markets.

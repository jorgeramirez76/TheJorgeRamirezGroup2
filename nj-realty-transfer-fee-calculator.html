NJ Realty Transfer Fee — the full 2026 rate schedule

The NJ RTF is tiered, meaning different portions of your sale price are taxed at different rates (similar to federal income tax brackets). You pay the combined total at closing.

$0 – $150,000$2.90 per $500 (0.58%)
$150,000 – $200,000$4.25 per $500 (0.85%)
$200,000 – $550,000$4.80 per $500 (0.96%)
$550,000 – $850,000$5.30 per $500 (1.06%)
$850,000 – $1,000,000$5.80 per $500 (1.16%)
Over $1,000,000$6.05 per $500 (1.21%)

$0 – $150,000$2.00 per $500 (0.40%)
$150,000 – $200,000$3.35 per $500 (0.67%)

$0 – $150,000$1.40 per $500
$150,000 – $550,000$2.15 per $500
$550,000 – $850,000$2.65 per $500

Charged in marginal bands, so each rate applies only to the portion of the price inside that band. Which schedule you use depends on the whole sale price: a $600,000 sale uses the over-$350,000 schedule for all its bands. Worked totals at the standard rate: $4,175 at $500,000, $5,185 at $600,000, $9,575 at $1,000,000. Above $1,000,000 the seller also owes the Graduated Percent Fee, which has no reduced rate.
*Reduced rate applies to qualifying sellers aged 62 or over, blind or permanently disabled, and to low-and-moderate-income housing. Note the reduced schedule has different breakpoints from the standard one — $2.15 runs all the way to $550,000. It applies to the entire consideration, not only the portion above $350,000, and requires an Affidavit of Consideration (Form RTF-1) filed with the deed. At $600,000 the reduced fee is $2,405 against $5,185 standard, a saving of $2,780. Schedules per the NJ Division of Taxation (nj.gov/treasury/taxation/realty.shtml, updated 05/21/26).

Who qualifies for the reduced rate?

Senior citizens (62+), permanently disabled individuals, and blind sellers qualify if the home is their primary residence and they're selling directly to a third party (not to family or through a trust transfer). Must file Form RTF-1 at closing.

Low- or moderate-income homeowners qualify if they meet the state's income thresholds AND the home was their primary residence AND they're not using the proceeds to buy another home worth more than the one being sold.

Separate: the Graduated Percent Fee (the old “Mansion Tax”) — now YOURS

This reversed in 2025. The 1% “mansion tax” on consideration in excess of $1,000,000 used to be paid by the buyer. P.L. 2025, c. 69 struck “grantee” and inserted “grantor” in N.J.S.A. 46:15-7.2, moving the fee to the seller and making it graduated. It applies to deeds submitted for recording on or after July 10, 2025. New Jersey REALTORS® put it plainly: the Graduated Percent Fee “will now be entirely paid for by the seller.”

It is a cliff, not a bracket — each rate applies to the entire sale price, not just the portion above the threshold: 1% over $1M through $2M, 2% to $2.5M, 2.5% to $3M, 3% to $3.5M, and 3.5% above that. A $2,000,000 sale owes $20,000; one dollar more owes just over $40,000. Because the statute reaches consideration in excess of $1,000,000, a sale at exactly $1,000,000 owes nothing.

The reduced rate above does not help here. Per New Jersey REALTORS®, “unlike the Realty Transfer Fee, the Graduated Percent Fee does not have a provision for discounts.” A qualifying senior gets a reduced RTF and still owes the Graduated Percent Fee in full.

Commercial property, and the classes the fee does — and does not — reach

The Realty Transfer Fee itself applies broadly to deed recordings. The Graduated Percent Fee is narrower, and the Division of Taxation lists exactly which property classes it reaches. It applies to deeds where the land conveyed is classified as:

  • Class 2 — residential
  • Class 3A — farm, but only where the farmland contains a building or structure intended or suited for residential use
  • Class 4A — commercial, other than industrial or apartment
  • Class 4C — cooperative units

Read the fourth word of that third line carefully, because it is where the money is: industrial and apartment properties fall outside the Graduated Percent Fee. A $4,000,000 warehouse and a $4,000,000 office building are treated differently. New Jersey REALTORS® summarises the same rule as the fee applying to “all residential and most commercial properties” — the “most” is doing real work.

One filing requirement that catches commercial sellers. The affidavit for this fee is Form RTF-1EE, and per the Division it must be annexed to every deed for consideration over $1,000,000 and with every commercial property transfer — that second condition has no price floor. A $300,000 commercial deed still needs the RTF-1EE even though no Graduated Percent Fee is due on it. Note this is a different form from the RTF-1 affidavit used to claim the senior, blind, disabled or low-and-moderate-income reduction on the base fee.

The calculator above computes the residential case. For a commercial transaction the base Realty Transfer Fee schedule works the same way, but confirm the property class and the exemption position with your closing attorney before relying on a number — class determines whether the Graduated Percent Fee applies at all, and that is a five- or six-figure question above $1,000,000.

Transfers that are exempt, and the affidavit you must file

Some transfers fall outside the fee entirely. The Realty Transfer Fee does not apply to consideration of less than $100, or to transfers between spouses or between parent and child. Certain transactions may be exempt from both the RTF and the Graduated Percent Fee.

Exemptions are not automatic. An Affidavit of Consideration (Form RTF-1) must be filed with any deed claiming a full or partial exemption — including the senior, blind, disabled and low-and-moderate-income partial exemptions described above. Miss the affidavit and you pay the full fee even though you qualified.

Where the money goes: the RTF is split between the state and the counties, with the state’s share directed to neighborhood revitalization, shore protection and the general fund. The Graduated Percent Fee goes to the General Fund. The Division of Taxation answers RTF questions at (609) 292-7974.

NJ Realty Transfer Fee — FAQs

Who pays the NJ Realty Transfer Fee?

The seller — and since 2025 that includes the fee on $1M+ sales. The RTF is deducted from your proceeds on the settlement statement; there is no buyer-side RTF in New Jersey. The Graduated Percent Fee on sales above $1,000,000, formerly the buyer’s 1% “mansion tax,” also became the seller’s obligation for deeds submitted for recording on or after July 10, 2025 under P.L. 2025, c. 69.

How much is the NJ Realty Transfer Fee in 2026?

Two marginal schedules. On sales above $350,000 the fee runs $2.90 per $500 on the first $150,000, then $4.25, $4.80, $5.30, $5.80 and $6.05 per $500 through the higher bands — an effective rate of 0.58% rising to 1.21%. Sales of $350,000 or less use a lower schedule starting at $2.00 per $500. In dollars: $4,175 on a $500,000 sale and $9,575 on $1,000,000. Above $1,000,000, add the seller-paid Graduated Percent Fee of 1% to 3.5% of the entire price. Seniors 62+, blind, disabled and low-and-moderate-income sellers qualify for a reduced RTF, but there is no discount on the Graduated Percent Fee.

What is the NJ senior citizen RTF exemption?

Sellers who are 62 or older, blind or permanently disabled, and low-and-moderate-income housing, pay a reduced schedule rather than a flat percentage. It replaces the standard schedule on the ENTIRE consideration: $1.40 per $500 on the first $150,000, then $2.15 to $550,000, $2.65 to $850,000, $3.15 to $1,000,000 and $3.40 above. On a $600,000 sale that is $2,405 against $5,185 standard — a saving of $2,780. Requires an Affidavit of Consideration (Form RTF-1) filed with the deed, and it does not reduce the Graduated Percent Fee.

Is the NJ Realty Transfer Fee deductible?

Not directly on federal income tax. BUT it's added to your cost basis when calculating capital gains, which reduces your taxable gain when you sell. Your CPA handles this at tax time.

Can I negotiate the NJ Realty Transfer Fee?

The rate is statutory and not negotiable. What IS occasionally negotiable is having the buyer pay RTF as a concession — rare but it has happened in strong buyer-favoring markets.

Does RTF apply to FSBO sales?

Yes. The RTF is owed regardless of whether you sell with an agent or For Sale By Owner. It's tied to the sale, not the marketing method.

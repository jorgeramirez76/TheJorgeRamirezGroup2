Keep property marketing separate from the legal dispute

A real-estate agent can organize property facts, comparable sales, preparation, access, marketing, and written offers. The agent does not decide ownership rights, court issues, settlement terms, or division of proceeds. A workable plan begins with authorized instructions and defined professional roles.

Start with the property

Create one property record both owners can verify

The listing file should distinguish jointly verified property facts, individual preferences, attorney instructions, and unresolved legal questions. That separation reduces contradictory marketing decisions.

01

Shared property facts

Document condition, known material information, updates, permits, occupancy, title contacts, included items, disclosures, and comparable evidence in a record available to both owners as authorized.

02

Owner instructions

Identify who can approve price, preparation, access, media, offers, credits, repairs, dates, possession, and changes. Refer conflicts about authority or ownership to the attorneys.

03

Communication boundaries

Set written channels, recipients, response procedures, access rules, and emergency contacts. Do not use the listing agent to carry privileged legal messages or resolve personal disputes.

Working plan

A property workflow with legal decisions outside the listing lane

Both owners, their attorneys, and the real-estate professionals need a consistent understanding of what the agent can do and which instructions require legal clarification.

01

Authority and contacts

Confirm ownership names, authorized instructions, attorney contacts, occupancy, access, document recipients, and unresolved court or settlement questions before public marketing.

02

Property analysis

Review current comparable sales, condition, preparation choices, disclosures, title questions, seller constraints, and the assumptions behind a proposed list-price strategy.

03

Launch and reporting

Publish only approved property facts, follow agreed access instructions, and provide the same activity and evidence reports to the authorized recipients.

04

Offer and closing coordination

Compare written terms consistently, record owner instructions, and route contract, authority, title, tax, proceeds, possession, and dispute questions to the attorneys and closing professionals.

Prepare the evidence

Clarify authority before preparing the public listing

This checklist is not a substitute for legal advice. It identifies the information a listing professional needs and exposes questions that should return to counsel.

  • Names on title, current occupancy, authorized representatives, and attorney contacts
  • Written direction about approvals, recipients, access, showings, media, and communication
  • Property condition, known material information, repairs, permits, disclosures, and included items
  • Mortgage, lien, association, title, insurance, or other contacts for professional review
  • Move and possession constraints plus any limits the agent is authorized to communicate
  • Unresolved questions about ownership, authority, settlement, court orders, or proceeds for counsel

Official-source notebook

What the public sources say—and what they do not

Each source below answers a defined question. Open the original, check its current date and coverage, and keep legal or tax decisions with the appropriate professional.

New Jersey Courts

Divorce and Related Issues

Use: Provides official New Jersey Judiciary information about divorce, economic issues, forms, dispute-resolution paths, and finding legal help.

Limit: A real-estate licensee does not decide ownership rights, court issues, settlement terms, or how sale proceeds are divided.

New Jersey Department of Banking and Insurance

Real Estate Commission Bulletin 24-11

Use: Explains New Jersey brokerage relationships, written brokerage services agreements, the property condition disclosure statement, and negotiable broker compensation.

Limit: It is statewide regulatory guidance, not advice about a particular contract, title issue, property, or dispute.

New Jersey Division of Taxation

Realty Transfer Fee

Use: Explains the current Realty Transfer Fee framework, forms, rates, exemptions, and the seller's statutory responsibility for the fee when it applies.

Limit: The amount and any exemption depend on the transaction and current law; a closing or tax professional should calculate the property-specific figure.

New Jersey Division of Consumer Affairs

Seller's Property Condition Disclosure Statement

Use: Shows the state's residential disclosure questions concerning occupancy, systems, defects, environmental conditions, flood risk, and other known property facts.

Limit: The form records the seller's knowledge and does not replace inspections, legal advice, or a property-specific review of disclosure duties.

New Jersey Department of Environmental Protection

Flood Disclosure Law

Use: Explains New Jersey flood-risk notifications for real-estate sales and rentals and provides the state's address-level flood notification tool.

Limit: Flood maps and notices answer defined questions; they do not predict whether or when a property will flood or replace professional advice.

United States Environmental Protection Agency

Lead-Based Paint Disclosure Rule

Use: Explains the federal lead-based-paint disclosure process that generally applies before a buyer is obligated under a contract for most pre-1978 housing.

Limit: The federal page describes coverage and exceptions; property age and transaction facts should be checked with the appropriate professionals.

Common questions

Divorce-related New Jersey property-sale coordination

Can one owner list the property without the other?

Authority depends on title, agreements, court matters, and the transaction facts. The listing professional should obtain clear authorized instructions and send ownership or signing questions to the attorneys and title professionals before marketing or accepting directions.

How can both owners receive the same market information?

Agree in writing on authorized recipients and provide the same comparable analysis, listing drafts, activity summaries, feedback themes, written-offer worksheet, and decision requests to them. Attorneys can advise when communications or instructions should be handled differently.

Does the listing agent decide how proceeds are divided?

No. The listing agent can estimate transaction items for planning and communicate agreed property terms. Attorneys, title or settlement professionals, court orders, and the parties' agreements control legal entitlement and disbursement.

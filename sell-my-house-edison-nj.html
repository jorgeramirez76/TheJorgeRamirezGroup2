The Cash-Offer Math Nobody Shows You

Cash buyers in New Jersey typically offer 70–80% of market value. Against a $580,000 median in Edison, that discount runs into six figures on most homes — far more than every selling cost combined. In a market where homes go under contract in 25–35 days, paying that much for speed rarely makes sense.

The exceptions are real: significant condition problems, an estate that needs to close, a job relocation with a hard date. Pricing a Edison Home in a 25–35-day Market

A 25–35-day market gives you room to be strategic but not room to be careless. Edison buyers in this range are comparing three or four homes at once, so condition and presentation decide which one gets the offer. The homes that stall here are almost always priced against what the seller hoped for rather than what the last three comparable sales actually closed at.

At a $580,000 median, your buyer pool is dominated by first-time and move-up buyers using financing. That has two consequences most sellers miss: the appraisal matters, and the inspection matters more than it would at a higher price point. Buyers at this level have less cash to absorb surprises, so unresolved repairs turn directly into credit requests.

Pricing is not uniform across Edison. Menlo Park, Stelton, Clara Barton, North Edison, South Edison do not trade identically, and a median pulled from the whole municipality can be well off for your specific street. The comparable sales that matter are the ones a buyer's appraiser will actually reach for — usually within a few blocks, not townwide.

School attendance is a real pricing input here, and J.P. Stevens / Edison High School is part of why families shortlist Edison in the first place. What that is worth to your sale depends on how your street compares to the rest of the district, which is a question about your specific address rather than about the town.

Selling With a Tenant, an Estate, or a Divorce in Edison

These are the situations cash buyers advertise hardest against, because sellers in them assume a normal sale is impossible. Usually it is not.

  • Tenant-occupied: New Jersey tenant protections survive the sale, so the lease and the notice requirements shape your buyer pool more than your price does. Marketed to the right buyers — including investors who want the tenant — an occupied Edison property does not have to be discounted.
  • Inherited or in probate: you generally need authority to sell before you can close, and that timing is workable if it is handled in parallel rather than in sequence. Selling as-is and selling at a discount are two different decisions, and they get conflated constantly.
  • Divorce: the practical problem is decision-making, not marketing. Agreeing in advance on the list price, the response to offers, and who signs what prevents the delays that actually cost money.
  • Deferred maintenance: in Middlesex County, inspection findings are negotiated, not fatal. Knowing which items a buyer will press on lets you decide what to fix before listing instead of conceding it afterward.

If one of these is your situation, the useful first step is a straight conversation about your constraints — not a valuation.

Edison Seller Questions

How fast can I sell my house in Edison NJ?

Well-priced Edison homes are going under contract in 25–35 days in 2026. Total time to close typically runs 8-14 weeks once you add attorney review and the buyer's mortgage. A cash buyer can close in 2-3 weeks, but generally pays 20-30% below market to do it.

Should I sell my Edison house to a cash buyer?

Usually not. We-buy-houses companies in New Jersey typically pay 70-80% of market value. Against a $580,000 median, that discount is far larger than every selling cost combined. Cash makes sense for genuine condition problems, estate complications or a hard deadline — and if that is your situation, competing investor offers beat a single lowball.

What does it cost to sell a house in Edison?

Total seller costs in New Jersey generally run 6-8% of the sale price once you include commission, the NJ realty transfer fee, attorney fees (typically $1,500-$2,500) and pre-listing preparation. The realty transfer fee is tiered, so it scales with your sale price.

Do I need to renovate before selling in Edison?

Rarely a full renovation. In a 25–35-day market the work that reliably returns money is cosmetic and mechanical: paint, lighting, floors, and anything an inspector will flag. Large discretionary projects usually return less than they cost. I walk the house first and tell you which items are worth doing and which are not.

What is my Edison home actually worth?

The $580,000 median is a starting point, not your number. Your actual value depends on your street, condition, lot and how your home compares to the most recent genuinely similar sales in Middlesex County. An online estimate cannot see condition, which is why the figure it gives you is often wrong in both directions.
